# Phase-8 supersphere block and the Mirron as a conformal map

**Date:** 2026-09-20  
**Wanted:** one object on phase 8 that contains the events of a superblock aeon as a supersphere, and reflects by a conformal Mirron map.  
**4D only.** Phase 8 is a tick, not a fifth axis.

---

## 1. What cannot be asked

A three-sphere does not have enough dimensions for every event of a 4-world to *be* a point of that sphere. If “contains” means “is identical with the set of events,” the request is 3D and the aeon dies.

**Contains**, here, means: the aeon is written *onto* an \(S^3\) by a conformal compactification (or by its spacelike future boundary). The sphere stores the aeon. It is not the aeon as a 4-volume.

**Key.** Containment: conformal encoding on \(S^3\), not a dimension drop.

---

## 2. The object

Call it the **phase-8 block**

\[
\mathcal{A}_8
\simeq
S^3.
\]

It sits on slot 8 (reset / bounce). Two honest realizations, both 4D:

**Boundary realization.** The aeon is de Sitter-like at late time. Then \(\mathscr{I}^+\) is an \(S^3\). All bulk events leave an imprint on that sphere (light-cone cuts, conformal data). Phase 8 is the tick at which that sphere is read and reflected.

**Cylinder realization.** Closed slicing of the aeon is \(\mathbb{R}\times S^3\) (or a finite conformal-time interval \(\times S^3\)). At the reset tick one takes the slice \(\mathcal{A}_8=\{t_{\rm bounce}\}\times S^3\). The whole history is not that slice; the bounce *acts* on that slice.

Use the boundary realization if you want “contains all events” in the conformal sense. Use the cylinder slice if you want a literal three-sphere at the tick. They can be identified when the bounce is \(\mathscr{I}^+\leftrightarrow\) next occupancy.

**Key.** \(\mathcal{A}_8\): phase-8 supersphere block. \(\mathscr{I}^+\): future conformal boundary, \(S^3\) in the dS-like case.

## 3. The Mirron as a conformal map of that sphere

Conformal group of \(S^3\):

\[
\mathrm{Conf}(S^3)
\simeq
\mathrm{SO}(4,1).
\]

The Mirron is an element

\[
M
\in
\mathrm{Conf}(S^3),
\qquad
M:\mathcal{A}_8\to\mathcal{A}_8.
\]

Examples that match older language:

- Reciprocal / inversion: \(\hat\Omega\check\Omega=-1\) pulled back to functions on \(\mathcal{A}_8\). Inversion in an equatorial \(S^2\subset S^3\).
- Antipodal map on \(S^3\): \(x\mapsto -x\). Orientation of the fabric reverses; past/future bounce.
- Special conformal transformation: sends a point of \(\mathscr{I}^+\) to a point of the child occupancy.

\(M\) is the reflection of the parent aeon into the child (or of Absolute Future into Absolute Past) *as a map of the block*, not as a 5D bounce and not as a CTC.

**Key.** \(M\): Mirron, conformal self-map of \(\mathcal{A}_8\). \(\mathrm{SO}(4,1)\): conformal group of \(S^3\) (also the isometry group of 4D de Sitter — same algebra, different job).

## 4. How the aeon gets onto the sphere

Penrose compactification of the 4-metric of the superblock:

\[
\tilde g_{\mu\nu}
=
\Omega^2 g_{\mu\nu},
\qquad
\Omega\to 0\text{ at }\mathscr{I}^+.
\]

The finished \(\mathscr{I}^+\) *is* \(\mathcal{A}_8\). Fields and light-cone cuts of the bulk pull back to data on \(\mathcal{A}_8\). That is “all events contained as a supersphere.”

Then phase 8 fires: \(M\) acts. Child occupancy reads \(M(\mathcal{A}_8)\). Local conformal factor of the tree note is the bulk extension of this boundary map.

**Key.** \(\Omega\): bulk compactification factor. Data on \(\mathcal{A}_8\): pullbacks of bulk fields and cuts.

- Not a derivation of \(\rho_\Lambda\) from \(\mathrm{Vol}(S^3)\).
- Not every 4D point becoming a point of \(S^3\).
