## 1. Causal Dynamical Triangulations (CDT) and Spectral Dimension Reduction
CDT is a non-perturbative lattice approach to quantum gravity using causal (Lorentzian) triangulations. A key feature is that the **spectral dimension** (effective dimension probed by diffusion/random walks) flows from ~4 at large scales to ~2 (or lower, approaching 1 in some regimes) at short (Planckian) scales. This supports the idea of effective lower-dimensional spacetime at short distances.

**Key Papers**:
- **Foundational CDT**: J. Ambjørn, J. Jurkiewicz, R. Loll, "Dynamically triangulating Lorentzian quantum gravity", Nucl. Phys. B 610 (2001) 347. [arXiv:hep-th/0105267](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0105267)
- **Spectral Dimension Scale Dependence** (classic result showing flow to lower dimension at short scales): J. Ambjørn, J. Jurkiewicz, R. Loll, "The Spectral Dimension of the Universe is Scale Dependent", Phys. Rev. Lett. 95, 171301 (2005). [arXiv:hep-th/0505113](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0505113) (widely cited for d_s → ~2 at short distances).
- **Recent CDT Overview (2026)**: J. Ambjørn, R. Loll, "Causal Dynamical Triangulations: New Lattice Theory of Quantum Gravity", arXiv:2604.05641 (2026). Explicitly notes "large quantum fluctuations lead to unexpected properties on short scales, most prominently, a spectral dimension near 2, replacing the classical value of 4." [arXiv:2604.05641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05641)
- **2D CDT with matter**: J. Ambjørn et al., "The spectral dimension in 2D CDT gravity coupled to scalar fields", arXiv:1412.3434 (2014). Studies spectral dimension in lower-dimensional cases.

These models provide concrete numerical evidence for dimensional reduction at short scales, addressing UV issues in quantum gravity.

## 2. Asymptotic Safety in Quantum Gravity
Asymptotic safety proposes that quantum gravity is renormalizable non-perturbatively due to a UV fixed point (Reuter fixed point) in the renormalization group flow, making the theory predictive at all scales without new physics. Gravity becomes "safe" from divergences at high energies. This framework naturally incorporates scale-dependent couplings and effective dimensional behavior.

**Key Papers**:
- **Foundational RG Equation for Gravity**: M. Reuter, "Nonperturbative evolution equation for quantum gravity", Phys. Rev. D 57, 971 (1998). [arXiv:hep-th/9605030](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9605030) (introduces the exact renormalization group flow for gravity).
- **Review of Asymptotic Safety Scenario**: M. Niedermaier, "The Asymptotic Safety Scenario in Quantum Gravity", Living Rev. Relativity 9, 5 (2006). Comprehensive review of the approach, fixed points, and implications. [DOI/link via Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.12942/lrr-2006-5)
- **Recent Comprehensive Review (2026)**: A. Eichhorn, "Asymptotically safe quantum gravity and its phenomenology -- a review", arXiv:2606.21522 (submitted Jun 2026). Covers the Reuter fixed point, evidence in 4D Euclidean gravity, and phenomenology across scales. [arXiv:2606.21522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21522)
- **Scholarpedia Entry**: "Asymptotic Safety in quantum gravity" (updated 2013, building on Reuter/Wetterich work). Good overview with references to Wetterich's functional RG methods.

This approach reconciles GR and QM by making gravity asymptotically safe at high energies, with running couplings leading to effective lower-dimensional or modified behavior at short scales.

## 3. Models with Modified Dispersion Relations or Minimal-Length Cutoffs
These implement a fundamental minimal length (often Planck length) or modified dispersion relations E² = p² c² + m² c⁴ + corrections (e.g., Planck-scale suppressed terms) to resolve singularities, UV divergences, and black-hole information issues. At short distances, this leads to effective dimensional reduction or "fuzzy" spacetime, sometimes behaving as 1D-like in extreme UV limits. Connected to Doubly Special Relativity (DSR) and minimal-length quantum mechanics.

**Key Papers**:
- **Minimal Length Scale Scenarios Review**: S. Hossenfelder, "Minimal Length Scale Scenarios for Quantum Gravity", Living Rev. Relativity 16, 2 (2013). Detailed review of models with minimal length, modified dispersion, and implications for quantum gravity, black holes, and phenomenology. Directly relevant to the "one-dimensional at short distances" idea. [PMC / arXiv version available; original in Living Reviews].
- **Modified Dispersion Relations (DSR context)**: G. Amelino-Camelia, foundational papers on Doubly Special Relativity and Planck-scale modified dispersion (e.g., "Testable scenario for relativity with minimum length", Phys. Lett. B 510 (2001) 255; and later reviews). These introduce modified E-p relations that can lead to effective lower dimensionality or deformed Lorentz symmetry at high energies.
- **Combinations with Minimal Length**: Many works by Hossenfelder and collaborators link minimal length cutoffs to modified dispersion to preserve unitarity and causality. See also papers on "minimal length quantum mechanics" and applications to Hawking radiation or cosmology.
- **General Context**: Papers combining these with asymptotic safety or CDT often show consistent UV completion via dimensional flow or fixed points.

These models address the "major problem" (e.g., consistency with observations, unitarity in curved spacetime, or matching to low-energy physics) mentioned in popular discussions.

## Summary of Relevance to "One-Dimensional Space at Short Distances"
- **CDT**: Strongest numerical evidence for spectral dimension d_s flowing to ~2 (sometimes lower) at short scales → effective lower-dimensional spacetime.
- **Asymptotic Safety**: Theoretical framework where RG flow to a fixed point naturally produces scale-dependent effective dimension and safe UV behavior.
- **Minimal Length / Modified Dispersion**: Phenomenological implementations that "cut off" or deform physics below a minimal length, leading to 1D-like or fuzzy behavior in extreme UV; Hossenfelder's review ties directly to the video source.

These approaches help reconcile Einstein's spacetime with quantum mechanics by modifying short-distance geometry without ad-hoc parameters in the best cases.
