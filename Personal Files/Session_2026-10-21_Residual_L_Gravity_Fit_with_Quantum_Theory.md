## Definitions and Core Statement

Residual-L gravity is the Superblock account of laboratory gravity. It is **not** a fundamental quantized interaction and does **not** postulate a graviton.

- Unbound EM (or null) force completes a full bounce between Absolute Past and Absolute Future. It is light-like and leaves no residual angular momentum.
- Bound EM force completes the bounce toward Absolute Past but has its rebound toward Absolute Future stopped at the Present. The stopped rebound deposits residual angular momentum \(L_{\rm res}\).
- Spatial compression arises from the residual angular momentum of these stopped rebounds. Bound fields tend toward the highest local concentration of bound fields (positive-feedback compression).
- Effective gravitational constant \(G_{\rm eff}\) of a set of globs is that of the dominant glob’s center of gravity. Stochastic gravity is realized by fractal globbing of hierarchical binding quanta.

This is an emergent, effective description. It matches laboratory \(G\) on glob scales under the residual-L mechanism and screening. Casimir/virtual-particle effects remain floor objects and are refused as sources of laboratory \(G\) or \(\rho_\Lambda\).

## Fit with Quantum Theory

Quantum theory (non-relativistic QM and QFT for the Standard Model forces) describes the excitations, phases, and bounces of the universal fields. Residual-L gravity is the macroscopic consequence of the **bound** sector of those same fields:

1. **No graviton required**. Gravity is not an additional quantum field to be quantized. The residual angular momentum is already present in the bound quantum excitations (quarks, gluons, colour, and the inverted-colour EM/electroweak flux on the floor). Quantizing residual-L would double-count the underlying quantum degrees of freedom.

2. **Emergent from binding**. Dynamic Refresh (DR) acts only on the binding floor (colour + inverted-colour EM/electroweak). Higher binding levels (hadronic singlets, nuclear residual colour, atomic EM, molecular/condensed EM) are globs. Isolated centers of mass can remain wave-like. Residual-L compression is a cross-cut property of every glob: \(\sigma^{(g)}\) from residual \(L\) at that generation. This is compatible with quantum composition of centers of mass.

3. **Scale separation**. At microscopic scales the description remains ordinary QFT (or the Superblock bounce/phase clockwork). Residual-L becomes the dominant effective force only when hierarchical binding produces a macroscopic residual-angular-momentum density. No conflict with precision quantum tests (atomic spectra, Casimir, interferometry) because those probe the floor or unbound sector where residual-L is either screened or negligible.

4. **No continuous free parameters for the mechanism**. The functional form (stopped rebound \(\to\) residual \(L\) \(\to\) compression toward high bound-field density) is fixed by the bound/unbound distinction. The numerical value of laboratory \(G\) is matched by the residual-L account; it is not derived from a new coupling constant. The mass floor \(\Delta = 309.3\,{\rm MeV}\) (neutron dressing protocol) remains an inserted scale, not a derived quantum-gravity cutoff.

5. **Relation to other approaches**. Conceptually adjacent to emergent-gravity ideas (Verlinde-style entropic or residual-entropy forces, or continuum limits of discrete quantum geometry) but distinct: residual-L is specifically the angular-momentum residue of stopped EM rebounds, not an entropy gradient or a spin-network area. Loop-quantum-gravity or string-theory gravitons are not required and are not sought. The framework refuses a fundamental graviton.

\[

\begin{cases}
0 & \text{unbound (completed Past \(\leftrightarrow\) Future bounce)} \\
L_{\rm stop} & \text{bound (rebound stopped at Present)}
\end{cases}
\]

**Key**  
- \(L_{\rm res}\): residual angular momentum deposited by the process.

- Unbound: null/light-like completion of the full bounce; no residual.

\]

**Key**

**Key**

- COM: center of mass / center of gravity of the glob that carries the largest residual-\(L\) contribution among the coherent bound sets.  
- \(G(\cdot)\): the residual-L compression strength belonging to that dominant center.
