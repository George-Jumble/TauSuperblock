## Plain-language summary

The axis of evil is the observed alignment of the CMB quadrupole (\(\ell=2\)) and octopole (\(\ell=3\)) preferred axes (and often higher multipoles), first highlighted by Land & Magueijo (2005). Early analyses rejected statistical isotropy at the ~99.9 % level (probability ~0.1 %). Under the assumption that this preferred direction is produced by rotational dynamics of the six co-located domains, one can assign a rotational percentage to the dark-energy density \(\Lambda\).

Using the earlier sample domain-orbital kinetic energy (\(E_{\rm orb}\sim 2.9\times 10^{66}\) J from cluster-scale \(\beta\)) relative to the total dark-energy energy contained in the observable volume yields a fractional contribution of approximately \(1.5\times 10^{-5}\) (0.0015 %). Alternatively, the raw statistical cue itself (p ≈ 0.001) can be read as a ~0.1 % rotational modulation. Neither figure is derived from residual-L or from the clock; both are phenomenological anchors consistent with a non-zero but small rotational residual.

## Analytical estimate

**Key to symbols**  
- Axis of evil: preferred alignment of the axes of the CMB multipoles \(\ell=2\) and \(\ell=3\) (and often \(\ell=4,5\)).  
- \(p_{\rm AoE}\): probability of the observed alignment under the null hypothesis of statistical isotropy (early analyses ~0.001).  
- \(E_{\rm orb}=3M\langle\beta^2\rangle c^2\): phenomenological domain-orbital kinetic term.  
- \(M_{\rm local}\approx 9.68\times 10^{53}\) kg: matter mass in one domain’s light-cone.  
- \(V\approx 3.57\times 10^{80}\) m\(^3\): comoving volume of the observable universe.  
- \(\rho_\Lambda=\Omega_\Lambda\rho_{\rm crit}\): dark-energy density.  
- \(\varepsilon_\Lambda=\rho_\Lambda c^2\): dark-energy energy density.  
- \(E_\Lambda=\varepsilon_\Lambda V\): total dark-energy energy in the volume.  
- Rotational percentage: \(100\times E_{\rm orb}/E_\Lambda\) or \(100\times p_{\rm AoE}\).

**From the energy ratio (cluster-derived sample \(\beta\))**  
Planck 2018: \(\Omega_\Lambda\approx 0.685\), \(\rho_{\rm crit}\approx 8.62\times 10^{-27}\) kg m\(^{-3}\).  
\[
\varepsilon_\Lambda\approx 5.31\times 10^{-10}\ \mathrm{J\,m^{-3}},\qquad E_\Lambda\approx 1.89\times 10^{71}\ \mathrm{J}.
\]
With the earlier sample \(E_{\rm orb}\approx 2.91\times 10^{66}\) J:  
\[
\frac{E_{\rm orb}}{E_\Lambda}\approx 1.54\times 10^{-5}\quad (0.0015\,\%).
\]

**From the statistical cue itself**  
Land & Magueijo (2005) and related analyses found the mean inter-angle of the preferred axes \(\sim 20^\circ\) versus an isotropic expectation \(\sim 57^\circ\), with only ~0.1 % of isotropic simulations matching the data. Taking this probability as a fractional modulation:  
\[
\text{rotational percentage}\sim 0.1\,\%.
\]

Both readings are possible under the assumption; neither is fixed by locked Superblock structure.
