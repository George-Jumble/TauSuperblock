## Executive Summary (Plain Language)

In the Superblock framework the cosmic web — the network of galaxy clusters, filaments, and walls — and the cosmic voids that separate them arise from the same residual-L mechanism that produces laboratory gravity, operating hierarchically on a randomized initial “Big-Bang gas” of bound quanta.  

Attractive residual-L (the time-like channel) pulls matter toward the highest local concentrations of bound energy. This hierarchical “globbing” builds the overdense skeleton of the web. The complementary space-like residual-L channel deposits an outward push on the non-metric hard wall; in regions of low bound-energy density this push expands voids.  

Voids are therefore not empty of process: they are the high-complement regions where residual-L accumulation remains weak. Rare low-dark-halo outlier galaxies sit in the extreme tail of the same statistical process (seed-free or phase-delayed patches).  

The six co-located domains share kinematic simultaneity labels (\(T_{\rm priv}\)), so the geometric pattern of the web is organizationally correlated across domains without requiring a metric fifth dimension.

## 1. Initial Conditions and the Randomizing Set

### Analytical
The primordial distribution of bound hierarchical quanta is modelled as an inhomogeneous Poisson point process \(\mathcal{P}\) with intensity \(\lambda(\mathbf{x})\):

\[
\lambda(\mathbf{x}) = \lambda_0 \cdot \prod_{g=1}^{G} \bigl(1 + \delta^{(g)}(\mathbf{x})\bigr)
\]

where  
- \(\lambda_0\) is the base (homogeneous) intensity,  
- \(g\) indexes hierarchical generation (glob level),  
- \(\delta^{(g)}(\mathbf{x})\) is the fractional overdensity contribution raised by dominant-glob selection at level \(g\),  
- \(G\) is the maximum generation considered in a given volume.

Dominant-glob selection raises the local intensity wherever a larger coherent bound set already exists, producing the positive-feedback bias required by residual-L gravity.

### Plain Language
Start with a gas of tiny bound energy packets scattered almost randomly after the Big Bang. Wherever a few of them happen to clump a little, residual-L attraction makes that clump a bit more attractive to neighbours. The process repeats at larger and larger scales, turning a nearly uniform spray into a web of filaments and nodes. The mathematics is simply a Poisson point process whose intensity is boosted wherever bigger globs already dominate.

## 2. Dual-Channel Residual-L Gravity

### Analytical
Residual angular momentum \(L\) left by stopped rebounds of bound electromagnetic quanta sources gravity:

- **Time-like channel** (\(+L\)): propagates at \(c\) toward the dominant glob’s centre of gravity. Laboratory \(G\) is recovered from the dominant glob of any coherent set.  
- **Space-like channel** (\(-L\) on the now-slice \(\Sigma_{\rm now}\)): deposits an outward push onto the non-metric atemporal hard-wall circle \(C_{\rm atemp}\). Phenomenological dark-energy density is  

\[
\rho_\Lambda^{\rm pheno} = \eta \cdot \frac{|L|_{\rm space}}{V_{\rm slice}}
\]

where \(\eta\) is a free efficiency (not derived) and \(V_{\rm slice}\) is the volume associated with a hierarchy-relative simultaneity slice.

The time-like channel builds the cosmic web; the space-like channel expands voids.

### Plain Language
Gravity in Superblock is leftover angular momentum from electromagnetic bounces that never quite finish. That leftover pulls matter inward (ordinary gravity, matching lab measurements). At the same time a complementary outward push is written onto an atemporal “hard wall.” In empty regions the outward push wins and the region expands into a void; in dense regions the inward pull wins and structures grow.

## 3. Formation of the Cosmic Web (Overdense Structures)

### Analytical
The time-like residual-L force on a test mass \(m\) at position \(\mathbf{x}\) relative to a dominant glob of mass \(M^{(g)}\) and centre-of-gravity \(\mathbf{x}_{\rm COG}^{(g)}\) is

\[
\mathbf{F}_{\rm TL} = -G_{\rm eff}^{(g)} \frac{m M^{(g)}}{r^2}\hat{\mathbf{r}}, \qquad r = |\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_{\rm COG}^{(g)}|
\]

where \(G_{\rm eff}^{(g)}\) is fixed by the residual-\(L\) density of that glob and matches laboratory \(G\) for the dominant glob of the set.  

Hierarchical application of this force on the modulated Poisson process yields the nested skeleton:  
clusters (nodes) \(\to\) filaments \(\to\) walls.

### Plain Language
Matter falls toward the biggest nearby clump. That clump grows, becomes the dominant glob, and pulls still more material. Repeated across scales this carves the familiar cosmic web of clusters linked by filaments and sheets. No extra dark-matter particle is required; the attraction is residual-L gravity itself.

## 4. Formation and Nature of Cosmic Voids (Emphasis)

### Analytical
In regions where the hierarchical intensity remains low (small \(\delta^{(g)}\)), the space-like channel dominates. The local expansion rate is enhanced by the wall push:

\[
\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}\Big|_{\rm void} \propto +\eta \cdot \frac{|L|_{\rm space}}{\rho_{\rm bound}}
\]

Void effective radius grows faster than the mean cosmic expansion.  

Low-dark-halo outlier galaxies occupy the extreme low-intensity tail of the same modulated Poisson process (seed-free or phase-delayed patches). Their occurrence probability is of order

\[
P_{\rm low} \sim \mathcal{O}(0.5\%{-}2\%)
\]

consistent with the observed rarity of systems such as NGC 1052-DF2/DF4.

Voids are the high-complement of the bound-energy distribution; they are not metric holes and they are not restored Tau-cylinder walls.

### Plain Language
Wherever the initial random scatter left almost nothing to glob, the outward residual-L push expands the region into a cosmic void. Voids fill most of the volume of the universe because most places never gathered enough bound energy to reverse the expansion. The rare galaxies that sit inside voids with almost no dark halo are simply the unluckiest patches of the same statistical process — places that never received even a small seed clump.
