## 4. Why the wall is not that circle

A Casimir sum needs a boundary condition and a length. The conformal cycle, as written in the 20 September notes, has:

- \(\mathscr{I}^+\), a spacelike 3-surface, future infinity of a \(\Lambda>0\) patch;
- void walls, the high-Weyl complement, causally open now, future-isolated only inside \(r_{\rm eh}\) for an interior observer;
- phase-8 reflection, time or null, not a metric hoop.

None of these is an \(S^1\) whose circumference enters \(\hbar c/L^4\). A 3-boundary does not become a circle by being called the wall of the cycle. de Sitter’s horizon has a Gibbons–Hawking temperature \(\hbar H_\Lambda/2\pi k_B\). That temperature is fixed by \(H_\Lambda\), which is fixed by \(\rho_\Lambda\). It does not derive \(\rho_\Lambda\).

Using \(r_{\rm eh}\) as the Casimir length is circular in the other sense too: \(r_{\rm eh}=c/H_\Lambda\) and \(H_\Lambda\) is set by \(\rho_\Lambda\). Feeding \(r_{\rm eh}\) back into \(\hbar c/r_{\rm eh}^4\) does not recover the density that defined it. The table shows it does not even come close.
