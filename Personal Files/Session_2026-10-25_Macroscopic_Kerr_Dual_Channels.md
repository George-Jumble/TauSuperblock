# Session Log: Macroscopic Kerr Ring and the Dual Channels of Gravity
**Date:** 2026-10-25  
**Project:** Tau-Superblock Universe  
**Topic:** How does the macroscopic Kerr ring interact with the dual channels of gravity (+L) & (-L)?

---

## Dual Channels in the Corpus
- **Bound channel (\(+L\))**: residual angular momentum \(L_{\rm res}>0\) locked by microscopic Mirron monodromy; sources hierarchical gravity via
  \[
  G_{\rm eff}\big|_{\rm inside\ glob}=G\bigl(T_{\rm priv}^{\rm dominant}\bigr).
  \]
- **Unbound channel (\(-L\) / \(L=0\))**: generators that reach the light-cone hard-wall \(\mathcal{W}\) with \(L_{\rm res}=0\).

(The label \((-L)\) denotes the unbound sector that carries no residual; the corpus does not posit an independent negative-residual source.)

---

## Origin Remains Microscopic
Both channels are generated at the Planck-scale Mirrons (\(\Sigma_{\mathrm{M}}\), \(r_M\approx 0.630\,\mathrm{fm}\)).  
A macroscopic Kerr ring (\(R_{\rm ring}\gg r_M\)) does not lock, unlock, or create residual angular momentum of either sign. Any \(+L\) or unbound ray that encounters the ring already carries the value of \(L_{\rm res}\) fixed by earlier microscopic processes.

---

## Differential Interaction
- **Bound (\(+L\)) channel**:  
  The ring can deflect or temporarily trap trajectories carrying \(\langle L_{\rm res}\rangle\). These trajectories never satisfy the monodromy-unwind condition, so their residual remains intact. The ring may contribute to \(M_{\rm eff}\) in
  \[
  \kappa\langle L_{\rm res}\rangle=\frac{G M_{\rm eff}}{c^2},
  \]
  influencing which \(T_{\rm priv}\) dominates, but it does not change the magnitude or sign of the residual.

- **Unbound (\(-L\) / \(L=0\)) channel**:  
  Null generators are redirected by the classical Kerr geometry. After redirection they continue to the same light-cone hard-wall and still obey
  \[
  \phi_{\rm mon}\to 0\quad\Rightarrow\quad L_{\rm res}\to 0,\qquad
  \sigma\big|_{\mathcal{W}}=0,\qquad\Psi_0\big|_{\mathcal{W}}=0.
  \]
  The ring imprints no new monodromy phase.

---

## No Channel Mixing
The macroscopic ring does not convert bound residual into unbound, nor unbound into bound.
- A generator carrying microscopic \(+L\) remains in the bound sector after scattering.
- A generator already on an unbound trajectory remains unbound and reaches \(\mathcal{W}\) with vanishing residual.

The incidence relation, shear/Weyl fall-off, and bound-sector average are unchanged.

---

## Causal and Hierarchical Consequences
All generators, regardless of channel, stay inside the light cone of the enclosing \(T_{\rm priv}\) and terminate only at \(\mathcal{W}\).  
The ring can dominate \(M_{\rm eff}\) and hence local \(G_{\rm eff}\), yet the dual-channel split enforced at the light-cone wall is preserved.

---

## Exclusions
- No new residual of either sign generated at \(R_{\rm ring}\).
- No global monodromy or universal hard-wall.
- Hard floor \(\Delta=\hbar c/r_M=313.1\,\mathrm{MeV}\) remains the microscopic Compton scale.

## Verdict
The macroscopic Kerr ring scatters both channels but does not mix them or generate new residual angular momentum. Bound (\(+L\)) trajectories keep their residual and may dominate \(M_{\rm eff}\); unbound trajectories still unwind at the light-cone hard-wall. The dual-channel structure and the rest of the hard corpus remain intact.

---

## Plain-Language Summary
The big Kerr ring can bend both kinds of gravitational influence — the residual spin that makes ordinary gravity and the free rays that carry none — but it does not create or destroy that spin. The residual spin still comes only from the tiny Mirrons, and free rays still lose whatever spin they have only when they hit the outer light-cone wall of the cell. The two channels stay separate.
