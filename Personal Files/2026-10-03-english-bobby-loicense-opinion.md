# Session note: English bobby — “Have you got a loicense for that opinion?”

Date: 2026-10-03

## Query

english bobby asking, "Have you got a loicesnse for that opinion?"

## Reading

The line is the meme catchphrase usually written **Oi, you got a loicense for that, mate?** The scrambled spelling in the query (`loicesnse`) is the same joke word: British *licence* pronounced in an exaggerated Cockney way as *loicense*.

- **Bobby**: informal British word for a police constable, from Sir Robert Peel, who founded the Metropolitan Police in 1829. Related nicknames: copper, Old Bill, plod, rozzer.
- **Licence vs license**: in British English the noun is *licence* (driving licence, TV licence). The meme deliberately uses the phonetic *loicense*.
- **Joke**: Britain already licences driving, television, shotguns, dogs, street trading, and (in some periods) knives without a good reason. The meme extends that habit to opinions, browsing, or posting.

## Origin and spread (from public meme record)

- Traction from about 2017 around UK knife rules and a widely shared butter-knife incident meme ("loicense foh that buttah noyf").
- Subreddit r/Loicense created 2 August 2018.
- Resurfaced in 2025 around UK online-safety / age-verification rules, with variants about a loicense to browse or post.

Source summary: Know Your Meme, "Oi, You Got A Loicense For That, Mate?"

## What is not true

There is no UK opinion licence. Nearest real law is narrow: public-order and communications offences about threatening or abusive words likely to cause harassment, alarm or distress — not a permit to hold a view. The comic gap is the point.

Older cousin: George Formby, "You Don't Need a Licence for That" (1937), satirising the same licensing habit.
