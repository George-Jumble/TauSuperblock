## Step-by-Step Derivation

### 1. Phase-Space Partitioning
The 8-phase clockwork (rooted in octonionic structure + G₂ automorphism) partitions the internal phase space of the Tav manifold into exactly 8 discrete slots.  
- 1 slot = reset phase (“Absolute Past” / naked singularity).  
- 7 slots = dynamically active phases.  

This creates a fundamental 7/8 occupancy limit.

### 2. Occupancy Rule from the 7-Fold Structure
Any local excitation, flux line, or domain-pairing configuration that attempts to occupy the reset phase simultaneously with an active phase in a closed topological loop violates G₂ automorphism invariance.  

**Result**: Certain combinations of states (or domain occupancies) are **topologically forbidden**. This is the seed of the exclusion principle.

### 3. Six-Domain Supersphere Constraint
The six co-located 3+1D domains on the internal supersphere, combined with octonionic multiplication rules, restrict allowed flux connections and domain pairings.  

When this constraint is imposed on top of the 7-active-phase rule, no two identical fermionic-like excitations can share the exact same domain-phase combination without forcing a forbidden reset-phase overlap.

### 4. Enforcement via Conformal Twistor Gravity
Twistor-space averaging (the geometric origin of conformal gravity in the model) projects multi-domain configurations onto effective 4D spacetime.  

Any configuration violating the 7-active-phase + six-domain rule acquires a prohibitive (effectively infinite) action cost in the twistor-averaged geometry. This manifests macroscopically as an **exclusion principle** for fermionic degrees of freedom.

### 5. Hierarchical Preservation Across Scales
The conformal scaling that distributes the total exponent stretch
