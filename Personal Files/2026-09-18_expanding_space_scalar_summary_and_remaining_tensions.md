## 1. What the scalar is

Expanding 3-space cannot stretch an existing Mirron: \(r_{\mathrm{M}}\) and \(c\) are clock-locked. Volume \(\propto a^3\) would thin the proper density unless new 2-surfaces appear.

The scalar is the switch for that appearance. In voids the conformal factor \(\Omega\) runs ahead, stiffness \(\varphi\) drops, and when \(\varphi<\varphi_{\mathrm{crit}}\) (and the void gradient is steep enough) a new Mirron nucleates.

\[
\Gamma
=
3H n_{\mathrm{M}}
\,\Theta(\varphi_{\mathrm{crit}}-\varphi)
\,\Theta\bigl(\lvert\nabla\log\Omega\rvert-\Lambda_\Omega\bigr).
\]

**Key.** \(3H n_{\mathrm{M}}\): the rate that keeps \(\dot n_{\mathrm{M}}+3H n_{\mathrm{M}}=0\) when the thetas equal \(1\). \(\Theta(\varphi_{\mathrm{crit}}-\varphi)\): stiffness must fall. \(\Theta(\lvert\nabla\log\Omega\rvert-\Lambda_\Omega)\): void wall / gradient gate. New mirrors are created geometrically; they are not stretched copies of old ones.

That is the whole expanding-space scalar at present. It owns creation and proper density. It does not own \(\alpha_{\mathrm{fs}}\), \(e\), \(Z_0\), or \(G_{\mathrm{N}}\).
