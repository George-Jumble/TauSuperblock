# Extremal ring under six-face accretion

**Date:** 2026-09-21  
**Object:** shared Planck–Kerr ring, \(a=M\) (geometrized), six faces B–W–B–W–B–W.  
**Question:** can it stay extremal?

---

## 1. What extremal means

\[
a
=
\frac{J}{Mc}
=
\frac{GM}{c^2},
\qquad
\kappa=0,
\qquad
T_{\rm H}=0.
\]

Surface gravity off \(\Rightarrow\) mass-inflation exponential off. That was the loophole. Accretion moves \((M,J)\). Extremality is the line \(J=GM^2/c\). One extra rest-mass increment with the wrong angular momentum pushes the hole *below* the line (\(\kappa>0\)) or tries to push it *over* (censorship / third law).

**Key.** \(a,M,J\): Kerr parameters. \(\kappa\): surface gravity. Line: \(J=GM^2/c\).

---

## 2. Third law and Thorne

You do not reach \(\kappa=0\) by a finite process in finite time (third law, Israel). Thin-disk spin-up stalls near \(a/M\simeq 0.998\) (Thorne). Starting *on* the line and staying there is a different, also unstable, problem: generic influx has \(E>L\Omega_{\rm H}\) or the wrong sign and drives \(\kappa\) up.

A Planck-mass engine has no thin disk of the astrophysical kind. The same inequality still applies: random 4-momentum crossing a face changes \(M\) and \(J\) independently.

---

## 3. Six-face bookkeeping

Net rates:

\[
\dot M
=
\sum_{i=1}^{6}
\varepsilon_i\,\dot M_i,
\qquad
\dot J
=
\sum_{i=1}^{6}
\varepsilon_i\,\dot J_i,
\]

\(\varepsilon_i=+1\) on a black face (in), \(\varepsilon_i=-1\) on a white face (out). Colouring BWBWBW \(\Rightarrow\) three in, three out.

Extremality preserved iff, at every secular tick,

\[
\dot J
=
\frac{G}{c}2M\dot M
\quad\text{when }J=GM^2/c.
\]

That is one real condition on six faces. It is not implied by B_lin or by Hopf. It is a tuning: outgoing faces must carry away the same \(J/M\) the ingoing faces bring, at the extremal ratio.

If the three white faces exactly invert the three black faces (same \(|\dot M_i|\), same \(a_i=GM/c^2\)), the nets vanish and the point on the line does not move. That is a pairing axiom, not a theorem.

**Key.** \(\varepsilon_i\): in/out. Extremal-hold: \(\dot J=(2GM/c)\dot M\).

---

## 4. What generic accretion does

- Influx of dust with \(L=0\): \(M\) up, \(J\) fixed \(\Rightarrow\) \(a/M\) drops, \(\kappa>0\), inflation can start.
- Influx at the horizon angular velocity: walks along near-extremal; Planckian hole has no large ISCO to enforce Thorne’s cap, but the third law still blocks crossing \(\kappa=0\) from below.
- White-face emission of low-\(L\) quanta: \(M\) down faster than \(J\) \(\Rightarrow\) overspin attempt. Classical censorship plus backreaction typically refuse the overspin; the hole sheds or the face cannot emit that quantum.
- Six independent streams: six chances to miss the line.

Mass inflation of a slightly sub-extremal inner horizon then runs away. The loophole closes unless the pairing holds.

---

## 5. Decision

The live frame does **not** keep the ring extremal under generic six-face accretion.

To keep \(\kappa=0\) you must add a pairing rule: white faces invert black faces at the extremal ratio, or net \(\dot M=\dot J=0\). That rule is extra. Without it, treat the engine as *near*-extremal at best, and do not use “\(\kappa_-=0\) therefore no inflation” as a theorem.

Planckian \(M=M_{\rm Pl}\) does not help: the ratio \(a/M\) is scale-free.
