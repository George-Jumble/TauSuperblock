## 1. Core Definition

**P-wave** (also written **P wave** or **\(\ell = 1\) wave**) is the spectroscopic designation for any quantum-mechanical partial-wave component that carries orbital angular momentum quantum number

\[
\ell = 1 \qquad \text{(or \( L = 1 \))}.
\]

### Spectroscopic Series
| Letter | \(\ell\) | Name |
|--------|----------|------|
| S      | 0        | Sharp |
| **P**  | **1**    | **Principal** |
| D      | 2        | Diffuse |
| F      | 3        | Fundamental |
| G      | 4        | … |

The letters originate from the optical spectra of alkali atoms (19th century).

### Mathematical Expression (Partial-Wave Expansion)

A two-particle wave function (or scattering amplitude) is expanded as

\[
\psi(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty} \sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell} R_{\ell}(r)\, Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi)
\]

- \( R_{\ell}(r) \): radial wave function belonging to partial wave \(\ell\)
- \( Y_{\ell}^{m}(\theta,\phi) \): spherical harmonic of degree \(\ell\) and order \(m\)
- For the **P-wave** term one retains only the \(\ell = 1\) contributions:

\[
\psi_{\text{P}}(\mathbf{r}) = \sum_{m=-1}^{1} R_{1}(r)\, Y_{1}^{m}(\theta,\phi)
\]

**Parity** of a pure P-wave state is odd:

\[
P = (-1)^{\ell} = (-1)^{1} = -1
\]

## 3. Classical-Wave Meaning (for completeness)

In acoustics, seismology and electromagnetism a **P-wave** is a longitudinal (compressional) wave whose particle displacement is parallel to the direction of propagation. This classical usage is unrelated to the quantum partial-wave definition above and is **not** the meaning intended in the LHCb or TSB literature.
