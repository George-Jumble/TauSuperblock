# Session 2026-10-09 — An expanding circle from 0 to infinity

**Framework**: SBT / TSB. Mirron, never soliton. Tau metric package stays rejected. One \(3+1\) manifold. Infinite space.  
**Date**: 2026-10-09  
**Charge**: Add another circle, an integral from \(0\) to \(\infty\), as the ever-expanding circle which holds all of the fractal tree of emergent superblocks. Can that work?

## 0. Verdict

**As a boundary at infinity, yes. As a container, no.**

An integral from the seed to infinity can be the growth of the tree. A single circle that holds every chamber encloses the complement the chambers are born in. That deletes the outside the wall needs. Infinite radius is not a hoop.

---

## 1. Complete key

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| \(R(t)\) | Radius of a chamber wall at time \(t\). Growing. |
| \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\) | Integral over the growth, or over all chambers’ radii. A sum of history. Not a new coordinate. |
| circle at infinity | Conformal boundary of the one manifold. Not a finite hoop. |
| container | A finite circle with every chamber in its interior. Refused. |
| \(\tau\) | Retired. This integral does not restore it. |
| complement | High-Weyl side. Must remain outside each chamber wall. |

---

## 2. What works

Each chamber has an edge. The edges grow. The integral

\[
\int_0^{\infty} R(t)\,dt
\]

is a length built from that growth, if \(R\) is specified. **Key.** \(R(t)\): wall radius of a chamber. \(t\): the chamber’s clock. The integral totals the history. It does not draw a new circle around the tree. The tree already has no last generation. Its “circle at infinity” is the conformal boundary of the one infinite manifold: the end of the chart, not a hoop of some radius \(L\) with a Casimir \(\hbar c/L^4\).

---

## 3. What does not

A circle that holds all superblocks has an interior containing every chamber and an exterior containing nothing of the tree. Chambers are born in the complement of a parent. The complement has to stay outside the parent wall. A universal container puts that complement inside. Same failure as the infinite black-hole container: the exterior the boundary needs is gone.

A circle of radius infinity is a line, or the boundary at infinity. It does not enclose. Assigning it a finite radius, including \(7\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}\), restores the retired hoop. Not done.

| Proposal | Status |

| One circle holding every chamber | **No.** Deletes the complement. |
| \(\tau\) restored | **No.** |
