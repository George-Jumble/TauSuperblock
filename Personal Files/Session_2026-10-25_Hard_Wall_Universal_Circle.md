## Analysis

### Current Status of the Hard-Wall
In the Superblock/Mirron corpus the hard-wall \(\mathcal{W}\) is the ultimate glob of each *local* Tau cell. It is the locus where unbound bounces complete and \(L_{\rm res}=0\), thereby supplying that cell’s outermost private time. Gravitic twistors of the microscopic KK cylinders extend from \(r_M\) out to this local wall. The framework repeatedly affirms local cells and the absence of any global repeating circle.

### Hypothetical: Universal-Circle Hard-Wall
The proposal treats the hard-wall as one shared compact circle to which every local cell (and therefore every KK cylinder) ultimately attaches. Residual-\(L\) data and gravitic twistors would then terminate on a single global \(S^1\).

### Consequences

1. **Reintroduction of a global compact dimension**  
   A universal circle is a global extra dimension. The monodromy that locks residual \(L\) ceases to be local to each KK cylinder and becomes common to the entire universe. Microscopic KK modes are no longer guaranteed to decouple from macroscopic distances, violating the scale separation required for laboratory gravity to remain Newtonian.

2. **Collapse of the hierarchical \(T_{\rm priv}\) rule**  
   The assignment \(G_{\rm eff}\big|_{\rm inside\ glob}=G\bigl(T_{\rm priv}^{\rm dominant}\bigr)\) depends on intermediate globs collecting residual-\(L\) contributions inside a cell whose own hard-wall sets the ultimate boundary condition. A single universal circle supplies only one outermost \(T_{\rm priv}\) for all cells. Dominant-glob collection inside each cell loses its independent boundary; the hierarchy reduces to a global scale.

3. **Globalisation of the bound/unbound split**  
   On a local hard-wall the unbound sector can unwind while bound residual-\(L\) remains available for gravitational sourcing. On a universal circle that unwind is identical for every cell. Free null rays acquire a global monodromy, contradicting the requirement that free light leave no residual spin. Gravitic-twistor extensions from unrelated cells mix on the shared circle and can no longer be separated by the dominant-glob rule.

4. **Twistor / conformal structure**  
   The existing description treats each extension as a null congruence internal to one cell. A universal circle imposes a global projective structure. Overlapping congruences acquire non-local monodromy phases absent from the present corpus.

5. **Observational and structural constraints**  
   - Heavy KK modes risk macroscopic coupling.  
   - Large-scale features (cosmic dipole, etc.) that the framework attributes to local or hierarchical effects acquire an unwanted global periodicity.  
   - The independent floor-number discrepancy remains unaffected, but the geometric origin of residual-\(L\) gravity is compromised.

### What Survives
- Microscopic size of each KK cylinder.  
- Definition of the Mirron.  
- Formal statement that unbound bounces satisfy \(L_{\rm res}=0\) (only its domain changes).  
- Protocol step 1 for the TEP floor.

### Verdict
**Yes, the proposal breaks essential structure.**  
Elevating the hard-wall to a universal circle reintroduces the global extra dimension and global monodromy that the Superblock/Mirron framework has excluded in favour of local Tau cells, hierarchical \(T_{\rm priv}\), and scale-separated residual-\(L\) gravity. The gravitic-twistor extension to a *local* hard-wall remains consistent; the universal-circle version does not.

## Plain-Language Summary
Right now each gravitational cell has its own outer hard wall. If we turn that wall into one big circle shared by the whole universe, we put back a global extra dimension. That mixes the residual spin from different places, collapses the private-time hierarchy that sets ordinary gravity, and lets the tiny extra-dimensional modes talk to large distances. Laboratory gravity would no longer stay cleanly Newtonian, and free light would pick up a residual spin it is not supposed to have. So the change breaks the framework.
