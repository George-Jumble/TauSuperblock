# Session 2026-10-24: Residual Dark-Energy Contribution Given the Rotational Percentage of Lambda

**Date**: 2026-10-24  
**Framework**: Tau-Superblock (TSB / Superblock) — live 4D frame, residual-L gravity matching laboratory \(G\), six co-located domains, kinematic UPS, Tau/Tav package hard-rejected.  
**Context**: Follows the phenomenological rotational percentages of \(\Lambda\) (0.0015 % from energy-ratio sample; ~0.1 % from axis-of-evil statistical cue).  
**Status**: Residual fraction is \(1\) minus the rotational percentage. Does not derive \(\rho_\Lambda\). Residual-L laboratory match preserved.

## Plain-language summary

Given a rotational contribution that accounts for either 0.0015 % or 0.1 % of the total dark-energy density, the residual (non-rotational) dark-energy contribution is essentially the entire remainder: 99.9985 % or 99.9 % of \(\Lambda\), respectively.  

In the Superblock reading the rotational piece is a free phenomenological orbital term of the domains; the residual piece is whatever accounts for the bulk of the observed accelerated expansion (entropic wall potential, external \(\Lambda\), or other non-locking annotation). Subtracting the small rotational fraction does not change the conclusion that residual-L gravity continues to match laboratory \(G\) on glob scales, nor does it derive the value of \(\rho_\Lambda\).

## Analytical result

**Key to symbols**  
- Rotational percentage: the fractional contribution assigned to domain orbital kinetic energy (\(E_{\rm orb}/E_\Lambda\) or \(p_{\rm AoE}\)).  
- Residual fraction: \(1 - \) (rotational fraction).  
- Residual percentage: \(100\times\) residual fraction.  
- \(E_{\rm orb}/E_\Lambda \approx 1.54\times 10^{-5}\) (energy-ratio sample).  
- \(p_{\rm AoE}\approx 0.001\) (statistical cue).  

**Energy-ratio case**  
\[
\text{residual fraction} = 1 - 1.54\times 10^{-5} = 0.9999846 \quad (99.9985\,\%).
\]

**Statistical-cue case**  
\[
\text{residual fraction} = 1 - 0.001 = 0.999 \quad (99.9\,\%).
\]

The residual dark-energy contribution is therefore \(\approx 99.9985\%\) or \(\approx 99.9\%\) of the total observed \(\Lambda\), depending on which phenomenological reading of the rotational percentage is adopted.
