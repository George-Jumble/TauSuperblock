## What the observations provide

\[
d_{\rm proper}(z)
\;=\;
\int_{t_{\rm em}}^{t_0}
\frac{c\,dt}{a(t)}
\quad\text{(external integral)}
\]

yields a measured growth factor of order 2 relative to the emission-era causal scale for the highest-\(z\) sources. This number can be inserted as a boundary condition on any phenomenological \(f(d_{\rm proper}/d_{\rm wall})\) or on a fitted \(\eta\) in
\[
G_{\rm eff}^{\rm large}
\;=\;
G_{\rm residual\text{-}L}
\;-\;
\eta\,
\frac{\rho_{\rm empty}}{\rho_{\rm bound}+\rho_{\rm empty}}.

**Key.** The integral and the growth factor are external inputs.

## Why this is not a derivation

- Locked structure fixes residual-\(L\) inside bound globs and a kinematic simultaneity skeleton. It does not contain an operator that converts the observed horizon growth into a unique value of \(\eta\) or of \(\rho_\Lambda\).
- The early-galaxy distances are already accounted for in standard cosmology by a fitted \(\Lambda\) (or \(w_0w_a\)); importing them into Superblock merely re-fits the same free large-scale term.
- Prior non-locking readings (Mirron/entropic wall potential attracted to lack of mass, Timescape-style backreaction, cone-residue diminishment) remain compatible with the data precisely because their strengths are free. The galaxies do not close that freedom.
- No restoration of the Tau package occurs, and laboratory \(G\) continues to be set by residual-\(L\) under screening.

## Verdict

Early galaxies can be used as observational input to calibrate the free coefficients of an already-written large-scale annotation, but they do not set up a derivation of the dark-energy problem.
