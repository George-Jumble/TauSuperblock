## Entanglement entropy from environmental which-path tagging
When an unbound packet coordinate \(\mathbf{R}\) becomes entangled with an environmental degree of freedom that records which spatial path was taken, the reduced density matrix of the packet (after tracing the environment) is mixed. The associated entanglement entropy \(S(\rho_{\rm COM})\) quantifies the loss of purity and the reduction of interference visibility. From the 2026-09-30 COM notes this is decoherence, not a conversion of the coordinate from unbound to bound. Residual \(L\) remains zero on the unbound coordinate; the entropy arises from the external record, not from a stopped rebound.

Visibility of COM interference survives when the path-dependent entanglement (with \(\chi\) or the environment) is small enough that the off-diagonal elements of \(\rho_{\rm COM}\) remain appreciable. The stamp, being a universal floor phase advance identical for every co-located copy, does not generate path-distinguishing correlations and therefore does not itself increase \(S(\rho_{\rm COM})\).
