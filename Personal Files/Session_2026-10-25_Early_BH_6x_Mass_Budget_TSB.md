## 1. The ΛCDM Tension with Early Massive Black Holes

JWST observations have revealed a population of active black holes in the first billion years after the Big Bang (z ≳ 5–10) whose masses and host relations challenge standard hierarchical growth within ΛCDM.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="0" />

Key observational facts:
- Black hole masses \( M_{\rm BH} \sim 10^6 \)–\( 10^9 \, M_\odot \) already in place by z ~ 6–10.
- Host stellar masses \( M_* \) often comparable or only 10–100× larger, yielding \( M_{\rm BH}/M_* \) ratios of order 0.01–1 (or higher in extreme cases), versus the local relation \( M_{\rm BH}/M_* \sim 0.001 \)–0.002.
- Example: UHZ1 at spectroscopic z = 10.073 (universe age ~470 Myr). Estimated \( M_{\rm BH} \sim 4 \times 10^7 \, M_\odot \) (from X-ray luminosity assuming near-Eddington), \( M_* \sim 1.4 \times 10^8 \, M_\odot \), ratio ~0.05–1.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="30" />
- Little Red Dots and other high-z AGN show similar overmassive character, with some systems having the BH mass exceeding the stellar mass.

**Growth-time tension:**  
From a stellar-remnant seed of ~100 \( M_\odot \), continuous Eddington accretion requires ~700–800 Myr to reach \( 10^9 \, M_\odot \). At z ≳ 10 the available cosmic time is shorter, and feedback typically limits sustained Eddington rates. Heavy seeds (direct-collapse black holes, DCBH ~10^5–10^6 \( M_\odot \)) or super-Eddington episodes are invoked to resolve the timing.

**Mass-budget tension:**  
In a typical high-z host, the halo mass is modest (\( M_{\rm h} \sim 10^{10} \)–\( 10^{11} \, M_\odot \)). With cosmic baryon fraction \( f_b = \Omega_b / \Omega_m \approx 0.156 \), the total baryonic reservoir is \( f_b M_{\rm h} \). Only a fraction of that gas can reach the central BH after star formation, supernova feedback, and outflows. When \( M_{\rm BH} \) is an appreciable fraction of \( M_* \) or of the available baryons, the mass supply is marginal or insufficient under standard efficiencies.

In plain language: the galaxies are still small and young, yet they already contain black holes that appear to have swallowed a surprisingly large share of the material that was available. Standard models struggle both with the short time available and with the limited amount of gas that can be delivered to the center.

## 3. The 6× Mass-Available Argument

**Proposition:** Because a black hole is a black hole in every domain, the mass available to form and grow it is the sum of the bound-energy reservoirs across all six co-located domains.

### Variables and functions (key)
- \( N_{\rm dom} = 6 \): number of co-located domains.
- \( a = 1,\dots,6 \): domain index; \( a=1 \) is the observationally accessed (“prime”) domain.
- \( M_{\rm baryon}^{(a)} \): baryonic (or bound-field) mass available for accretion or residual-L contribution in domain \( a \).
- \( M_{\rm baryon}^{(1)} \): the mass reservoir inferred from observations of the prime domain (stellar + gas).
- \( M_{\rm avail} \): total mass available to the shared Mirron/BH.
- \( f_{\rm acc} \leq 1 \): effective accretion / residual-L conversion efficiency (includes feedback losses).
- \( M_{\rm BH} \): mass parameter of the black hole (residual-L sourced compression scale of the dominant glob).
- \( \Sigma \): apparent seam (physical horizon locus).
- Residual L: residual angular momentum left when a bound force quantum’s rebound toward Absolute Future is stopped at the Present.

### Formal statement
\[
M_{\rm avail} = \sum_{a=1}^{N_{\rm dom}} M_{\rm baryon}^{(a)} \approx N_{\rm dom} \times M_{\rm baryon}^{(1)} = 6\, M_{\rm baryon}^{(1)}
\]
assuming rough equipartition or comparable occupancy across domains (consistent with co-location and shared Mirron).

The black-hole mass is then bounded by
\[
M_{\rm BH} \leq f_{\rm acc} \times M_{\rm avail} = 6\, f_{\rm acc}\, M_{\rm baryon}^{(1)}.
\]
In a single-domain ΛCDM accounting the corresponding bound is simply \( M_{\rm BH} \leq f_{\rm acc}\, M_{\rm baryon}^{(1)} \). The co-located six-domain structure therefore multiplies the available mass budget by a factor of 6.

**Plain-language reading:**  
Observers see the stars, gas, and black-hole luminosity of only one domain. In the Superblock the same spatial location is occupied by six domains. The black-hole structure (the Mirron and its apparent seam) is common to all of them. Consequently the material that can contribute residual angular momentum—and therefore the compressive mass that defines the black hole—comes from all six domains. There is six times as much “fuel” available in the same place as the single-domain calculation assumes.

The residual-L that sources the time-like attractive channel (laboratory G) is generated wherever bound rebounds are stopped. Because the Mirron is shared, residual-L contributions from all six domains add to the effective mass of the same apparent seam \( \Sigma \).

This does not alter the laboratory match of the time-like residual-L channel, nor does it restore the rejected Tau package. It is a direct geometric consequence of co-location plus the identification of the black hole with the shared Mirron structure.

## 4. Quantitative Illustration with UHZ1

Using the numbers for UHZ1:
- \( M_*^{(1)} \approx 1.4 \times 10^8 \, M_\odot \)
- \( M_{\rm BH} \approx 4 \times 10^7 \, M_\odot \) (order-of-magnitude)
- Implied single-domain ratio \( M_{\rm BH}/M_* \sim 0.3 \)

If the total baryonic reservoir in one domain is of order a few times \( M_* \) (accounting for remaining gas), the BH already represents a large fraction of that reservoir. Multiplying the available reservoir by 6 reduces the required efficiency \( f_{\rm acc} \) by the same factor, bringing the mass budget into a more comfortable regime even for light seeds or moderate super-Eddington episodes.

The same factor-of-six relief applies to the broader population of overmassive systems and Little Red Dots reported by JWST.
