## The proposal

Merge the Casimir effect computed for each of the six co-located domains and sum the results to obtain an effective cosmological constant:

\[
E_{\rm Cas}^{\rm tot}
=
\sum_{a=1}^{6}
E_{\rm Cas}^{(a)},
\qquad
\rho_\Lambda
\stackrel{?}{=}
\frac{E_{\rm Cas}^{\rm tot}}{V_{\rm ref}}.
\]

**Key.** \(E_{\rm Cas}^{(a)}\): Casimir energy associated with domain \(D_a\) (mode sum restricted by that domain’s phase walls or by a laboratory-style boundary condition inside it). \(V_{\rm ref}\): an arbitrary reference volume used to convert energy to density. The question mark indicates that the equality is not derived.

Because the domains are co-located, the six mode sums are not spatially distinct reservoirs; they are six restrictions of the same vacuum, or six phase-wall conditions on the shared occupancy. The sum is therefore either six times a single Casimir energy or a coherent combination weighted by domain-cross factors. Either way an overall scale (plate separation, cutoff, or reference volume) remains free.

## Why the sum does not produce lambda

- Laboratory Casimir energy density for any realistic plate separation is many orders of magnitude larger than \(\rho_\Lambda\) (or, for the Higgs-depth gap \(L\sim 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{m}\), still far from the observed value without an additional suppression). Matching requires a free cutoff or a free power of a contrast factor, exactly as in the earlier off-books time-mediated density notes. The match is numerical, not a derivation.
- The 2026-10-07 author cut explicitly refuses Casimir as a source of \(\rho_\Lambda\) or of laboratory \(G\). Promoting the six-domain sum to a dynamical cosmological term conflicts with that cut.
- The preferred non-locking carrier for a thermodynamic lambda remains the gradient of the Mirron/Entropic wall potential (unbound completed bounces). Laboratory Casimir is kept distinct from that wall potential; the two may be proportional only through a fitted constant.
- Domain-cross energies \(E_\times=\theta_{ab}\Delta_{\rm Tav}\) already exist as an internal protocol. They are not Casimir energies; adding them does not convert the mode-sum into a derived \(\rho_\Lambda\).

A formal sum can still be written for bookkeeping:

\[
E_{\rm Cas}^{\rm tot}
=
6\,E_{\rm Cas}^{(1)}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\sum_{a<b}w_{ab}E_{\rm Cas}^{(a,b)},
\]

where \(w_{ab}\) are optional weights built from the domain angles. The conversion to an effective density continues to contain at least one unfixed scale.
