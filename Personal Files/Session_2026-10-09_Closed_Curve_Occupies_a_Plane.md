## 0. Verdict

**Accepted, as an embedding statement. The locus is still one-dimensional.**

A simple closed curve does not sit in a line. Its smallest flat home is a plane. That plane is two-dimensional. The curve in it has one free coordinate. “Occupies two dimensions” names the plane. It does not add a second coordinate along the ring.

---

## 1. Complete key

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| intrinsic dimension | Number of free coordinates on the set itself. Ring: \(1\), the angle \(\phi\). |
| embedding dimension | Dimension of the smallest flat space that holds the set without forcing a self-intersection. Simple closed curve: \(2\). |
| \(a\) | Embedding radius. Circumference \(2\pi a\). |
| disk | Convex hull of the ring in that plane. Dimension \(2\). Not the singularity. |

---

## 2. The distinction

\[
\dim_{\mathrm{intrinsic}}(\mathrm{ring})=1,
\qquad
\dim_{\mathrm{embed}}(\mathrm{ring})=2.
\]

**Key.** Left: only \(\phi\) runs on \(r=0\), \(\theta=\pi/2\). Right: a circle of radius \(a\) needs an equatorial plane. Both are standard. A closed curve occupies a plane the way a knot occupies a volume: the ambient dimension is not the dimension of the set.

The singularity remains the boundary, not the disk. The disk center remains the regular throat.

---

## 3. Status

| Claim | Status |
|-------|--------|
| Closed curve occupies a 2D plane | **Yes.** Embedding. |
| Therefore the singularity is a 2D manifold | **No.** One free coordinate. |
| Disk is the occupied 2D set | **Yes.** Not the singularity. |
