# Session 2026-10-18: Entropic Force as Anti-Gravity Attracted to the Lack of Mass

**Date**: 2026-10-18  
**Query**: Examine the entropic force as being a type of anti-gravity. Instead of being attracted to mass, it’s attracted to the lack of mass.

## Live Constraints

- Residual-\(L\) gravity: residual angular momentum of stopped rebounds produces spatial compression proportional to local bound-field density. Attracted to the highest concentration of bound fields (presence of mass/energy). Matches laboratory \(G\). Attractive.
- Entropic-force / expanding hard-wall (preceding notes): non-locking speculation. Hard wall expands into the void-state (lack of mass). Magnitude fitted to observed dark energy. Requires screening or scale separation so residual-\(L\) continues to match laboratory \(G\) on glob scales.
- Void wall: causal edge of occupancy. High-Weyl complement is the lower-density side.
- Tau metric package remains hard-rejected.
- Casimir: floor object; refused as source of \(\rho_\Lambda\) or \(G\).
- Second law: satisfied internally by residual-\(L\) clumping + radiation; wall expansion is an additional large-scale contribution.

## Analysis

### Anti-gravity attracted to lack of mass
Ordinary residual-\(L\) gravity is attracted to mass: the force (compression) is stronger where bound-field density is already higher. The proposed entropic force inverts this at the largest scales: it is attracted to the lack of mass (the void-state, the high-Weyl complement, the underdense region beyond the hard wall). The hard wall is driven outward precisely because the exterior is emptier.

This is the defining large-scale behaviour already associated with dark energy or with Timescape-style apparent acceleration: underdense regions expand faster. Framing it as “anti-gravity attracted to the absence of mass” is a direct reading of that inversion.

### Mathematical statement
Residual-\(L\) (attracted to mass):
\[
\mathbf{F}_{\rm res}
\propto
-\nabla\rho_{\rm bound}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\frac{\delta V}{V}
\propto
-\frac{|L_{\rm res}|}{\rho_{\rm bound}}
\]
- \(\mathbf{F}_{\rm res}\): effective force from residual angular momentum.
- \(\rho_{\rm bound}\): local density of bound fields.
- Direction: toward higher density.

Entropic / anti-gravity term (attracted to lack of mass):
\[
\mathbf{F}_{\rm ent}
\propto
+\nabla\rho_{\rm void}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\frac{\mathrm{d}^2 R_{\rm wall}}{\mathrm{d}t^2}
=
+\frac{\partial V_{\rm ent}}{\partial R_{\rm wall}}
\quad\text{(outward when exterior is empty)}
\]
- \(\mathbf{F}_{\rm ent}\): entropic force.
- \(\rho_{\rm void}\): density deficit (lack of mass) in the exterior void-state.
- \(R_{\rm wall}\): hard-wall radius.
- \(V_{\rm ent}\): effective potential that decreases as the wall moves into lower density.
- Sign: toward the absence of mass.

The two forces can coexist if they operate on different scales or if the entropic term is screened inside bound globs.

### Compatibility
- On laboratory and glob scales residual-\(L\) remains attracted to mass and continues to match measured \(G\), provided the anti-gravity term is negligible or screened there.
- On superblock / void-wall scales the entropic term dominates and drives expansion into the lack-of-mass region. This matches the phenomenological role of dark energy without identifying it with Casimir or with a fifth dimension.
- The second law is preserved: internal clumping (residual-\(L\)) increases entropy by concentrating mass; the large-scale expansion into voids is an additional entropic contribution associated with the growing wall area or the filling of underdense volume.
- No restoration of the 7 Mpc Tau cylinder.
- The strength remains a free parameter fitted so that the large-scale effect equals the observed \(\rho_\Lambda\) (optionally together with integrated residual angular momentum). It is not derived from the locked structure.

### Falsifiability
The anti-gravity reading is falsifiable if it predicts a distinctive correlation between the local density deficit and the expansion rate that cannot be absorbed into ordinary residual-\(L\) hierarchical contrasts or standard \(w_0w_a\). Examples:
- a specific functional dependence of the effective equation of state on void fraction;
- a measurable repulsive correction in the deepest voids once the screening length is fixed;
- a scale at which the transition from “attracted to mass” to “attracted to lack of mass” occurs, testable with cluster versus void dynamics.

### Repercussions
- Provides a clear sign inversion: residual-\(L\) → mass; entropic force → lack of mass.
- Keeps residual-\(L\) as the sole account of laboratory \(G\) under screening.
- Leaves the magnitude fitted, not derived.
- Compatible with the hard-wall-as-attractor picture.
- Does not conflict with floor-only Dynamic Refresh or the 4D arena.

## Conclusion

Interpreting the entropic force as anti-gravity attracted to the lack of mass is a consistent non-locking reading of the expanding hard-wall. Residual-\(L\) remains attracted to mass and matches laboratory \(G\) on glob scales (with screening). The entropic term is attracted to the void-state and drives the wall outward on the largest scales, reproducing the phenomenological role of dark energy after fitting. The construction stays speculative, potentially falsifiable, and inside the live 4D frame with the Tau package rejected.
