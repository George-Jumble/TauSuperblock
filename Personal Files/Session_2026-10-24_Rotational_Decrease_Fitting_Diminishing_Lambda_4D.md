## Plain-language summary

Current DESI results (DR1 and especially DR2) show a preference for dark energy whose density is decreasing with time (thawing or quintessence-like today, more phantom-like in the past). In the 4D Superblock the rotational contribution to the effective \(\Lambda\) is a free orbital kinetic term of the domains.  

As the universe expands, the specific angular momentum of a domain orbital is conserved while the physical scale grows. The associated kinetic-energy density therefore dilutes faster than a pure cosmological constant (roughly as an extra power of the scale factor relative to vacuum energy). The rotational percentage therefore decreases over cosmic time. This supplies a natural phenomenological mechanism that can account for a diminishing \(\Lambda\) without introducing a new fundamental field, without restoring the rejected Tau package, and without altering residual-L matching to laboratory \(G\).

## Analytical sketch

**Key to symbols**  
- \(a(t)\): cosmic scale factor.  
- \(E_{\rm orb}=3M\langle\beta^2\rangle c^2\): phenomenological domain-orbital kinetic energy (total).  
- Rotational energy density \(\varepsilon_{\rm rot}\propto E_{\rm orb}/V(a)\).  
- Volume \(V\propto a^3\).  
- For free orbital motion the physical velocity redshifts as \(1/a\) (angular-momentum conservation), so kinetic energy per mass \(\propto 1/a^2\). Combined with volume dilution this yields an effective density that falls faster than constant \(\Lambda\).  
- Effective equation-of-state contribution from the rotational piece is therefore \(w_{\rm rot} > -1\) at late times, consistent with the DESI preference \(w_0 > -1\), \(w_a < 0\).

The residual (non-rotational) piece can remain closer to a constant or can itself carry a milder evolution (entropic-wall annotation). The rotational fraction, already small today (\(\sim 0.0015\%\)–\(0.1\%\)), was larger in the past, producing a net diminishing total effective \(\Lambda\).
