# Kruskal diagram extensions — investigation

**Date:** 2026-09-20  
**Scope:** what the analytic extensions actually are, then what they can and cannot do for a six-face trans-domain Kerr ring.  
**4D.** No \(\tau\).

---

## 1. Schwarzschild / Kruskal — four regions, one 4-geometry

Kruskal–Szekeres covers the *maximal analytic vacuum extension* of Schwarzschild. One chart \((T,X)\) (plus the 2-sphere). Four quadrants:

| Region | Name | \(r\) |
|---|---|---|
| I | exterior (Prime’s usual outside) | \(r>2M\) |
| II | black-hole interior | \(0<r<2M\) |
| III | second exterior | \(r>2M\) |
| IV | white-hole interior | \(0<r<2M\) |

Horizons are the 45° lines \(T=\pm X\). Singularities \(r=0\) are the hyperbolae \(T^2-X^2=1\) (spacelike). I and III are joined by a non-traversable Einstein–Rosen throat. IV is the time-reverse of II.

This is **one** 4-manifold. III is not a Superblock hidden domain. It is the other exterior sheet of the *same* vacuum solution. An astrophysical hole formed by collapse **does not contain III or IV**; those are cut off by the collapsing star. Kruskal’s four-block diagram is the eternal, vacuum idealization.

**Key.** \((T,X)\): Kruskal coordinates. I–IV: four blocks of one 4-geometry. Collapse: only I+II.

## 2. Kerr — infinite chain, ring, Cauchy horizon

Boyer–Lindquist is incomplete at \(r_\pm=M\pm\sqrt{M^2-a^2}\). The maximal analytic vacuum extension (Boyer–Lindquist / Carter / Kruskal-like patches) is an **infinite ladder**:

- outer horizon \(r_+\) — like Schwarzschild’s horizon,
- inner Cauchy horizon \(r_-\) — unstable in perturbation (mass inflation),
- ring singularity \(r=0\), \(\theta=\pi/2\), timelike,
- passage through the disk \(r=0\), \(\theta\neq\pi/2\) into \(r<0\) (another asymptotic, “antiverse”),
- then another \(r_-\), another \(r_+\), another exterior, forever.

Carter–Penrose diagram at fixed \(\theta\neq\pi/2\): a stack of diamonds, black then white then black… along the time direction. Identifications can cut the stack down to four sheets (Boyer–Lindquist 1967). Without identifications it is infinite.

Global hyperbolicity dies at \(r_-\). Penrose’s singularity theorem does not police the interior beyond that. CTCs exist for \(r<0\).

**Key.** \(r_\pm\): outer / inner horizons. Ring: timelike curvature singularity. Ladder: BH–WH–BH–… in vacuum Kerr. \(r_-\): Cauchy, not to be trusted as physics.

## 3. What “extension” means

Analytic continuation of the metric through a coordinate horizon, as far as the curvature allows. It is a theorem about vacuum PDE, not a theorem about what collapse produces.

Physical hole: extend through the future outer horizon into II (or Kerr’s \(r_+>r>r_-\)). Stop when matter, instability, or a Mirron cut is declared. Do not take the infinite Kerr ladder as an existence proof of infinitely many universes.

## 4. Six faces are not six Kruskal blocks

Live Superblock: six co-located \(3+1\) copies, one shared Planck–Kerr ring, faces B–W–B–W–B–W around \(\chi\).

That is **not** “Kruskal has four regions, so add two more quadrants.” Kruskal’s I–IV are regions of *one* metric. The six faces are attachments of *six* metrics to one ring.

A consistent reading:

- Each domain \(D_i\) has its own Kruskal-type chart of the *same* ring geometry, or its own I/II (collapse cut).
- The ring \(\chi\in[0,2\pi)\) is shared. The face \(i\) is the sector \(\varphi_i\to\varphi_{i+1}\) on that ring.
- Crossing a joint is changing which copy’s exterior you read, via \(K\) / binding, not jumping I\(\to\)III inside one Kruskal plane.
- White strokes on the ring are the Mirron-facing sectors (outgoing / reset), not automatically region IV of every copy.

Do not draw six extra Kruskal diamonds and call them domains. That multiplies 4-geometries in the wrong place.

**Key.** Face: sector on the shared ring. Block I–IV: regions of one 4-metric. Different axes.

## 5. What can be borrowed

- Kruskal’s *language*: future-in (black), past-out (white), two exteriors if you keep the eternal idealization.
- Kerr’s *ring* as the engine; the BWBWBW colouring lives on \(\chi\), not on the \((T,X)\) plane.
- Carter’s warning: inner horizon is not a clean door. A Mirron cut at the ring or at \(r_+\) is more honest than riding the vacuum ladder through \(r_-\).
- Collapse cut: parent superblock’s hole need not carry a white-hole block or a second exterior. White on the six-stroke loop is a *face colour*, which may be the phase-8 map rather than region IV.

---

## 6. Phase-8 vs Kruskal IV

Kruskal IV is a past interior of the eternal vacuum solution. Phase 8 is a bounce map on \(\mathcal{A}_8\) / on the ring. They rhyme (both “white,” both past-to-future) and they are not the same object. Identifying them is optional and is not forced by the extension theorem.
