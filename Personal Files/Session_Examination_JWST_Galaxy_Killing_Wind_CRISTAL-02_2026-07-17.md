## Article Summary and Key Findings

The Space.com article (published ~11 June 2026) reports on observations from the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) combined with the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) of the galaxy system **CRISTAL-02**.

### Core Discovery
- **Galaxy / System**: CRISTAL-02 — not a single galaxy but multiple galaxies in the final stages of a cosmic collision/merger.
- **Cosmic Epoch**: Observed as it existed approximately **1 billion years after the Big Bang** (redshift corresponding to light travel time of ~12.7 billion years).
- **Star Formation Rate (SFR)**: ~260 solar masses per year (M☉ yr⁻¹). This is approximately twice (or up to three times) the rate of main-sequence galaxies of similar mass and redshift — a rapid "growth spurt".
- **Galaxy-Killing Wind**: A powerful outflow of **cold gas** forming a huge plume extending almost as long as the galaxy itself (~7 kpc to the north-east). The wind is ejecting material at roughly **twice the star-formation rate** (~520 M☉ yr⁻¹).
- **Power Source**: Intense star formation triggered by the galactic collision. When the most massive stars explode as supernovae, they launch the winds that blast away the cold gas fuel required for further star formation.
- **Quenching Timescale**: If the blowout continues at this rate, the galaxy could exhaust its remaining star-forming fuel and become quiescent ("dead") in **less than 50 million years**.

### Broader Implications ("Lived Fast and Died Young")
JWST has revealed an unexpectedly large population of massive, already-quiescent galaxies in the early universe (within the first 1–2 Gyr). Standard models struggled to explain how galaxies could assemble so much stellar mass so quickly and then shut down star formation so abruptly.  

This observation provides direct evidence that **merger-driven starburst winds** can rapidly quench galaxies, offering a natural explanation for the "live fast, die young" evolutionary path. Nearly half of early massive galaxies show signs of interactions, suggesting this is a widespread process rather than a rare event.

### Researcher Quotes (from press materials)
> “Dense regions of the universe are like very active cities. Galaxies collide and undergo frenzied bursts of star-formation. But when the biggest stars burn out, they explode as supernovae, launching powerful winds that blast away the very gas galaxies need to keep forming stars.”  
> — Dr Rebecca Davies (Swinburne University of Technology), lead author

> “The galaxy has a powerful wind that is ejecting material twice as fast as the galaxy forms stars… If this rapid blowout continues, the galaxy could be dead in less than 50 million years… CRISTAL-02 offers a natural solution to the mystery of why these massive galaxies live fast and die young.”
