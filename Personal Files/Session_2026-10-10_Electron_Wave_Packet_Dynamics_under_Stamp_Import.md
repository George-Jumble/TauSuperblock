## Setup and complete key
A free electron is an unbound packet on its own centre-of-mass coordinate. In the framework the inverted-colour EM flux to which it couples is floor-derived and receives the imported one-tick phase advance; the electron lepton itself is not a floor excitation.

Symbols used below:
- \(\psi_e(\mathbf{x},t)\): Schrödinger (or Dirac) wave function of a free electron packet.
- \(\mathbf{R}_e\): centre-of-mass coordinate of the electron (identical with its position for a structureless lepton).
- \(\phi_{\rm EM}\): phase of the inverted-colour EM flux already present at the floor.
- \(\Delta\phi_{\rm tick}\): discrete phase increment at \(\Phi_8\).
- \(L\): residual angular momentum (zero on an unbound coordinate).
- \(\Phi_8\): reset seam.
- Floor: colour + inverted-colour EM/electroweak modes only.

The stamp relation remains
\[
\phi_{\rm EM}\big|_{\rm after\ \Phi_8}
=
\phi_{\rm EM}\big|_{\rm before\ \Phi_8}
+
\Delta\phi_{\rm tick}.
\]

## Free-electron packet
On the unbound coordinate the rebound completes between Absolute Past and Absolute Future, so residual \(L=0\). The packet therefore propagates as an ordinary free wave packet whose phase evolution is fixed by the free Dirac (or Schrödinger) Hamiltonian. The stamp does not act directly on \(\psi_e\).  

Indirect effect: the electron couples to the inverted-colour EM field. When that field is sampled after a \(\Phi_8\) tick its phase has advanced by \(\Delta\phi_{\rm tick}\). In the interaction picture this appears as a discrete jump in the relative phase between the electron packet and the virtual-photon cloud that dresses it. Because the dressing is virtual and the coordinate is unbound, the jump does not accumulate residual angular momentum and does not alter the laboratory mass or charge.  

Spreading of the free packet continues to be governed by the usual dispersive term \(\propto t/m_e\) in the free evolution; the stamp supplies only a global phase reference update at each seam and does not modify the dispersion relation.

## Bound electron (atomic or metallic envelope)
Inside an atom or metal the electron coordinate is an internal degree of freedom of a higher glob. Residual \(L\) resides in the stopped rebounds of the EM lines that form the envelope. The stamp advances the floor modes that feed those lines, thereby updating the phase of the Coulomb flux, but it does not re-bind the orbital or rewrite the COM of the parent atom.  

The parent COM packet (as in the Pedalino sodium-cluster interferometry) can remain unbound and interfere; the internal electronic phases are carried along as part of the bound body. Visibility of the COM interference is limited by entanglement with the environment, not by the stamp itself.

## Interference and which-path
A which-path tag arises when an external degree of freedom (gas collision, thermal photon, etc.) entangles with the packet coordinate. The stamp, being a universal floor phase advance, does not itself constitute a which-path record. Because it is the same for every co-located copy and is applied at the shared seam, it preserves the relative phase between paths that have not yet decohered.

## Relation to residual-\(L\) gravity
A free electron packet carries no residual \(L\) from its own unbound coordinate. Any gravitational effect it sources is the ordinary residual-\(L\) compression generated by the stopped rebounds of the EM flux that dresses it, evaluated at the proper-time rate set by the local lapse. The stamp modulates that sampling rate by one tick but adds no new residual. Laboratory \(G\) matching is unchanged.

## Summary
Electron wave-packet dynamics remain standard free or bound evolution. The imported stamp updates the phase of the inverted-colour EM flux to which the electron couples; it does not stamp the lepton coordinate itself. Free packets stay unbound (\(L=0\)); bound envelopes retain their residual \(L\); COM interference constraints (Pedalino) continue to hold; all core Superblock locks are preserved.
