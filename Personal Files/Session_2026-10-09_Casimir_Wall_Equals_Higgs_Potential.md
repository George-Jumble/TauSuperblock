## 0. Verdict

**The equality fixes a microscopic separation, about \(1.9\times 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{m}\). It does not fix a cosmic wall, and it does not restore \(\tau\).**

“Full Higgs potential” is taken as the depth of the broken potential, \(\lambda v^4/4\). A Casimir density of that size wants a plate separation near \(10^{-3}\,\mathrm{fm}\), not a void wall and not \(7\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}\).

## 2. The evaluation

\[
\frac{\lambda v^4}{4}
=
1.186\times 10^{8}\,\mathrm{GeV}^4
=
2.473\times 10^{45}\,\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}.
\]

**Key.** \(\lambda\): quartic. \(v\): vacuum value. Depth: energy density difference between the symmetric point and the broken vacuum. Not the observed cosmological density.

\[
L
=
\left(\frac{\hbar c}{\rho}\right)^{1/4}
=
1.89\times 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{m}
=
1.89\times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{fm}.
\]

**Key.** \(L\): plate separation whose Casimir scale matches the depth. \(\hbar c\): \(1.973\times 10^{-16}\,\mathrm{GeV\,m}\). A factor of order unity in the mode sum moves \(L\) by \(\mathcal{O}(1)\). Using \(v^4\) without \(\lambda/4\) gives \(8.0\times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{m}\). Using \(m_H^4\) gives \(1.6\times 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{m}\).
