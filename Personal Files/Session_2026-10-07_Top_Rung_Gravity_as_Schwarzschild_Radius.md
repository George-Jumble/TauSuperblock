Reading \(r_s\) as the replacement top rung fails for a different reason. \(r_s\) is not a generation. It is a radius defined for every mass. A rock, a planet, and a proton each have one, and for all of them it lies far inside the glob. The only live use is a **saturation test on a given glob**: when that glob’s coherent radius meets its own \(r_s\), the exterior chart changes. That test sits on the gravitic cross-cut. It does not add a rung above molecular binding, and it does not restore a cosmic node.

## 4. Examination

### 4.1 Not a generation

A binding generation is a coherent set with one center of mass, built by absorbing child packet addresses into \(\chi\). \(r_s\) does not do that. Every glob already has an \(r_s(m^{(g)})\), including the hadronic glob. If \(r_s\) were the top rung, the proton would already be on it, which empties the ladder.

The saturation condition that *is* well-defined:

\[
R^{(g)}\;\stackrel{?}{=}\;r_s\bigl(m^{(g)}\bigr)
=
\frac{2G\,m^{(g)}}{c^2}.
\]

**Key.** \(R^{(g)}\): coherent material radius of glob \(g\). \(m^{(g)}\): its mass. \(r_s(m^{(g)})\): its Schwarzschild radius. The comparison is per glob. It is not a universal scale inserted above molecules.

Three regimes:

| Regime | Condition | What gravity is doing |
|--------|-----------|------------------------|
| Sub-critical | \(R^{(g)}\gg r_s(m^{(g)})\) | Cross-cut only. \(\sigma^{(g)}\) from residual \(L\). \(r_s\) is an interior mathematical radius, not the surface and not a binding shell. Rock, planet, ordinary star. |
| Saturated | \(R^{(g)}\sim r_s(m^{(g)})\) | Exterior chart of *this* dominant glob changes. Apparent seam. Not a new force and not a new generation. |
| Retired top | \(\max\mathrm{TW}^{G}\) at \(\tau\) | Not available. Do not replace \(\tau\) by declaring \(r_s\) cosmic. |

### 4.2 Chart zero is not the seam

In Schwarzschild coordinates \(g_{tt}\) vanishes at \(r_s\). Kruskal–Szekeres removes that as a coordinate artifact and extends through. The 20 September Kruskal note already separated that extension from the TSB bounce. The live seam is the Mirron world-tube \(\Sigma\): an apparent locus, algebraic reset \(\Phi_8\), sign flip of spatial compression. Cadoni’s “apparent horizons only” is an analogue of that negative claim (no static event horizon as the physical object). It is not a derivation of \(\Sigma\), and it does not make \(r_s\) a globbing unit.

### 4.3 Relation to the refined stack

From the globbing refinement the same day: gravity is present at every stopped glob; \(G_{\rm eff}(S)\) belongs to the dominant center of gravity. The Schwarzschild test is how you recognize when that dominant glob has no further material exterior:

\[
\mathcal{B}^{\star}(S)\ \text{saturated}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
R\bigl(\mathcal{B}^{\star}(S)\bigr)
\sim
r_s\bigl(m(\mathcal{B}^{\star}(S))\bigr).
\]

**Key.** \(\mathcal{B}^{\star}(S)\): dominant coherent glob of set \(S\). \(R(\mathcal{B}^{\star})\): its coherent radius. \(m(\mathcal{B}^{\star})\): its mass. \(r_s\): as above. Saturation selects which glob’s exterior is an apparent seam. It does not create the glob, and Dynamic Refresh does not refresh it. A cosmological mass drift \(M\propto a^{k}\) remains refused as Dynamic Refresh (30 September CCBH note).

Below saturation the dominant center of gravity is still the rock, the planet, or the star, and \(r_s\) is unused as a surface. That is why “Schwarzschild / rock-scale” cannot be one rung: the rock’s binding radius and the rock’s \(r_s\) differ by many orders, and only the binding radius is a glob.
