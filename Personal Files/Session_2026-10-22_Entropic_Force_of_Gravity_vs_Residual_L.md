**Query**: What about the entropic force of gravity?

## Locked account of gravity

Residual-\(L\) gravity is spatial compression arising from the residual angular momentum of stopped (bound) electromagnetic rebounds. Bound fields tend toward the highest local concentration of bound fields (positive-feedback compression). Effective \(G\) of a set belongs to the dominant glob’s center of gravity. This matches laboratory \(G\) under hierarchical screening. Unbound completions leave \(L_{\rm res}=0\) and do not source residual-\(L\) gravity. No fundamental graviton is postulated.

\[
L_{\rm res}
=
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{unbound (completed bounce)} \\
L_{\rm stop} & \text{bound (rebound stopped at Present)}
\end{cases}
\qquad
\text{compression}
\propto
L_{\rm res}.
\]

**Variable key:**  
- \(L_{\rm res}\): residual angular momentum.  
- \(L_{\rm stop}\): angular momentum deposited by a stopped rebound.  
- Compression: spatial compression toward high bound-field density.

## Entropic force in the framework

The entropic force discussed in the recent non-locking dark-energy sector is the gradient of the Mirron/Entropic wall potential sourced by unbound photonic (and other null) energy on the wall (locus of completed bounces). It is directed toward the lack-of-mass / high-Weyl side and is fitted, not derived. It is not the source of laboratory gravity.

Verlinde-style entropic gravity (force from an entropy gradient on a holographic screen) is conceptually adjacent but distinct: residual-\(L\) is specifically the angular-momentum residue of stopped EM rebounds, not an entropy gradient. The framework does not replace residual-\(L\) with an entropic derivation of \(G\).

**Variable key:**  
- \(\mathbf{F}_{\rm ent}\): non-locking entropic force from unbound wall potential.  
- Wall: locus of completed unbound bounces (\(L_{\rm res}=0\)).

## Can residual-L gravity be read as an entropic force?

Only as a non-locking analogy. One may note that the tendency of bound fields toward highest concentration increases the local entropy of the bound sector (clumping), and that the associated compression can be written in thermodynamic language. That rewriting does not change the microphysical source (stopped rebounds) and does not derive the numerical value of laboratory \(G\). It remains an organizational remark, not a lock.

The unbound entropic force (wall potential) and the bound residual-\(L\) compression remain cleanly separated: the former can accelerate on the largest scales toward voids; the latter compresses overdense regions and matches laboratory \(G\).

## Conclusion

There is no locked “entropic force of gravity” that replaces residual-\(L\). Laboratory gravity continues to be residual angular momentum of stopped bound rebounds. The entropic force already under discussion is the non-locking, fitted push from unbound energy on the wall; it does not source ordinary gravity. An entropic rewriting of residual-\(L\) is possible as an analogy but adds no derivation and is not required. All live locks (residual-\(L\) = laboratory \(G\), bound/unbound split, Casimir cut, Tau rejection) are preserved.

**Plain language.** Ordinary gravity in Superblock is the leftover spin from electromagnetic rebounds that get stopped; it pulls things together and matches what we measure in the laboratory. The “entropic force” we have been discussing is a separate, adjustable push that comes from free light collected on a distant wall and can make the universe accelerate outward. One can talk about gravity in entropy language if one wishes, but that does not change where the leftover spin comes from, and it does not turn the wall-push into ordinary gravity.
