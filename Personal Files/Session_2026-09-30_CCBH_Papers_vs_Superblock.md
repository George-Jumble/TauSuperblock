## 2. What each paper actually says

### 2.1 Farrah et al. 2023 (ApJL 944 L31) — the observational claim

Kerr is treated as provisional: its falloff at infinity is incompatible with an expanding universe. Compact objects with a realistic cosmological boundary can change active gravitational mass with \(a\) even with no accretion and no mergers,

\[
M(a)=M(a_i)\left(\frac{a}{a_i}\right)^k,\qquad a\ge a_i.
\]

**Key.** \(M(a)\): active gravitational mass at scale factor \(a\). \(M(a_i)\): mass at coupling onset. \(k\): dimensionless coupling. Photons redshift with \(k=-1\). Pure vacuum-energy interiors are predicted to sit at \(k=3\), which is the causal maximum for positive energy density. Decoupled Kerr / Nolan is \(k=0\).

They compare \(M_{\mathrm{BH}}\)–\(M_*\) planes of local ellipticals against five high-\(z\) elliptical samples (\(0.7\lesssim z\lesssim 2.5\); WISE, SDSS, COSMOS). Combined posterior:

\[
k=3.11^{+1.19}_{-1.33}\quad(90\%),\qquad\text{Gaussian fit }k=3.09\pm 0.76.
\]

Zero coupling is excluded at \(99.98\%\) confidence in *that* analysis. For \(k=3\), number density falls as \(a^{-3}\) while mass grows as \(a^3\), so the *energy density contributed by the BH population is constant* and the continuity equation assigns \(w=-1\). They then argue that stellar-remnant production from the cosmic SFRD can reproduce Planck \(\Omega_\Lambda\approx 0.68\) with \(\lesssim 3\%\) baryon consumption, consistent with their MACHO cuts, and that acceleration onset near \(z\sim 0.7\) follows first light at \(z\sim 25\).

This is a *proposal*, not a measurement of \(\Omega_\Lambda\) from BH counts. Subsequent papers (LIGO–Virgo rate/mass functions; SMBH accretion-history reconstructions) prefer much smaller \(k\), sometimes \(k\simeq -1\). The Farrah posterior is therefore a contested data point, not a closed fact.

### 2.3 Cadoni et al. 2026 (arXiv:2601.03296) — regular-horizon embedding

Exact Einstein gravity sourced by an anisotropic fluid, no radial energy influx, arbitrary FLRW, full backreaction. Metric (conformal time):

\[
\mathrm{d}s^2=a^2(\eta)\Bigl[-e^{\alpha(\eta,r)}\,\mathrm{d}\eta^2+e^{\beta(\eta,r)}\,\mathrm{d}r^2+r^2\mathrm{d}\Omega_2^2\Bigr].
\]

**Key.** \(a(\eta)\): scale factor. \(\eta\): conformal time. \(\alpha,\beta\): potentials. \(r\): comoving radius. The fluid is split \(\rho=\rho_c+\rho_l\), \(p=p_c+p_l\), with \((\rho_c,p_c)\) obeying the background Friedmann equations.

Local mass after subtracting the cosmological ball:

\[
M_l(r,\eta)=M_{\mathrm{MS}}(r,\eta)-\frac{4\pi}{3}\ell^3\rho_c(\eta),\qquad\ell=ar.
\]

Apparent horizon: \(2M(r_h,a)=\ell_h\). Curvature invariants are regular at that locus because \(\rho_c\) sits in the metric functions. McVittie’s horizon singularity is avoided. A new Schwarzschild CE is given, regular except at the *central* singularity. The paper states there are **no static event horizons**, only apparent horizons. Theoretical mass scaling remains in the causal window \(M\sim a^k\) with \(-3\le k\le 3\). Energy-condition violations near the horizon (DEC, \(\omega<0\)) are allowed and read as an effective description of inhomogeneity.

Cadoni does **not** claim that remnant BHs are dark energy. It is a GR existence result: cosmological embedding of a static spherical object can be horizon-regular once anisotropy and backreaction are admitted.
