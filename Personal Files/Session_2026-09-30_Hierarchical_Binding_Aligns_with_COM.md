## 0. Result in one block

Dynamic Refresh writes occupancy at the floor. Hierarchical binding is how those occupied lines **compose** into larger stopped envelopes, each of which owns a center of mass.

COM is the translational coordinate of a *coherent bound set*. Hierarchy is the rule that says which set is coherent, which residual \(L\) dominates, and which address \(\mathbf{R}^{(g)}\) is the packet of generation \(g\).

Pedalino tested \(\mathbf{R}\) of one metallic generation. It did not test DR. It also did not test whether generation \(g+1\) is a new COM or only a heavier \(\chi\). That is the live question.

## 3. Hierarchy as a tower of COMs

Each generation is a bound body plus a packet address.

\[
\mathcal{B}^{(g)}
\;=\;
\bigl\{\,\mathcal{B}^{(g+1)}_k\,\bigr\}_{\!k}
\quad\text{held as one glob},
\qquad
\mathbf{R}^{(g)}
=
\frac{1}{m^{(g)}}\sum_k m_k^{(g+1)}\,\mathbf{R}_k^{(g+1)}.
\]

**Key.** \(\mathbf{R}^{(g)}\): mass-weighted COM of the child envelopes. Children have their own \(\mathbf{R}_k^{(g+1)}\) and their own \(L_k^{(g+1)}\). Parent coherence means the child COMs are no longer independent isolated packets; they are internals of \(\chi^{(g)}\).

Split at every generation, the same split Pedalino used once:

\[
T^{(g)}
=
\frac12 m^{(g)}\lvert\dot{\mathbf{R}}^{(g)}\rvert^2
+
T_{\mathrm{int}}^{(g)}(\chi^{(g)}).
\]

**Key.** First term: unbound packet motion of that glob, if isolated. Second term: bound relatives — the whole lower hierarchy.

So:

- Floor DR feeds \(T_{\mathrm{int}}\) at the bottom.
- Hierarchy decides how many times that split is nested.
- Isolated \(\mathbf{R}^{(g)}\) at the *highest coherent* generation is the only coordinate Pedalino-class experiments can superpose without opening the glob.

The old stochastic-gravity sentence is this tower, not DR: effective \(G\) belongs to the largest coherent bound set among groups of sets where that \(G\) dominates. That set is exactly the one whose \(\mathbf{R}\) is the packet the lab would call “the object.”

## 4. Implications

### 4.1 Binding level = which COM is still free

A coordinate is bound when it has been absorbed into some parent \(\chi\). It is unbound when it is still a packet address.

| Level | Example | COM status |
|-------|---------|------------|
| Floor | colour line, gluon dressing | no lab COM; DR grain |
| Hadronic | nucleon as three-line glob | \(\mathbf{R}^{N}\) bound inside the nucleus if the nucleus is coherent |
| Atomic / metallic | Na droplet | \(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{atom}}\) bound inside the cluster |
| Cluster | Pedalino Na nanoparticle | \(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{cluster}}\) **unbound** in the beam |
| Bench | cluster stuck to an apparatus | \(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{cluster}}\) absorbed into apparatus \(\chi\) |

Hierarchical binding is the map that moves a row down this table. DR never does that move. DR only restocks the first row.

### 4.2 Pedalino’s real target

The experiment says: the metallic glob was coherent enough to have *one* \(\mathbf{R}\), and that \(\mathbf{R}\) was isolated enough to stay a wave.

Implications for the hierarchy, not for DR:

- Coherence of generation “metal” survived milliseconds in vacuum. The child COMs (atomic, ionic) stayed internals.
- Isolation of the parent COM was good: no parent envelope (thermal photon, gas, bench) absorbed \(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{cluster}}\) into a larger \(\chi\).
- A theory that *required* every coherent glob’s COM to be classically pinned by virtue of being bound *inside* would be dead. Binding of children does not pin the parent packet. That is the same Newtonian split written up the tower.

### 4.3 Gravity follows the same COM, not DR

Residual-\(L\) gravity is assigned to the dominant glob. That glob’s \(\mathbf{R}\) is where the bound-sector stress sits for the purpose of “where is the mass.” Isolated packet motion of that same \(\mathbf{R}\) can still be a wave. Hierarchy says *which* \(L\) adds; it does not say the packet of the sum is classical.

Do not identify \(G_{\mathrm{eff}}\) with a DR duty cycle. DR can change floor occupancy; hierarchy decides whether those changes still belong to the same \(\mathbf{R}^{(g)}\).

### 4.4 Domain-cross stays on the floor

\(E_{\times}=\theta_{ab}\Delta_{\mathrm{Tav}}\) is work on a bound *line*, not on a cluster COM. A metallic glob does not hop domains as one object unless every floor line in it does. Hierarchical binding can *fail* across a domain wall: the glob splits because child lines cannot share a parent \(\mathbf{R}\) if they occupy incompatible frames. That is a COM-coherence failure, caused by a floor TEP event, not a DR of the metal.

### 4.5 What would test hierarchy

Not another slogan, and not Pedalino again.

1. Superpose \(\mathbf{R}\) of a glob, then couple one child envelope to a which-path detector. Fringe death measures when a child COM has been promoted out of \(\chi\) and is no longer internal. That is the hierarchy cut, experimentally.
2. Ask whether two globs with the same floor content but different coherence (same quarks, different residual-\(L\) packing) source different \(G_{\mathrm{eff}}\). That is the gravity sentence of the tower.
3. Keep DR path-blind. If floor rewrite ever depends on parent \(\mathbf{R}\), hierarchy and DR have been illegally fused.
