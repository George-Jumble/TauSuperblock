## What the Casimir Effect Is

The Casimir effect is the force arising from the restriction of vacuum electromagnetic fluctuations between boundaries (or in a medium) relative to the unrestricted exterior. It is ordinarily attractive for ideal parallel plates. The generalized Lifshitz theory shows that the force depends on the dielectric permittivity and magnetic permeability of the materials; under suitable conditions (e.g., ferrofluid with field-dependent \(\mu(H)\)) the force can reverse sign and become repulsive.

Recent laboratory demonstration: Zhang et al., *Nature Physics* 20, 1282–1287 (2024) — magnetic-field tuning of the Casimir force between a gold-coated sphere and a silica plate immersed in a water-based ferrofluid. The force was reversibly switched from attractive to repulsive by varying the applied magnetic field (which alters \(\mu\) of the ferrofluid). Related theoretical prediction: Ma et al., *PR Materials* 10, 025002 (2026).

## Equations (complete key)

Ideal parallel-plate Casimir pressure:

\[
P_{\rm Cas} = -\frac{\hbar c \pi^2}{240 \ell^4}
\]

- \(P_{\rm Cas}\): force per unit area arising from the imbalance of vacuum electromagnetic modes.
- \(\ell\): plate separation.
- \(\hbar\): reduced Planck constant.
- \(c\): speed of light.
- Minus sign: attractive for ideal conductors.

General Lifshitz pressure (schematic):

\[
P_{\rm Lif} = \frac{k_B T}{\pi} \sum_{n=0}^\infty{}' \int_0^\infty k_\perp\, dk_\perp\, \sum_{\rm pol} \ln\bigl(1 - r_1^{\rm pol} r_2^{\rm pol} e^{-2\kappa\ell}\bigr)
\]

- \(P_{\rm Lif}\): Lifshitz pressure (force per unit area).
- \(k_B\): Boltzmann constant.
- \(T\): temperature.
- \(n\): Matsubara frequency index; prime means the \(n=0\) term is halved.
- \(k_\perp\): transverse wave-vector magnitude.
- \(\kappa\): imaginary wave-vector component perpendicular to the plates.
- \(r_j^{\rm pol}\): Fresnel reflection coefficient of medium \(j\) for polarization pol (TE or TM).
- The reflection coefficients depend on \(\varepsilon(i\xi_n)\) and \(\mu(i\xi_n)\) (or \(\mu(H)\) for ferrofluids). Sign of \(P_{\rm Lif}\) can reverse when the reflection products change character.
