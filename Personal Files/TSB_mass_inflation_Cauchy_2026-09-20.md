# Mass inflation at Cauchy horizons

**Date:** 2026-09-20  
**Point:** why the inner Kerr horizon is not a door. Superblock reading after the GR.

---

## 1. Mechanism (Poisson–Israel)

A stationary charged or spinning hole has an inner horizon \(r_-\). In the vacuum idealization that surface is a Cauchy horizon: unique evolution from a Cauchy surface stops there.

Penrose (1968): an observer approaching \(r_-\) sees the exterior’s future infinitely blueshifted.

Poisson and Israel (1990): add the radiative tail of collapse (Price: \(\delta m\sim v^{-p}\), \(p\geq 12\) for quadrupole) and a leftover outgoing stream. The two streams counter-stream. The interior mass parameter exponentiates,

\[
\Delta m
\sim
v^{-p}\,e^{\kappa_- v},
\]

with \(\kappa_-\) the surface gravity of the inner horizon. Curvature goes Planckian on a surface near \(r_-\) (null at first, then typically spacelike). The *exterior* mass stays finite. The drama is inside. Ori (1991) wrote an explicit matching model; the early part of the singularity is often deformationally weak (finite tidal integral) even while scalars blow up.

**Key.** \(r_-\): inner / Cauchy horizon. \(\kappa_-\): its surface gravity. \(v\): advanced time. \(\Delta m\): interior mass increment. Blueshift: \(\sim e^{\kappa_- v}\).

Hamilton–Avelino picture: a relativistic two-stream accelerator. Gravity of the streaming energy feeds the acceleration. Smaller exterior accretion can inflate *faster* (less outgoing matter to cushion).

---

## 2. Not only eternal Cauchy horizons

Carballo-Rubio, Di Filippo, et al. (2024): the same exponential buildup appears for *slowly evolving* inner trapping horizons in dynamical, accreting holes, even when no exact Cauchy horizon exists. Finite but huge. Non-extremal inner horizons, including Kerr in GR and many “regular” holes with an inner horizon, are then dynamical transients, not long-lived endpoints.

Extremality (\(\kappa_-\to 0\)) kills the exponential. That is why extremal Kerr is a special case — and why a Planck–Kerr engine that stays extremal is not automatically the same object as a sub-extremal astrophysical interior.

---

## 3. What this does to Kruskal / Kerr diagrams

The vacuum ladder through \(r_-\) into white-hole blocks and further exteriors is not a physical itinerary. Perturbations replace that door with a curvature wall. You may still draw the Carter diagram. You should not send an observer, a domain face, or a child superblock through \(r_-\) on the strength of the vacuum extension.

Collapse already cut off Kruskal III and IV. Mass inflation cuts off the Kerr inner continuation.

---

## 4. Superblock reading (not a solution)

The shared Planck–Kerr ring sits at \(r=0\), \(\theta=\pi/2\), *inside* where inflation would fire if the hole is sub-extremal and has an inner horizon.

Options, none derived here:

1. **Cut before \(r_-\).** The Mirron / phase-8 map acts at or outside the inner horizon and does not use the vacuum ladder. Most honest.
2. **Keep the engine extremal.** \(\kappa_-\to 0\), exponential off. Then you owe a reason the ring stays extremal under accretion from six faces.
3. **Use inflation as the engine.** Blueshifted tail as the stress that feeds \(J_{\rm sec}\). That is a new hypothesis: mass inflation *is* the transfer current. Not written as a theorem.

Six faces do not cancel the blueshift. They can add six streams. Counter-streaming gets worse unless the faces are paired (B against W) so that net flux at \(r_-\) is arranged to vanish. That pairing is not in B_lin.

Do not write “trans-domain ring solves mass inflation.”
