## 2. Ergosphere drag

Frame-dragging changes the angular velocity a field must have. It does not assign an energy until the field or the seed has a mass. The only model-independent output of the extremal ergosphere is a fraction of that mass. A fraction times an unnamed \(M_{\rm seed}\) is not a floor.

The envelope speeds already on the books are

\[
\beta_i\in\left\{0,\tfrac{1}{15},\tfrac{1}{5},\tfrac{4}{15},\tfrac{2}{5},\tfrac{3}{5}\right\}.
\]

**Key.** \(\beta_i=V_{\rm orb}^{(i)}/c\) under \(B_{\rm lin}\). These are speeds of whole copies relative to Prime, a convention, not a measured drag of a fundamental field across \(\Phi_7\). Kinetic energy \(E=(\gamma-1)mc^2\) needs the \(m\) of whatever is orbiting. That \(m\) is not in the speed map.

Local \(v>c\) in the ergosphere remains false. Drag is co-rotation, not a superluminal transit.

## 3. Emergent electromagnetic mass

Inverted colour exciting local electromagnetic fields is the 21 July reading of \(W\) and \(Z\): the other two quarks in a three-quark state raise the locking probability. Those bosons have measured masses, \(M_W\approx 80.4\,\mathrm{GeV}\) and \(M_Z\approx 91.2\,\mathrm{GeV}\). They can be external numbers. They are electroweak masses, not the transit work of one colour line, and not \(309.3\,\mathrm{MeV}\). No clock function takes \(M_W\) to the floor without being fitted. Emergent electromagnetic flux at the Dynamic Refresh floor is a flux, not automatically a mass term.

## 4. Inversion cost

Agreed that the inversion costs energy. That cost is already stated:

\[
E_{\rm violation}\ge\Delta,\qquad\Delta>0.
\]

**Key.** \(E_{\rm violation}\): cost of a forced \(\Phi_7\to\Phi_8\) overlap. \(\Delta\): unknown positive quantum. The inequality is the requirement. It does not fix the MeV. Adding the ergosphere or \(M_W\) as a second cost is a different protocol, and it needs a rule for how the two add. None is written.
