## 1. Live Predictive Possibilities (Current State)

**Plain language**: Gravity you measure in the lab or solar system is residual angular momentum that was “stopped” when a bound bounce hit the present. **Observable consequences**:

- No unscreened fifth force or equivalence-principle violation at those scales.

### 1.2 Hierarchical residual-L on SPARC rotation curves
**Proposition**: Rotation curves of disk galaxies are produced by residual-L hierarchical globbing under the dominant-glob rule with laboratory \(G\) normalization. A single free core radius per galaxy (or a hierarchy-derived core) should improve \(\chi^2\) relative to pure baryonic models and be competitive with NFW/Einasto profiles.

**Plain language**: The “missing mass” in galaxies is not a new particle; it is the extra residual angular momentum accumulated by nested bound systems. The same rule that gives laboratory \(G\) should also shape galactic rotation curves, needing only one adjustable size scale per galaxy.

**Observable consequences**: Independent SPARC fits (real MassModels data) should show residual-L hierarchical models better than pure baryons in a majority of galaxies and competitive with simple dark-matter profiles. Planted modules must not be used for the claim.

### 1.3 Low dark-halo outliers

**Proposition**: \(E_\times(a\to b) = \theta_{ab}\Delta\). With printed \(\Delta = 309.3\,\mathrm{MeV}\): adjacent hop \(\approx 485.5\,\mathrm{MeV}\), antipodal hop \(\approx 971.0\,\mathrm{MeV}\). Unbound light pays zero. Blocked target adds \(E_{\rm violation}\ge\Delta\).
