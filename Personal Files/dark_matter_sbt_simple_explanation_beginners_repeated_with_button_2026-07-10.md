## What Is the "Dark Matter" Puzzle in Normal Science?

In everyday astronomy and physics, scientists notice that galaxies and clusters of galaxies behave as if they contain much more mass (stuff that has gravity) than we can actually see.

- Stars at the edges of galaxies spin around the center much faster than they should based on the visible stars, gas, and dust alone.
- Light from distant galaxies bends (lenses) more than expected.
- Galaxy clusters hold together even though the visible matter should not provide enough gravity.

Scientists call this invisible extra gravity source "**dark matter**." The standard explanation is that it must be made of some unknown kind of particle (often called WIMPs or axions) that we have not yet detected in laboratories.

The Tav-Superblock model offers a completely different answer: **there is no new particle**. The extra gravity comes from the way our visible universe is connected to hidden extra layers of reality.
