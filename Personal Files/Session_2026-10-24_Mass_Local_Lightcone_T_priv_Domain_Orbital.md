## Plain-language summary

The mass inside the local light-cone (the causal volume out to the hard wall) belonging to one local privileged-observer time \(T_{\rm priv}\) is taken to be the total matter mass of the observable universe, as measured by Planck 2018 parameters. That mass is approximately \(9.68 \times 10^{53}\) kilograms. Because the Superblock has six co-located domains, the sum over all six is six times larger: approximately \(5.81 \times 10^{54}\) kilograms.

This is an observational anchor, not a prediction of the locked residual-L dynamics.

## Analytical calculation

### Local light-cone mass of one \(T_{\rm priv}\)

The hard wall of a local \(T_{\rm priv}\) is identified, for the purpose of this estimate, with the causal boundary of the observable universe (comoving radius \(\approx 46.5\) Gly).

**Key to symbols**  
- \(\rho_{\rm crit}\): critical density for a flat universe.  
- \(H_0\): Hubble constant.  
- \(\Omega_m\): matter density parameter (baryons + cold dark matter + massive neutrinos).  
- \(\rho_m = \Omega_m \rho_{\rm crit}\): mean matter density.  
- \(R\): comoving radius of the observable universe.  
- \(V = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3\): comoving volume.  
- \(M_{\rm local}\): total matter mass inside that volume for one domain.  
- \(M_\odot = 1.989 \times 10^{30}\) kg: solar mass.

Planck 2018 (TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing) values used:  
\(H_0 = 67.4\) km s\(^{-1}\) Mpc\(^{-1}\), \(\Omega_m = 0.315\).  
Corresponding \(\rho_{\rm crit} \approx 8.62 \times 10^{-27}\) kg m\(^{-3}\).  
Thus  
\[
\rho_m = 0.315 \times 8.62 \times 10^{-27} \approx 2.72 \times 10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}.
\]

With \(R \approx 46.5\) Gly \(\approx 4.40 \times 10^{26}\) m,  
\[
V \approx 3.57 \times 10^{80}\ \mathrm{m^3}.
\]

Therefore  
\[
M_{\rm local} = \rho_m V \approx 9.68 \times 10^{53}\ \mathrm{kg} \approx 4.87 \times 10^{23}\,M_\odot.
\]

This is the standard estimate for the total matter (dark + baryonic) content of the observable universe; ordinary baryonic matter is roughly 5 % of that figure.

### Sum over six co-located domains

The live Superblock frame has six co-located copies. Summing the local light-cone mass over all six gives  
\[
M_6 = 6 \times M_{\rm local} \approx 5.81 \times 10^{54}\ \mathrm{kg} \approx 2.92 \times 10^{24}\,M_\odot.
\]

**Number reported**: \(M_6 \approx 5.81 \times 10^{54}\) kg.

### Domain orbital from galactic clusters and dark-mass observations

Earlier session (2026-10-24) wrote the equal-mass CPT domain orbital kinetic term as  
\[
E_{\rm orb} = 3 M \langle\beta^2\rangle c^2,
\]  
where \(M\) is a domain mass (taken here as \(M_{\rm local}\)), \(\beta = v/c\), and \(\langle\beta^2\rangle\) is an average of the squared relative speed. The factor 3 is bookkeeping for the equal-mass CPT assignment; it is not derived from residual-L or from the six-domain geometry. Volume and speeds remain unfixed by locked structure, so the term is a free phenomenological anchor.

**Key to symbols (additional)**  
- \(\sigma_v\): line-of-sight velocity dispersion of galaxies inside a rich cluster.  
- \(\beta = \sigma_v / c\).  
- \(c = 2.998 \times 10^8\) m s\(^{-1}\).  
- \(E_{\rm orb}\): kinetic energy of the domain orbital.

Rich galaxy clusters (masses \(10^{14}\)–\(10^{15}\,M_\odot\), \(\sim 80\)–\(90\) % dark matter) exhibit \(\sigma_v \sim 800\)–\(1200\) km s\(^{-1}\). Taking the representative value \(\sigma_v = 1000\) km s\(^{-1}\):  
\[
\beta = \frac{10^6}{2.998 \times 10^8} \approx 3.34 \times 10^{-3},\qquad \langle\beta^2\rangle \approx 1.11 \times 10^{-5}.
\]

Inserting \(M = M_{\rm local}\) yields  
\[
E_{\rm orb} \approx 3 \times (9.68 \times 10^{53})\times (1.11 \times 10^{-5})\times (9.0 \times 10^{16}) \approx 2.91 \times 10^{66}\ \mathrm{J}.
\]

This number uses the observed cluster velocity scale as a proxy for the free parameter \(\langle\beta^2\rangle\). It is not a prediction of the locked residual-L gravity, nor does it fix \(\rho_\Lambda\). Dark-mass observations enter only insofar as the cluster velocity dispersions themselves are inferred from the total (mostly dark) mass via the virial theorem.
