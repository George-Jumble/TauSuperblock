## 0. Verdict

**Not the equal-share constituent. That number is the floor.**

The constituent up is not a measured mass. Estimator A defines it as \(M_n/3\) or \(M_p/3\). Using it as \(M_*\) is the insertion on both sides of \(\Delta=f M_*\). Estimator B, the magnetic-moment constituent \(\approx 336\,\mathrm{MeV}\), is a different operator. It can be an external number. It is still not a clock output, and \(f\) is still unwritten.

Current up, \(2.16\,\mathrm{MeV}\), is measured and is not the constituent. It remains the Higgs remainder.

## 2. Why Estimator A fails as an anchor

A constituent quark mass is a model parameter in a non-relativistic or bag picture, chosen so that three of them roughly add to the nucleon. Estimator A is that choice made equal:

\[
m_u^{\rm const,A}=\frac{M_n}{3}.
\]

**Key.** Left side: a name. Right side: the neutron third. Putting this \(m_u^{\rm const,A}\) in as \(M_*\) gives \(\Delta=f M_n/3\). Unless \(f\) is fixed by the clock before this identification, the only natural \(f\) is \(1\), and the equation is the protocol again.

---

## 3. What Estimator B can and cannot do

Estimator B extracts a constituent mass from nucleon magnetic moments in the non-relativistic quark model, about \(336\,\mathrm{MeV}\). It is not defined as \(M_n/3\). It may sit on the right-hand side as an external number. A clock derivation would still have to supply \(f\) first, then hit or miss \(309.3\,\mathrm{MeV}\) or whichever operator was named. No such \(f\) is written. Magnetic moment is not per-line dressing. Agreement would be a comparison of two quark-model conventions, not a derivation from \((\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})^\times\).
