## Suppressive reading

Before inversion, \(T_{\rm priv}\) (or the contrast built from it) acts suppressively:

\[
\rho_{\rm eff}
=
\rho_{\rm bare}
-
\kappa\,
\bigl|T_{\rm priv}
-
\mathcal{G}(\{t_{\rm local}\})\bigr|
\,
\rho_{\rm carrier}.
\]

**Key.** \(\rho_{\rm bare}\): bare density (virtual/Casimir, wall potential, or radiation). The second term is the suppressive force associated with the privileged-observer contrast. \(\kappa>0\) reduces the effective density.

This is one concrete realization of the radiation-pressure or screening suppression examined in the preceding note: the privileged-observer locus supplies the reference that cancels or dilutes the bare term.

## Inversion to inertial addition

Invert the role. The same contrast changes sign and becomes an inertial addition:

\[
\rho_{\rm eff}
=
\rho_{\rm bare}
+
\kappa\,
\bigl|T_{\rm priv}
-
\mathcal{G}_{\rm inv}(\{t_{\rm local}\})\bigr|
\,
\rho_{\rm carrier}.
\]

**Key.** The minus sign has become a plus. \(\mathcal{G}_{\rm inv}\) indicates that the globbing arrow may also be reversed, but the essential change requested here is the sign of the \(T_{\rm priv}\) contribution. The addition is called inertial because it augments the effective source term that accelerates expansion.

Equivalently, one may keep a single expression and let a discrete inversion operator \(\mathcal{I}\) flip the sign:

\[
\rho_{\rm eff}
=
\rho_{\rm bare}
+
\mathcal{I}(T_{\rm priv})\,
\kappa\,
|\Delta t|
\,
\rho_{\rm carrier},
\]

where \(\mathcal{I}=-1\) in the suppressive phase and \(\mathcal{I}=+1\) in the additive phase.

## Does the inversion derive lambda?

No.

- The magnitude of the addition is set by the product \(\kappa\,|\Delta t|\,\rho_{\rm carrier}\). None of these three factors is fixed by the clock, the floor protocol, or the residual-L matching condition.
- If \(\rho_{\rm carrier}\) is the virtual/Casimir density, the inversion merely decides whether that density is subtracted or added; the \(10^{120}\) hierarchy must still be removed by a free suppression before the sum can equal the observed value.
- If \(\rho_{\rm carrier}\) is the unbound wall potential or a radiation-pressure term, the addition is one more phenomenological contribution to the already-fitted Mirron/Entropic term.
- The sign flip itself can be associated with the existing black-to-white stroke or with the bound/unbound distinction, but that association does not evaluate the coefficient.

The construction therefore lets \(T_{\rm priv}\) change from a suppressive force into an inertial addition, yet the numerical value of the addition remains free.
