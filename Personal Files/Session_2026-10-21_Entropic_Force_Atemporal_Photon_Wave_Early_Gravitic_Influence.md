## Atemporal dispersing photon wave

The photon is an unbound null quantum: \(d\tau=0\) on its ray, completed bounce deposits energy with zero residual angular momentum.

A dispersing wave means a null congruence (or a bundle of null rays) that spreads while remaining atemporal. Its energy density falls with geometric dilution and with any residual-L focusing or defocusing.

The entropic force is identified with the gradient associated with this wave:
\[
\mathbf{F}_{\rm ent}
\sim
-\nabla\rho_{\rm photon}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\mathbf{F}_{\rm ent}
\sim
\mathbf{k}_{\rm wave}\times\text{(dispersion factor)},
\]
where the direction is toward the wall (locus of completion).

**Variable key:**  
- \(\mathbf{F}_{\rm ent}\): entropic force.  
- \(\rho_{\rm photon}\): energy density of the unbound photonic wave.  
- \(\mathbf{k}_{\rm wave}\): wave vector / null direction.  
- Wall: locus where the bounce completes and \(L_{\rm res}=0\).

Force lessens near the wall: as the wave approaches the completion locus, its local gradient (or the rate of further dispersion) decreases. This can be read as the energy being deposited onto the wall potential, so the driving gradient flattens.

## Gravitic influence of the early compressed universe

Early universe: high bound-field density \(\Rightarrow\) strong residual-\(L\) compression (spatial compression from stopped rebounds).

Residual \(L\) acts on null geodesics by gravitational lensing / focusing (ordinary geodesic deviation of null rays). The optical scalar equations give the expansion \(\theta\) of a null congruence:
\[
\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda}
=
-\frac12\theta^2
-
|\sigma|^2
-
R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu
+
\text{residual-}L\text{ contribution}.
\]

**Variable key:**  
- \(\theta\): expansion scalar of the null congruence (positive = dispersing).  
- \(\lambda\): affine parameter along the null ray.  
- \(\sigma\): shear.  
- \(R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu\): Ricci focusing term (from bound energy density / residual \(L\)).  
- residual-\(L\) contribution: additional compression term proportional to local bound-field density (hierarchical residual angular momentum).

In the early, compressed universe the Ricci and residual-\(L\) terms are large and negative (focusing). They counteract geometric dispersion, keeping the photonic wave more collimated or delayed in its approach to the wall.  

As the universe expands and residual-\(L\) density drops, focusing weakens. Geometric dispersion (and any residual defocusing) dominates, the wave spreads more freely, and a larger fraction of its energy reaches the wall locus.  

The force lessens near the wall because, once the ray is close to completion, further focusing or dispersion has already acted; the remaining gradient is the final deposition onto \(V_{\rm wall}\).
