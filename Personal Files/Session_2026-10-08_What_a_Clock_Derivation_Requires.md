## 0. Verdict

**Three things, and none of them is another look at \(M_n/3\).**

A clock derivation needs an external dimensionful anchor that is not the floor, a dimensionless function of the clock fixed before the nucleon is consulted, and a named operator so the output can miss the data. The live clock supplies only the middle ingredient’s raw material. It does not supply the anchor. Until the anchor is named, the print stays \(309.3\,\mathrm{MeV}\) as a protocol.

## 2. Why the clock alone fails

The clock is a finite group and a phase step. TEP is an inequality. Together:

\[
E_{\rm violation}\ge\Delta,\qquad\Delta>0,
\qquad
f=f(6,\,2\pi/7,\,\Phi_7,\Phi_8).
\]

**Key.** \(E_{\rm violation}\): cost of a forced bound overlap. \(\Delta\): unknown positive quantum. \(f\): a pure number once it is specified. A pure number times no mass is not a mass. \(\hbar\) and \(c\) convert a mass to a length after the mass exists. They do not choose it.

## 3. The three requirements

### 3.1 An anchor that is not the answer

\[
\Delta_{\rm clock}=f\,M_*.
\]

**Key.** \(M_*\): a measured or theoretical mass fixed independently of the nucleon dressing share. \(f\): clock function. \(\Delta_{\rm clock}\): the output.

Allowed anchors, if stated as such: a lepton mass, a boson mass, \(\Lambda_{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}\) taken from a lattice scheme that did not use this floor, \(\sqrt{\sigma}\) likewise. Disallowed while current cuts stand: \(M_n\), \(M_n/3\), \(E_{\rm dress}/3\), \(\tau\), and \(M_{\rm Pl}\) (uses \(G\)).

### 3.2 The function written first

\(f\) has to be an expression in the clock alone — order 6, slip \(2\pi/7\), empty residue \(0\bmod 7\), associator class of the antipodal hop — published before anyone divides a nucleon mass. Fitting \(f\) so that \(f M_*=309.3\,\mathrm{MeV}\) is the retired Planck-average move. It is not a derivation.

### 3.3 An operator that can miss

Say which mass is being computed. Per-line dressing, string tension, and the lightest glueball are different operators. Lattice glueball masses sit near \(1.5\)–\(1.8\,\mathrm{GeV}\). \(\sqrt{\sigma}\approx 440\,\mathrm{MeV}\). The dressing share is \(309.3\,\mathrm{MeV}\). A derivation that can only hit one of them by renaming has not derived it.

Then compare. A miss is a result. Redefining \(\Delta\) as whatever came out is the insertion again.
