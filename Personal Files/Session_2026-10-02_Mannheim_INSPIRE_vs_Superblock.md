## 3. Mannheim equations that matter, with keys

### 3.1 Weyl action and Bach tensor

Conformal gravity replaces the Einstein–Hilbert term by the square of the Weyl tensor:

\[
I_W=-\alpha_g\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,C_{\mu\lambda\nu\kappa}C^{\mu\lambda\nu\kappa}.
\]

The vacuum field equation is the vanishing of the Bach tensor. Mannheim’s 2026 statement of that tensor is

\[
W^{\mu\nu}=\bigl[2\nabla_\kappa\nabla_\lambda-R_{\lambda\kappa}\bigr]C^{\mu\lambda\nu\kappa}.
\]

**Key**

- \(I_W\): conformal gravitational action.
- \(\alpha_g\): dimensionless conformal coupling.
- \(g_{\mu\nu}\): 4D metric; \(g=\det g_{\mu\nu}\); \(\sqrt{-g}\): volume density.
- \(x\): spacetime point; \(d^4x\): coordinate volume.
- \(C_{\mu\lambda\nu\kappa}\): Weyl conformal tensor. Vanishes iff the metric is locally conformally flat.
- \(W^{\mu\nu}\): Bach tensor. Fourth-order. Vacuum conformal equation is \(W^{\mu\nu}=0\).
- \(\nabla_\kappa\): Levi-Civita covariant derivative.
- \(R_{\lambda\kappa}\): Ricci tensor.
- Indices \(\mu,\nu,\lambda,\kappa\): 4D spacetime.

Robertson–Walker geometry forced by the cosmological principle has \(C=0\) and therefore \(W=0\). That is the starting point of arXiv:2508.02756.

### 3.2 Mannheim–Kazanas exterior metric

Exact static spherical vacuum solution of conformal Weyl gravity (Astrophys. J. 342 (1989) 635), written in the form used in later reviews:

\[
-g_{00}=\frac{1}{g_{rr}}=B(r)=1-3\beta\gamma-\frac{\beta(2-3\beta\gamma)}{r}+\gamma r-kr^2.
\]

A common reduced writing, after the constant \(1-3\beta\gamma\) is absorbed into the normalization of the static chart, is

\[
B(r)=1-\frac{2\beta}{r}+\gamma r-kr^2,
\]

with line element

\[
ds^2=-B(r)\,c^2\,dt^2+B(r)^{-1}dr^2+r^2 d\Omega^2.
\]

The 2012 galaxy fit (Phys. Rev. D 85, 124020) reads the linear and quadratic pieces as universal potentials

\[
V_{\rm lin}(r)=\frac{\gamma_0 c^2 r}{2},\qquad \gamma_0=3.06\times 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1},
\]

\[
V_{\rm quad}(r)=-\frac{\kappa c^2 r^2}{2}.
\]

**Key**

- \(B(r)\): redshift / lapse function in the static spherical chart.
- \(r\): areal radial coordinate.
- \(t\): static time.
- \(c\): speed of light.
- \(d\Omega^2=d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2\): unit 2-sphere.
- \(\beta\): integration constant, dimension length. Solar-system mass parameter. Related to \(GM/c^2\), not identical to the Schwarzschild radius once the \(\beta\gamma\) cross term is kept.
- \(\gamma\): integration constant, dimension \(1/\mathrm{length}\). Source of the linear potential. Universal piece \(\gamma_0\) is fixed by the cosmological background in the Mannheim–O’Brien fit, not by each galaxy.
- \(k\) or \(\kappa\): integration constant, dimension \(1/\mathrm{length}^2\). Quadratic cosmological term. de Sitter-like background in the conformal vacuum, not an Einstein \(\Lambda\) put in by hand.
- \(V_{\rm lin}\), \(V_{\rm quad}\): effective potentials whose gradients give the extra accelerations.
- \(G\): Newton constant, appearing only in the recovered \(1/r\) piece.
- \(M\): luminous mass. The fit’s free galactic parameter is the mass-to-light ratio, not a dark halo.

On galactic scales the claimed acceleration is

\[
a=-\frac{\beta(2-3\beta\gamma)}{r^2}-\gamma.
\]

**Key:** \(a\) is coordinate acceleration per unit mass. First term is the Newtonian piece. Second term is a constant acceleration set by \(\gamma\).

This is a published fit claim (111 spirals in the 2012 paper; no dark halo). It is not a community lock. Cluster lensing, CMB peaks, and light bending remain contested. Mannheim’s reply literature includes the 2015 comment Phys. Rev. D 93, 068501.

### 3.3 Ghost, PT inner product, CPT extension

Fourth-order gravity has a Pais–Uhlenbeck propagator schematically

\[
D(k)\sim\frac{1}{k^2-M_1^2}-\frac{1}{k^2-M_2^2}.
\]

The relative minus sign is the ghost if the inner product is the Dirac norm. Bender and Mannheim replace that norm by the PT inner product: the Hamiltonian is not Hermitian, but it is antilinear-PT symmetric, and the inner product is the overlap of a ket with its PT conjugate rather than its Hermitian conjugate (Phys. Rev. Lett. 100 (2008) 110402; Nuovo Cim. C 45 (2022) 27, arXiv:2109.12743).

The 2016 CPT extension (Phys. Lett. B 753, 288) says the CPT theorem can be stated for non-Hermitian Hamiltonians and unstable states, with antilinearity rather than Hermiticity as the guiding principle (J. Phys. A 51 (2018) 315302).

**Key**

- \(D(k)\): momentum-space propagator.
- \(k^2\): invariant square of the 4-momentum.
- \(M_1,M_2\): the two mass poles of the fourth-order theory.
- \(P\): linear parity.
- \(T\): antilinear time reversal.
- \(C\): charge conjugation, in the CPT extension.
- Dirac norm: \(\langle\psi|\psi\rangle\) with Hermitian conjugation.
- PT norm: overlap with the PT conjugate. This is Mannheim’s proposed cure, not a Superblock object.
