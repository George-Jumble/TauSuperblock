\begin{center}\textbf{Abstract}\end{center}

\begin{quote}\small
Superblock Cosmology is a strictly four-dimensional (3+1) geometric account of gravity and cosmic structure built on six co-located domains, a single Mirron fabric with a 313.1 MeV floor, and a seventh phase, $\Phi_7$. This paper answers one question: how the model's dark-energy ledger accounts for the observed dark-energy density, $\Omega_\Lambda\approx0.685$ (Planck 2018). Under the author's rulings of 17--18 November 2026, three sources supply dark energy: a persisting bound-bubble flow under the wall lid (92.8\% of observed, $w=-1$), a charm-plus-bottom impulse carried by the photonic-quark white bounce (20.6\%, $w=-1$), and a small dust-like down-plus-strange term (0.37\%, $w=0$). Together they supply $S\approx113.8\%$ of what is observed. The surplus is removed by a drain that is now derived rather than fitted: each Mirron's black--white Kerr-ring loop binds part of its expanding (space-like) residual-$L$ channel inside its own event horizon. Three half-bounces in the gluon exchange each halve the energy, giving a bound share $f_b=(1/2)^3=1/8$, shared across the two channels at $1/16$ each and counted once. The felt dark energy is $(1-f_b)S\approx99.6\%$ of observed, and the drain is $\Omega\approx0.097$ [estimate]. The time dependence (lid plus a fading $\eta$ spin-down) reproduces the DESI DR2 dynamical-dark-energy signal (DESY5: $w_0\approx-0.77$, $w_a\approx-0.70$, $\Delta\chi^2\approx-16$). The Hubble tension is not resolved: the best ledger gives $H_0\approx69.6$ with SH0ES calibration and about $4.1\sigma$ tension without it. Open items are listed explicitly.
\end{quote}

\noindent\textit{Plain-language summary.} The model has more sources of dark energy than the sky shows, about 14\% too much. This paper explains where the extra goes: every Mirron keeps one-eighth of its outward push locked behind its own tiny horizon, and the one-eighth comes from halving the energy three times. What is left over matches the observed dark energy to within half a percent. The model also reproduces the recent hint from DESI that dark energy is weakening, but it does not solve the disagreement about how fast the universe is expanding today.

## 1. The question

Observation fixes the dark-energy density today. Planck 2018 gives $\Omega_\Lambda=0.6847\pm0.0073$ [1]. DESI DR2 BAO, combined with the CMB and supernovae, prefers dark energy that is slightly weaker today than in the past ($w_0>-1$, $w_a<0$) at 2.8--4.2$\sigma$ [2]. Any dark-energy ledger therefore has to answer two questions:

1. **Amount.** Do the model's sources add up to the observed $\Omega_\Lambda$, without a free parameter tuned to make them do so?
2. **Shape.** Does the sum evolve with redshift the way DESI, DES and Pantheon+ see it?

In plain terms, a theory of dark energy must get both the size right and the way it changes over cosmic history right. Sections 3--5 deal with the size; Section 6 deals with the change.

## 2. Background from the canon

Only the parts of the canon needed here are summarised. Gravity is residual-$L$ ($L_{\rm res}$): a bound bounce stopped at the Present leaves $L_{\rm res}$, which sources gravity. It is a dual-channel force (canon D4, D5). The time-like channel (the collapsing-wave twistor) attracts and matches laboratory $G$. The space-like channel (the expanding-wave twistor, antigravity) is an outward push on the now-slice, deposited atemporally on the hard wall. Residual-$L$ is always positive; the space-like channel carries $+|L|$ with an outward direction and is never written as a negative $L$.

The Mirron floor is $\Delta=313.1$ MeV with $r_M=\hbar c/\Delta=0.6302$ fm (canon D10). The full photonic bounce is six signed ticks summing to zero ($L_{\rm res}=0$); the half-bounce is three ticks and leaves residual $L$ (canon D9). $\Phi_7$ is the Higgs fabric through all domains, matter when bound and kinetic repulsion when unbound (canon N27).

*Plain language:* gravity in this model is the leftover spin of matter caught in the present moment. It works in two ways at once: one pulls things together, the other pushes space apart. The push is what this paper books as dark energy.

## 3. The supply

**Author rulings (17--18 November 2026).** Ernest's ledger is canonical for the four-dimensional side (ruling 3, 17 Nov). The supply is counted as supply, not as felt dark energy (item 7). The photonic-quark bounce is the *carrier* of the charm-plus-bottom impulse, not a separate term (item 6, 18 Nov), and a small separate dust-like down-plus-strange term is admitted (item 6).

$$
S \;=\; f_{\rm flow} + f_{c+b} + f_{d+s} \;=\; 92.8\% + 20.6\% + 0.37\% \;\approx\; 113.8\%
$$

*Key:*

- $S$ is the total dark-energy supply today, as a percentage of the observed dark-energy density $\rho_\Lambda$.
- $f_{\rm flow}=92.8\%$ is the bound-bubble flow, $\gamma$-weighted over the forward flavours (Ernest import DE5). It is a persisting deposit under the lid, equation of state $w=-1$.
- $f_{c+b}=4.8\%+15.8\%=20.6\%$ is the charm and bottom impulse, $1/6$ of each flavour's measured mass per bound Mirron (rulings 4 and 11, 17 Nov), carried by the photonic-quark white bounce and booked at $w=-1$ inside the lid.
- $f_{d+s}=0.018\%+0.35\%\approx0.37\%$ is the down and strange photonic-quark term, dust-like ($w=0$), non-clustering.
- $w$ is the equation of state, pressure divided by energy density.

Without the down-plus-strange term the supply is Ernest's $92.8\%+20.6\%=113.4\%$.

*Plain language:* there are three taps filling the dark-energy tank. The big one is a steady flow left behind by bound matter. The second is a push from the heavy charm and bottom quarks, delivered by a bounce that moves like light without being light. The third is a tiny trickle from the down and strange quarks that thins out like ordinary matter. Together they pour in about 114\% of what we observe.

### 3.1 Why the photonic-quark bounce is booked at $w=-1$

The bounce runs at $c$ and closes as a null circuit, but the energy sits in the carrier at a fixed $m_q/6$, set by measured mass, not by a wavelength. The density is then number of carriers times fixed energy per carrier:

$$
\rho_{pq}\;\propto\; n_{\rm carrier}(a)\,\frac{m_q}{6},\qquad \rho\propto a^{-3(1+w)}
$$

*Key:*

- $\rho_{pq}$ is the energy density of the photonic-quark bounce term.
- $n_{\rm carrier}(a)$ is the number density of carriers at scale factor $a$.
- $m_q$ is the measured (PDG) mass of quark flavour $q$; $m_q/6$ is the energy per photonic quark (ruling 4).
- $a=1/(1+z)$ is the scale factor and $z$ the redshift; the second relation links the dilution law to $w$.

If the carriers ride on bound Mirrons ($n\propto a^{-3}$) the term is dust-like, $w=0$. If every point of space keeps a fixed share, $w=-1$, identical to the lid. Radiation-like behaviour ($w=1/3$) would need the energy per bounce to redshift like a photon, which the author excludes; it would also fail $N_{\rm eff}$ by a factor of roughly 30--1600 [estimate]. At the full 20.6\% amplitude, a dust-like booking is strongly excluded ($\Delta\chi^2=+68$ with SH0ES, $+95$ without, relative to the working ledger), and a fit with $w$ free returns $w\to-1.00$ [fit, ref. Photonic-quark report]. Only the small $d+s$ term is dust-like.

*Plain language:* what thins out as space grows is the number of carriers, not the energy each carries. The data allow the big charm-plus-bottom share only if it behaves like a cosmological constant, so it is counted inside the steady floor. The tiny down-plus-strange share can behave like matter without harm.

## 4. The drain: horizon-bound residual-$L$ (derived 18 November 2026)

The supply overshoots observation by about 13.8\%:

$$
\Delta\Omega \;\approx\; (S-1)\,\Omega_\Lambda \;\approx\; 0.138\times0.685\;\approx\;0.095\quad\text{[estimate]}
$$

*Key:*

- $\Delta\Omega$ is the excess dark energy before any drain, in units of the critical density.
- $S\approx1.138$ is the supply as a fraction of observed dark energy (Section 3).
- $\Omega_\Lambda\approx0.685$ is the observed dark-energy share (Planck 2018 [1]).

On 17 November the drain was first treated as a fit ($\approx0.09$ in $\Omega$) and then derived as a bookkeeping difference, supply minus felt dark energy, $\approx0.08$--$0.10$. On 18 November it was given a physical source.

*Plain language:* before anything is taken away, the model has about 0.095 too much dark energy in the usual cosmic units. Something has to remove it, and until now that something was only a number chosen to fit.

### 4.1 Mechanism (author ruling, 18 November 2026)

Every Mirron's black--white Kerr-ring loop binds part of its expanding (space-like, antigravity) residual-$L$ channel inside its own event horizon. Energy bound inside a horizon does not feed cosmic expansion. Seen from outside it counts only as mass; inside it acts as outward core pressure that holds off a singularity and fits the black-to-white bounce.

$$
S_{\rm felt} \;=\; (1-f_b)\,S
$$

*Key:*

- $S_{\rm felt}$ is the dark energy actually felt by expansion, as a fraction of observed.
- $f_b$ is the share of the expanding channel bound inside each Mirron's horizon (the drain).
- $S$ is the total supply (Section 3).

Two alternatives were checked and set aside. Binding only in astrophysical black holes removes less than 1\% of the supply, more than ten times too little. Full binding in every Mirron removes nearly all dark energy. The author ruled partial binding in every Mirron.

*Plain language:* each Mirron is a tiny black-to-white loop with its own horizon. Some of its outward push gets caught inside that horizon and never reaches the rest of the universe. Only the part that escapes is dark energy we can feel.

### 4.2 The bound share from three half-bounces (author rulings, 18 November 2026)

The author ruled, in order: the bound share is the Mirron's time-like share, doubled by the dual-channel nature of $L_{\rm res}$; that time-like share is the bound-quark domain's share of the superblock mass; the bound quark's domain is always Prime and is always the lightest quark in its own frame; the share is set by the gluon exchange at the half-bounce; and that exchange contains three half-bounces, each halving the energy.

$$
\epsilon_t \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{3} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{16} \;=\; 0.0625,
\qquad
f_b \;=\; 2\,\epsilon_t \;=\; \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{3} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{8} \;=\; 0.125
$$

*Key:*

- $\epsilon_t$ is the Mirron's time-like share per channel, equal to the bound-quark domain's share of the superblock mass as set by the gluon exchange.
- $\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{3}$ is the three half-bounces of the gluon exchange, each passing on half the energy.
- The leading $\tfrac{1}{2}$ splits that energy between the two $L_{\rm res}$ channels.
- The factor 2 in $f_b$ adds the two channels back together.
- $f_b$ is the total bound share (the drain).

*Plain language:* each half-bounce passes on half the energy, so after three only one-eighth is left. That eighth is shared between the two channels of gravity, a sixteenth each. It stays behind the Mirron's horizon. The bound quark's domain is light because, to every observer, its own domain is Prime and its own bound quark is the lightest; the share is set by gluon binding energy, not by bare quark mass.

**Count-once (closed, 18 November 2026).** Ruling 7 says energy acting in both channels is counted once. Here the $\tfrac12$ and the $2$ cancel: the bound one-eighth is a single energy, $1/16$ in each channel, not two eighths. The earlier wording, in which $0.06$ looked like a full share that the dual channel doubled, is superseded.

*Plain language:* one-eighth is caught, and it is the same eighth seen in both channels, so it is counted once.

**Rejected alternative.** Dividing among the six domains across the three half-bounces gives $\epsilon_t=1/18\approx0.056$, $f_b=1/9$, and $S_{\rm felt}\approx101.1\%$ [estimate]. It is close but worse, and halving is the natural rule for a half-bounce.

### 4.3 Result

$$
S_{\rm felt} \;=\; \tfrac{7}{8}\times113.8\% \;\approx\; 99.6\%,
\qquad
\Omega_{\rm drain} \;=\; f_b\,S\,\Omega_\Lambda \;\approx\; 0.125\times1.138\times0.685 \;\approx\; 0.097\quad\text{[estimate]}
$$

*Key:*

- $S_{\rm felt}$ is the felt dark energy as a percentage of observed. With the unrounded supply $S=113.77\%$ it is $99.55\%$.
- $\Omega_{\rm drain}$ is the drain expressed as a share of the critical density.
- $f_b=1/8$, $S\approx1.138$ and $\Omega_\Lambda\approx0.685$ are as above.

Without the down-plus-strange term, $S_{\rm felt}=\tfrac78\times113.4\%\approx99.2\%$ [estimate]. The derived drain, 0.097, sits inside the 0.08--0.10 band that the DESI DR2 fit required for drain plus wall surplus (Section 6) and close to the $\approx0.099$ found in the DESY5 full fit [fit].

*Plain language:* seven-eighths of the supply is felt, and that lands within half a percent of the observed dark energy, with no number tuned to make it so. The amount removed matches what the earlier data fit needed.

### 4.4 Side effects of the bound energy

Bound energy appears outside the horizon as extra mass and inside as core pressure. The working estimate carried with the ruling is that the added mass is at most about 0.1\% of matter, within Planck's margin [estimate; see open item 7, which records that this bound has not been re-derived for this paper].

*Plain language:* the caught energy does not vanish. From outside it weighs a little; the estimate is that it adds a very small amount to the matter budget.

## 5. Dark matter is not drawn on

The locked dark-to-ordinary matter ratio is $R_{\rm SB}=5.4015$ against Planck's $5.364\pm0.065$ (canon test, $+0.58\sigma$).

$$
\frac{R_{\rm SB}-R_{\rm Planck}}{\sigma_R} \;=\; \frac{5.4015-5.364}{0.065}\;\approx\;0.58
$$

*Key:*

- $R_{\rm SB}$ is the Superblock ratio of dark to ordinary matter (Ansatz A).
- $R_{\rm Planck}$ is the ratio inferred from Planck 2018 [1], $\Omega_c/\Omega_b$.
- $\sigma_R$ is its one-standard-deviation uncertainty.

The down-plus-strange term is admitted as a contributing source to dark energy (item 6, 18 November) and is booked there, so it does not add to this ratio. Were it booked as dark matter instead, it would add about 0.0025 in $\Omega$ and raise the overshoot to about $+1.7\%$ ($\approx1.4\sigma$) [estimate].

*Plain language:* the dark-energy ledger takes nothing from dark matter, and dark matter already agrees with Planck to well within the error bars.

## 6. Time dependence and the DESI signal

**Author rulings (17 November 2026):** the flow is a persisting deposit under the lid (flat $w=-1$); $Q$ is a gradual release after $z\approx2$ with $p\approx2.5$--$3$; $\eta$ spin-down stays a live fading share; $k$ is re-read upward to the base share; the friction brake scales as $a^{-3}$ with amplitude unset; the aeon radius expands without limit and $k$ sets only the lid ($R_M\cdot7^k\approx88\ \mu$m) and the aeon mass; $V_{\rm light\text{-}cone}$ is the volume inside the future event horizon. Lid plus $\eta$ spin-down is the minimal DESI-compatible ledger (item 8).

$$
\frac{H^2(z)}{H_0^2}\,h^2 \;=\; \omega_{m,\rm bg}(1+z)^3 + \omega_r(1+z)^4 + \omega_{\rm DE}\Big[(1-F_Q-F_\eta) + F_Q\,s_Q(z;p) + F_\eta\,e_\eta(z)\Big]
$$

*Key:*

- $H(z)$ is the expansion rate at redshift $z$; $H_0$ its value today; $h=H_0/(100$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1})$.
- $\omega_x=\Omega_x h^2$ are physical densities.
- $\omega_{m,\rm bg}=\omega_{cb}+\omega_\nu-\omega_{\rm fr}$ is background matter: clustering CDM plus baryons, massive neutrinos, minus the friction brake $\omega_{\rm fr}\ge0$, which enters as a matter-like, non-clustering term.
- $\omega_r$ is radiation; $\omega_{\rm DE}=h^2-\omega_{m,\rm bg}-\omega_r$ is felt dark energy (flatness).
- $1-F_Q-F_\eta\ge0$ is the lid floor, the persisting deposit with $w=-1$.
- $F_Q$ is the $Q$ share of today's dark energy; $s_Q(z;p)$ is the gradual release shape after $z=2$, normalised to $s_Q(0)=1$, with growth index $p$.
- $F_\eta$ is the $\eta$ spin-down share; $e_\eta(z)=(L/L_0)^2a^{-2}$ is the rotational-energy shape, with $L$ from linear tidal torque saturating at $z_{\rm coll}=0.6$.

*Plain language:* the felt dark energy is a flat floor, plus a top-up released gradually after cosmic noon, plus a spin-down share that fades. The brake behaves like a small negative amount of non-clumping matter.

### 6.2 Early tail and the observer-Prime ruling

The binding-burst tail is held at $w=-1$ and handed back to $\Phi_7$ at equality. Its plateau is $X$ times the radiation density at equality:

$$
f_{\rm EDE} \;=\; \frac{X}{4+X}
$$

*Key:*

- $f_{\rm EDE}$ is the early-dark-energy fraction at the hand-back midpoint, in the Poulin et al. definition [7].
- $X$ is the plateau's ratio to the radiation density at matter--radiation equality.

The author replaced the explored rule $X=\tfrac12$ by $X\le0.271$ on the peak measure (item 9, revised), which meets ACT DR6 $f_{\rm EDE}<0.088$ [8]; with the $d+s$ term admitted, $X$ drops to about 0.26 to stay within the same early budget [estimate]. The observer-Prime ruling (18 November) holds that every observer is Prime to itself, with its own time sign $+$ and flavour weight $\gamma=1$; $Q$ is relative only across domains and the fits are unchanged.

*Plain language:* a little extra dark energy in the very early universe is allowed, but only a small amount, and the model is now kept under the latest limit. Every observer sees its own domain as the home domain, which does not change the fits.

*Plain language:* the dark-energy ledger fits the shape of the expansion history well, but like other models of weakening dark energy it does not raise today's expansion rate enough to agree with the local measurement.

## 8. Open items

1. **$Q$ share.** DESY5 prefers $F_Q\to0$, Pantheon+ $\approx0.4$; not ruled.
2. **Brake amplitude.** $a^{-3}$ scaling ruled; amplitude unset. Its $\chi^2$ gain is not certified by the compressed prior and it raises $S_8$ by about 5--9\% [estimate].
3. **$\eta$ kinks at $z=0.6$ and $z=2$.** Artefacts of hard edges; smoother forms need new assumptions.
4. **Full CLASS/CAMB run.** Needed for the brake, the tail and the $d+s$ term, which the compressed CMB prior cannot fully test.
5. **Event-horizon cut on $M_{\rm local}$.** Only about 4\% of observable mass lies inside today's event horizon [estimate]; this cut has not been applied to $\rho_{\rm wall}$.
6. **Spin push.** Proposed, not ruled. Fits drove a push decaying on an orbital timescale to zero.
7. **Mass side of the drain.** The $\lesssim0.1\%$-of-matter estimate for the horizon-bound energy is carried from the ruling session and is not re-derived here; the bound energy is $\approx0.097$ in $\Omega$, so the bound requires that this energy not cluster as free matter on cosmological scales.
8. **Full rulings in canon.** None of the 17--18 November rulings are yet in the canon digest.

*Plain language:* the size of dark energy is now accounted for without tuning. What remains open is how the parts share out over time, the strength of the brake, a full early-universe calculation, and the Hubble constant.
