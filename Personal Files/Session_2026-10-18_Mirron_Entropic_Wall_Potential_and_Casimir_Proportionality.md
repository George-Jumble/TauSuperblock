## Investigation of the Proposed Proportionality

The suggestion is that the Casimir potential is probably proportional to the dark energy of the entropic force:
\[
V_{\rm Cas}\propto\rho_{\rm ent}\qquad\text{or}\qquad\rho_{\rm Cas}\propto\rho_{\rm ent}.
\]

### What can be proportional
1. **Laboratory Casimir energy density vs. observed \(\rho_\Lambda\)**  
   The ideal parallel-plate energy density scales as \(\hbar c/\ell^4\). The pure-Casimir matching length that reproduces the observed dark-energy density is \(\sim0.086\) mm. At any other separation the densities differ by many orders. A proportionality \(\rho_{\rm Cas}(\ell)=\kappa\rho_\Lambda\) therefore holds only for one specific \(\ell\) (or requires \(\kappa\) itself to be a free function of scale). This is the standard cosmological-constant matching problem rewritten as a proportionality. It does not derive \(\rho_\Lambda\); it chooses the scale at which the equality is imposed.

2. **Mirron/Entropic wall potential vs. laboratory Casimir**  
   One may write an effective relation at the hard wall
   \[
   V_{\rm wall}(R_{\rm wall})=\kappa\,V_{\rm Cas}^{\rm (eff)}
   \]
   where \(V_{\rm Cas}^{\rm (eff)}\) is some Casimir-like expression constructed from a wall-specific cutoff or from the Mirron density itself. Because \(V_{\rm wall}\) is already defined as the Mirron collective potential, the proportionality becomes a statement about how that collective potential scales with a Casimir-form expression. The constant \(\kappa\) (or the cutoff inside \(V_{\rm Cas}^{\rm (eff)}\)) remains free and is fixed by fitting \(V_{\rm wall}\) to the observed dark-energy density. Again, no derivation occurs.

3. **Energy-accounting direction**  
   - If the proportionality is merely kinematic (both quantities happen to be proportional after fitting), the author cut is untouched: laboratory Casimir is not the source.
   - If the proportionality is dynamical (Casimir energy is converted into Mirron wall potential or vice versa), then Casimir becomes a source or sink for the entropic term. That conflicts with the 2026-10-07 author cut when the entropic term is identified with \(\rho_\Lambda\).
