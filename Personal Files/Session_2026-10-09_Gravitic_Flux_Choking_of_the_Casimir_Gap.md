## 0. Verdict

**Yes, as a pinch of the gap. The modes see a smaller \(L\). No new force.**

Flux-choking here means residual-\(L\) compression reducing the plate separation, so fewer long-wavelength modes fit and the \(L^{-4}\) density rises. It does not assign a radius to the root wall.

---

## 1. Complete key

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| \(L\) | Unchoked plate separation. |
| \(L_{\rm ch}\) | Separation after the pinch. \(L_{\rm ch}<L\). |
| \(\rho\sim\hbar c/L^4\) | Casimir scale. |
| residual \(L\) | Compression that does the pinch. Not the plate gap. |
| choke | The fractional closure \((L-L_{\rm ch})/L\). |

---

## 2. The pinch

\[
\rho_{\rm ch}
=
\rho\left(\frac{L}{L_{\rm ch}}\right)^4.
\]

**Key.** \(\rho_{\rm ch}\): density after the pinch. Ratio to the fourth power because the scaling is \(L^{-4}\). A \(1\%\) closure is about a \(4\%\) rise in density. A closure by a factor of two is a factor of \(16\) in density.

The agent is the tide already named: residual angular momentum of stopped rebounds, laboratory \(G\) unchanged. No graviton carries the choke. A null wave does not cross the void wall to do it. The pinch is local to the gap.

---

## 3. What it does not do

It does not choose \(1.89\times 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{m}\), \(0.086\,\mathrm{mm}\), or \(7\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}\). Those are inputs or a rejected hoop. A choke factor would have to be computed from a stated mass and a stated distance. None is specified. Topological exclusion is unchanged: a bound forced overlap still pays \(\ge\Delta\).
