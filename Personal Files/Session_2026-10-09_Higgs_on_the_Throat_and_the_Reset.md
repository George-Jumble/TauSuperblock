## 2. Higgs on the disk

The Kerr vacuum is Ricci-flat outside the ring. A Higgs field is extra matter. Its absence from the textbook metric is the vacuum assumption, not a theorem that \(\phi=0\) on the disk. The disk is smooth, so a finite \(\phi\) can be specified there. A divergence would belong on the ring, where curvature already diverges, not at the center.

Repercussion. Reading \(\Phi_7\) as that scalar's holding channel at the passage is possible. It does not derive a vacuum expectation value, and it does not derive \(309.3\,\mathrm{MeV}\). The laboratory Higgs is a doublet with a measured vacuum value near \(246\,\mathrm{GeV}\). Putting that number on the disk is a coupling choice, not an output of \(r=0\).

## 3. Access to the reset

The wormhole accesses \(r<0\). The reset is a clock slot. Coupling them means: a congruence that crosses the disk is the congruence on which the reset is evaluated,

\[
\theta_{\rm exp}\;\longrightarrow\;-\theta_{\rm exp}
\quad\text{at the disk},
\]

with \(\Phi_8\) the name of that flip. **Key.** \(\theta_{\rm exp}\): expansion scalar. The arrow is the Mirron continuation. One manifold.

What this cannot do is make \(\Phi_8\) the region \(r<0\) as a second world. \(r<0\) is already the same extension. Calling it another universe breaks the core. Calling the flip \(\Phi_8\) and the scalar on the disk \(\Phi_7\) keeps the slots distinct. Merging them — Higgs field equals the reset — collapses slot 7 into slot 8.

Closed timelike curves for \(r<0\) where \(g_{\phi\phi}<0\) remain a feature of the extension. Access to the reset does not license them. The bounce is the sign flip, not a return to the same event.
