# Status of Dark Energy in Superblock Cosmology (as of 2026-10-21)

**Date**: 2026-10-21  
**Framework**: Live 4D Superblock (TSB). Six co-located 3+1 domains, one Mirron / Planck-Kerr 2-surface, residual-L gravity matching laboratory \(G\), floor-only Dynamic Refresh, mass floor \(\Delta=309.3\) MeV (nucleon-dressing protocol, insertion), Tau/Tav metric package hard-rejected.

## 1. Core locks that constrain dark energy

Residual-\(L\) gravity is spatial compression arising from the residual angular momentum of stopped (bound) rebounds of the electromagnetic sector. Bound fields tend toward the highest local concentration of bound fields. Effective \(G\) of any set belongs to the dominant glob’s center of gravity. This matches laboratory \(G\) under hierarchical screening.

Unbound completions (photons and other null quanta) leave \(L_{\rm res}=0\). Laboratory Casimir (Lifshitz pressure) is a floor object and is refused as a source of laboratory \(G\) or \(\rho_\Lambda\). The Tau metric package (\(\tau\) circle, \(n_{\rm hier}\), \(\rho_\Lambda=\hbar c/\tau^4\)) is hard-rejected. No fundamental graviton is postulated.

**Plain language.** Gravity in Superblock is the leftover spin from electromagnetic rebounds that get stopped. Free light leaves no leftover spin and does not make gravity. The ordinary Casimir force between plates is a small laboratory effect and is not allowed to be the source of cosmic acceleration or of ordinary gravity. There is no extra circular dimension whose size would set the dark-energy density.

## 2. What is allowed for dark energy

Dark energy is treated as an external phenomenological input (\(\Lambda\) or \(w_0w_a\)) or as a non-locking annotation. The leading non-locking candidate is the Mirron/Entropic wall potential: a collective potential of unbound energy (completed photonic bounces + unbound Mirrons) resident on the conformal hard-wall. Its gradient supplies an entropic force directed toward the high-Weyl / lack-of-mass side (anti-gravity attracted to voids). Strength is fitted, not derived.

Alternative non-locking readings include hierarchical residual-\(L\) domain averaging (Timescape-like apparent acceleration) and Bound Dark Energy (separate dark SU(3) sector). No candidate derives \(\rho_\Lambda\) from locked residual-\(L\) structure.

**Plain language.** Superblock does not yet calculate the amount of dark energy from its core rules. It can either take the observed acceleration as an input, or it can sketch an “entropic push” coming from free light collected on a distant wall, with the strength adjusted to match observations. Ordinary gravity stays attractive and matches laboratory measurements.

The hard wall is defined as the geometric locus where unbound bounces complete (\(L_{\rm res}=0\)). Energy of completed null quanta is therefore resident on the wall by definition. The Mirron/Entropic potential is the sum of that energy; its gradient is the entropic force.

**Variable key:**  
- Wall: locus of completed unbound bounces.  
- \(V_{\rm wall}\): Mirron/Entropic wall potential.  
- \(E_{\rm unbound}^{(i)}\): energy of the \(i\)-th completed null bounce.  
- \(\mathbf{F}_{\rm ent}\): entropic force.

**Plain language.** The “wall” is simply the place where free light finishes its journey and drops its energy without leaving any leftover spin. All that free-light energy sits on the wall; the slope of that energy pile is the push that can accelerate the universe outward toward emptier regions.

## 4. Entropic force as atemporal dispersing photonic wave

The entropic force may be read as the gradient of an atemporal (\(d\tau=0\)) dispersing photonic wave. The force is larger farther from the wall and weakens as the wave’s energy becomes more diffused relative to the wall, especially as the universe approaches Penrose CCC crossover conditions (\(\mathcal{I}^+\) identified with the next aeon’s bang across a spacelike 3-surface when \(\Lambda>0\)).

Early residual-\(L\) (ordinary \(G\) realized by compressed bound density) produces local focusing of null rays but does not supply an extra global slowing of the photonic shell capable of matching an early lambda variable. Virtual-scale residual \(L\) remains a floor fluctuation and cannot do so either.

**Plain language.** Free light spreads out while staying “timeless” on its own ray. The push it creates is stronger when the light is still far from the finishing wall and gets weaker as the light thins out near the end of an aeon (the Penrose crossover). Gravity in the early dense universe can bend light rays locally, but it is already the ordinary gravity we know; it does not create a separate early dark-energy term.

E_{\rm shell}

10^{66}\text{–}10^{67}\,\mathrm{J}.

This is a concrete order-of-magnitude reservoir for the wall potential. Inserting it into a phenomenological \(\Lambda_{\rm eff}\) still leaves free conversion constants; the observed \(\rho_\Lambda\sim5\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}\) is not derived.

**Plain language.** If one adds up roughly all the light ever emitted by stars (and a comparable amount from black-hole accretion) inside the region from which signals can still reach us, one gets a huge but finite energy. That energy can sit on the wall, but turning the exact number into the precise observed acceleration still requires adjustable factors that the core rules do not fix.

## 7. Overall status

Dark energy in live Superblock cosmology remains non-locking. The core (residual-\(L\) = laboratory \(G\), bound/unbound split, floor-only DR, Tau rejection, Casimir cut) is intact. The Mirron/Entropic wall potential sourced by unbound photonic completions on the wall is a coherent organizational statement whose gradient can point toward the lack-of-mass side and whose strength can be fitted to observations. No derivation of the numerical value of \(\rho_\Lambda\) from locked structure has been achieved. Hierarchical averaging remains an alternative non-locking reading. Phenomenological \(\Lambda\) is always available as an external input.

**Plain language.** Superblock keeps ordinary gravity exactly as measured in the laboratory and keeps free light from generating that gravity. Cosmic acceleration can be described either by inserting the observed number or by a flexible “push from free light collected on a distant wall.” The framework has not yet turned its core rules into a prediction of that number; the idea is organized and consistent but still adjustable.
