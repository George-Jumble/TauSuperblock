## 1. Isolation — yes

For an observer who stays in the void,

\[
r_{\rm eh}=\frac{c}{H_\Lambda}.
\]

Walls that remain outside that sphere never send another photon that arrives. No new EM from the rest of the universe. That is ordinary ΛCDM plus a void chart. It is the local \(\mathscr{I}^+_{\rm void}\).

Already inside the horizon: the CMB that was already there, redshifting as \(T_{\rm CMB}\propto 1/a\), plus Gibbons–Hawking radiation of the cosmological horizon,

\[
T_{\rm dS}
=
\frac{\hbar H_\Lambda}{2\pi k_{\rm B}}
\sim 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{K}.
\]

So “no more light” means **no more incoming light from the walls**. It does not mean the Maxwell field is gone.

**Key.** \(r_{\rm eh}\): event-horizon radius. \(T_{\rm dS}\): de Sitter temperature. \(T_{\rm CMB}\): leftover photon temperature, still falling.

## 2. \(P_{\rm vac}>P_{\rm rad}\) — already true, and then some

Radiation pressure of a photon gas is \(P_{\rm rad}=\rho_{\rm rad}/3\). Vacuum pressure of \(\Lambda\) is \(P_{\rm vac}=-\rho_\Lambda\).

Globally, \(\rho_\Lambda\gg\rho_{\rm CMB}\) **today** (\(\Omega_\Lambda\sim 0.7\), \(\Omega_\gamma\sim 5\times 10^{-5}\)). The inequality \(|P_{\rm vac}|>P_{\rm rad}\) for CMB-versus-Λ is not a future void event. It is an old one.

In the isolated void it becomes sharper: \(\rho_{\rm m}\) has left for the walls, \(\rho_{\rm CMB}\) keeps falling, \(\rho_\Lambda\) does not. The only leftover radiation is CMB plus \(T_{\rm dS}\) quanta. Vacuum pressure wins by a still larger factor. That is the correct local reading of your sentence. It is Friedmann, not Casimir of a circle, and it does not mint a megaparsec.

**Key.** \(P_{\rm vac}=-\rho_\Lambda\). \(P_{\rm rad}=\rho_{\rm CMB}/3\) plus a negligible GH piece. Crossing vs CMB is not the isolation moment.

## 3. “The Higgs potential expands” — two different objects

**A. Vacuum energy in GR.**  
\(\rho_\Lambda\) is constant. Physical volume of the patch grows. The *integrated* vacuum energy in that volume grows \(\propto a^{3}\). Energy bookkeeping in GR is not a closed-system first law. That is real and does not require a changing Higgs VEV.

**B. Electroweak Higgs.**  
The potential \(V(\Phi)\) and the VEV \(v\approx 246\,\mathrm{GeV}\) are particle-physics data. In de Sitter they receive corrections of order \(H_\Lambda^{2}\), which are \(10^{-84}\) of \(v^{2}\). The potential does not “expand” in any useful sense. It does not turn void growth into new electroweak-scale mass.

If “Higgs potential” is being used as a name for \(\rho_\Lambda\), say \(\rho_\Lambda\). Do not write \(v(a)\).

**Key.** \(v\): Higgs VEV. \(H_\Lambda^{2}/v^{2}\) is negligible. Growing integrated \(\rho_\Lambda\times\mathrm{Vol}\) is GR, not a running Higgs.

## 4. “Expansion creates new mass and energy in the true vacuum”

What exists:

- More integrated vacuum energy because volume grew at constant \(\rho_\Lambda\).
- Gibbons–Hawking quanta at \(T_{\rm dS}\). That is new energy in the QFT-on-dS sense, thermally negligible.
- In some CCC options, rest mass later *returns* after global \(\mathcal{X}\). That is a new aeon, not a void manufacturing baryons behind an event horizon.

What does not exist, in known 4D physics:

- A factory that turns void expansion into new rest mass at \(m_0\) or \(v\) while the observer is still in this aeon.
- “All quantum fields already exist” is true as a field content statement and does not by itself create particles. Occupation numbers stay near empty at \(T_{\rm dS}\).

**Key.** Creation rate from the horizon is set by \(T_{\rm dS}\), not by \(m_0\) and not by the Higgs VEV.
