## Geometric implications

1. **Common section, not a shared light-cone.**  
   Co-location places the six domains at the same 4D locus. The universal label selects six local slices that share one geometric stamp. It does not glue their light-cones into a single null surface; each domain retains its own null structure. Correspondence is a matching of labels, not an identification of null geodesics.

2. **Expansion toward the causal wall.**  
   Each local light-cone \(\mathcal{L}_a(p)\) grows until it meets the hard wall. The wall is the causal boundary of occupancy: signals (residual-\(L\) co-located coupling excepted) do not cross it. As the cone approaches the wall the fraction of its generators that remain interior decreases; the identifying contraction \(\mathcal{C}\) receives contributions from a progressively smaller causally connected set.

3. **Weakening of the link.**  
   Strength of the cross-domain identification falls with null volume already swept or with hierarchical separation from the wall. This is a dilution, not a disconnection: domains remain co-located and the universal label continues to exist as a kinematic contraction. No new horizon or preferred-frame signal is generated inside laboratory globs.

4. **Relation to residual-\(L\) and slices.**  
   Residual-\(L\) compression continues inside each dominant glob, independent of how far its light-cone has expanded. Local slices stay level sets of the kinematic UPS. Weakening affects only the clarity of the universal matching, not the laboratory match of \(G\).
