## Starting Expression
\[
\rho_{\rm eff}\propto\frac{\hbar c}{L_{\rm env}^4}\Bigl(\frac{N_{\rm in}}{N_{\rm out}}\Bigr)^p
\]

**Complete key**
- \(\rho_{\rm eff}\): effective energy density from differential proper-time rates among supersphere envelopes.
- \(L_{\rm env}\): characteristic envelope separation (length; input).
- \(N_{\rm in}/N_{\rm out}\): interior-to-exterior lapse ratio from the \(\eta\)-sphere(s).
- \(p\): unfixed exponent.
- \(\hbar\), \(c\): standard constants.
- Proportionality constant left free.

Observed target:
\[
\rho_\Lambda\approx 6.03\times 10^{-27}\,{\rm kg\,m^{-3}}
\]
(equivalent energy density \(\rho_\Lambda c^2\approx 5.42\times 10^{-10}\,{\rm J\,m^{-3}}\)).

**Complete key**
- \(\rho_\Lambda\): observed vacuum energy density (Prat et al. JCAP 2022 value).
- Pure Casimir matching length (contrast factor = 1): \(L_{\rm match}\approx 0.087\,{\rm mm}\).

## Ways to Adjust the Formula (all still inputs)
Any numerical match requires fixing the free quantities. None of the following is derived from the locked Mirron, residual-\(L\), six co-located domains, or floor-only Dynamic Refresh.

### 1. Set the envelope scale to the matching length
Choose
\[
L_{\rm env}=L_{\rm match}\approx 0.087\,{\rm mm}
\]
and set the contrast factor to unity (or absorb it into the proportionality constant). Then \(\rho_{\rm eff}\) matches \(\rho_\Lambda\) by construction.

**Key**: \(L_{\rm match}\) as defined above. This simply restates the observation; it does not explain the millimetre scale.

### 2. Keep a microscopic envelope and supply a large suppression
If \(L_{\rm env}\) is taken at a nuclear or Compton scale (example: old printed floor Compton length \(\lambda_{m_0}\approx 0.63\,{\rm fm}\)), the bare \(\hbar c/L^4\) is enormous. Matching requires
\[
\Bigl(\frac{N_{\rm in}}{N_{\rm out}}\Bigr)^p\sim 10^{-45}
\]
(order-of-magnitude; exact value depends on the precise \(L_{\rm env}\) and the proportionality constant).

**Key**
- \(\lambda_{m_0}\): Compton length associated with the printed mass floor (protocol value; not a derivation).
- The required contrast is an additional free parameter.

### 3. Keep a macroscopic envelope and supply an amplification
Example: \(L_{\rm env}=1\,{\rm mm}\) requires
\[
\Bigl(\frac{N_{\rm in}}{N_{\rm out}}\Bigr)^p\sim 1.7\times 10^{4}.
\]
A product of successive envelope contrasts (as in the earlier \(n_{\rm eff}\) sketch) can be arranged to yield this factor, again by choice.

### 4. Tie the exponent to the six-domain structure
Set \(p=6\) (or \(p=7\) to echo the old cycle) and solve for the per-envelope contrast:
\[
\xi=\bigl(N_{\rm in}/N_{\rm out}\bigr)=\bigl(\rho_\Lambda L_{\rm env}^4/(\hbar c)\bigr)^{1/p}.
\]
This is still a fit, not a derivation.

**Key**
- \(\xi\): per-envelope time-contrast factor.
- \(p=6\) or \(7\): optional integer suggested by domain count or 142857 cycle; not fixed by the live equations.

### 5. Hierarchical product (extends earlier \(n_{\rm eff}\))
Replace the single contrast by a product over nested envelopes:
\[
\rho_{\rm eff}=\frac{\hbar c}{L_0^4}\prod_b\xi_b^{p_b},
\]
and choose the \(\xi_b\) (or the \(p_b\)) so the product equals the required factor. The base length \(L_0\) remains an input.

**Key**
- \(L_0\): reference envelope scale (input).
- \(\xi_b\), \(p_b\): contrast and exponent on envelope \(b\).
