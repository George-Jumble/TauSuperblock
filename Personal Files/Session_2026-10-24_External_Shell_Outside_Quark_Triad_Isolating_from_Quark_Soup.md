## Laboratory picture of the external shell

Inside a baryon the three valence quarks are bound by the colour force. Outside that valence region the colour flux is confined: the system is a colour singlet, and the energy cost of separating a coloured excitation grows linearly with distance. The surface at which this confinement is realized — the bag boundary or the wall of the flux tube — is the external shell. It isolates the triad from free coloured exchange with neighbouring triads. At ordinary nuclear densities the residual interaction between these singlets is short-range and attractive (nuclear binding). Only at much higher densities or temperatures does the system deconfine into a quark-gluon plasma (“quark soup”).

The force that acts *on* the valence quarks is attractive (confining). The shell itself is the geometric consequence of that confinement: it is the surface beyond which free colour charge cannot propagate without paying the linear energy cost. It is not a force that repels the triad from itself.

**Key.** External shell = confinement surface of the colour singlet. Isolation = suppression of free coloured flux between singlets. Quark soup = deconfined phase.

**Plain language.** Each proton or neutron is wrapped in an invisible “skin” that keeps its three quarks from leaking colour to the outside world. That skin stops the quarks from melting into a single shared soup with the quarks of the neighbouring protons. The skin does not push the three quarks apart from one another; it just keeps their colour from escaping.

## Superblock reading

In the Superblock frame the colour residual \(L\) of a stopped rebound is the floor inertia of each valence line. The colour-singlet construction is the hadronic glob. The external shell is the boundary of that glob: the surface at which a forced colour overlap would have to pay the TEP cost \(E_{\rm violation}\ge\Delta\). Dynamic Refresh rewrites the floor (quarks, gluons, colour) only inside that shell; it does not refresh higher globs.

The residual colour force *between* singlets remains short-range. Hierarchical residual \(L\) at larger scales is the EM-type stopped-rebound account of laboratory-matching attractive gravity. The external shell does not itself become a long-range repulsive force.

**Plain language.** In this framework the “skin” around each three-quark packet is the edge of the lowest bound system. Inside the skin the colour bounce is stopped and leaves mass. Outside the skin the leftover colour force is weak and short-range. Ordinary gravity comes from the same stop-at-Present language applied to larger bound systems, not from the skin itself pushing outward.

## Relation to the earlier inverse-gravity / hard-wall proposal

The clarification removes the claim that the strong force is repulsive *to the triad*. The external shell is an isolating boundary, not an inverse-gravity repulsion that grows with distance.

Even with that clarification, promoting the external shell (or a hierarchical stack of such shells) to a long-range repulsive source whose collective potential at the hard wall equals \(\rho_\Lambda\) still requires:

- a free coupling or efficiency,
- screening that protects the residual-\(L\) match to laboratory \(G\),
- a sign or range change that is not present in the locked colour residual.

None of these is supplied by the existing locks. The construction remains free phenomenology and sits in tension with the 2026-10-07 author cut if the floor colour sector is promoted to a dynamical source of \(\rho_\Lambda\). The existing non-locking entropic wall potential already supplies a fitted large-scale repulsion attracted to lack of mass without reinterpreting the confinement shell.

**Key.** Hierarchical globbing of the external shell does not automatically invert its sign or extend its range. Screening and coupling remain free. Residual-\(L\) laboratory match is preserved only if any new long-range component is screened.

**Plain language.** The skin that keeps protons from melting into quark soup is real, but it is a short-range isolating boundary. Stacking those skins all the way out to the edge of the universe and turning them into the source of dark energy still needs extra free knobs (how far the skin reaches, how strongly it pushes at the edge). Those knobs can be tuned to match the observed dark energy, but they are not calculated from the locks we already have. The framework already has a separate, non-locking fitted annotation (the entropic wall potential) that does the large-scale repulsion job without changing what the skin does.

## Verdict

The external shell outside the quark triad is the colour-singlet confinement surface that isolates each baryon from free coloured exchange with its neighbours and thereby prevents ordinary matter from collapsing into a quark-gluon plasma. It is not a force repulsive to the triad itself, nor is it an inverse-gravity repulsion that grows with distance. Hierarchical globbing of this shell at the hard wall as a source of dark energy remains free phenomenology requiring screening and a free coupling; it does not follow from locked structure and stays in tension with the floor-object cut. The existing non-locking entropic wall potential continues to supply the fitted large-scale repulsion without reinterpreting the confinement shell.
