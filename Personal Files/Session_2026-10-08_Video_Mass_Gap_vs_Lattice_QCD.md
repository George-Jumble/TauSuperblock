## 0. Verdict

**The video is low by a factor of about five to six.**

Lattice Yang–Mills puts the lightest scalar glueball, which is the mass gap of the pure gauge theory, at \(1.5\)–\(1.8\,\mathrm{GeV}\). The video prints \(313\,\mathrm{MeV}\) and calls the lattice/PDG state an excitation. That identification is false. \(313\,\mathrm{MeV}\) is below the two-pion threshold only in a story where the gap has already been moved. It is not the lattice gap.

## 2. The comparison

\[
\frac{M_{0^{++}}}{313\,\mathrm{MeV}}
\approx
\frac{1.73\,\mathrm{GeV}}{0.313\,\mathrm{GeV}}
=
5.5.
\]

**Key.** Numerator: Morningstar and Peardon central value, Phys. Rev. D 60, 034509 (1999). Denominator: video print. The quenched window \(1.5\)–\(1.8\,\mathrm{GeV}\) gives a factor \(4.8\)–\(5.8\). Recent lattice work still treats a scalar glueball near \(1.7\,\mathrm{GeV}\) as the stable ground state in pure Yang–Mills, and identifies \(f_0(1710)\) as the phenomenology candidate. A 2026 review states the qualitative picture has been stable for twenty-five years: the \(0^{++}\) is the ground state and hence the Yang–Mills gap.

The ratio to the string tension is the more stable lattice output. \(1.73\,\mathrm{GeV}/440\,\mathrm{MeV}\approx 3.9\). The video’s \(313/440=0.71\) is not that ratio.

---

## 3. What the video misnames

It calls a PDG state near \(1630\,\mathrm{MeV}\) an excitation above a \(313\,\mathrm{MeV}\) ground state. Lattice spectroscopy has the ground state itself in the \(1.5\)–\(1.8\,\mathrm{GeV}\) window. There is no lattice \(0^{++}\) glueball at \(313\,\mathrm{MeV}\). The U+U excess predicted at that mass stays retired.

The Superblock floor at \(309.3\,\mathrm{MeV}\) is per-line nucleon dressing. It is numerically near the video’s print and physically a different operator. Matching the video to the floor does not match either to the lattice gap.
