## Complete key
- \(\psi_{\rm COM}(\mathbf{R},t)\): wave function of an unbound centre-of-mass coordinate.
- \(\chi\): internal state of a higher glob (electrons, phonons, metallic cohesion, etc.).
- Environment: external degrees of freedom (residual gas, thermal photons, scattering centres) that can become entangled with \(\mathbf{R}\).
- Which-path tag: a record that distinguishes one branch of a spatial superposition from another.
- Residual \(L\): angular momentum left by a rebound that is stopped at the Present (bound coordinate only).
- \(\Phi_8\): reset seam at which the harmonic stamp advances the phase of already-present floor excitations.
- Floor: colour + inverted-colour EM/electroweak modes only.
- \(\Delta\phi_{\rm tick}\): discrete one-tick phase increment.

## Primary mechanism: environmental entanglement
In the live frame a which-path tag is entanglement of the unbound packet coordinate with an external degree of freedom. From the 2026-09-30 COM notes: a gas collision, thermal photon, or leaking electron that records which transverse history \(\mathbf{R}\) took decoheres \(\psi_{\rm COM}\). That is loss of interference visibility, not a conversion of the coordinate from unbound to bound. Residual \(L\) appears only when a rebound is stopped at the Present; environmental scattering that leaves the packet free does not stop the rebound.

Visibility of COM interference (Pedalino sodium clusters) survives when \(\chi\) remains similar on both paths or when path-dependent entanglement with \(\chi\) or the environment is small. The bonds that hold the cluster together live in the internal envelopes and are not dissolved by the superposition.

## Relation to the stamp
The harmonic stamp is a universal phase advance of existing floor excitations at the shared \(\Phi_8\) seam. It is the same for every co-located copy and does not distinguish one spatial path from another. Consequently it does not itself constitute a which-path record. Relative phases between undecohere branches are preserved by the stamp. Any decoherence that occurs is produced by subsequent entanglement with the environment, not by the floor phase advance.

## Residual-\(L\) channel
Residual-\(L\) gravity is spatial compression arising from stopped rebounds of bound lines. An unbound packet coordinate carries \(L=0\) and therefore does not source residual compression from that coordinate. Environmental particles that scatter off the packet may themselves be bound or unbound; only their stopped rebounds contribute residual \(L\). The stamp modulates the proper-time sampling rate of those rebounds by one tick but adds no new residual and does not open a decoherence channel beyond ordinary environmental entanglement. Laboratory \(G\) matching is unchanged. No fundamental graviton is introduced.

## Hierarchical aspect
Children of a glob become internals of the parent \(\chi\). Decoherence of an internal coordinate (for example an electronic orbital) can entangle with the parent COM if the interaction distinguishes paths, but the stamp does not force that entanglement. Isolated parent COM can remain a wave; classical Dynamic Refresh of that COM is excluded (Pedalino constraint). Floor modes continue to be the only objects that receive the direct stamp.

## What is not a decoherence mechanism in this frame
- The stamp itself (universal, path-independent).
- Completion of an unbound bounce (no residual, no record).
- Mere presence of residual-\(L\) compression in the laboratory (it matches \(G\) but does not tag which-path information).
- Any Tau-circle or metric-fifth dynamics (hard-rejected).

## Summary
Quantum decoherence of wave packets in the Superblock frame is environmental entanglement that tags which path an unbound coordinate took. It is distinct from the bound/unbound distinction (stopped versus completed rebound). The imported stamp updates floor phases universally and does not supply a which-path record. Residual-\(L\) gravity remains the account of laboratory \(G\) and does not function as an additional decoherence channel beyond ordinary scattering. All core locks are preserved.
