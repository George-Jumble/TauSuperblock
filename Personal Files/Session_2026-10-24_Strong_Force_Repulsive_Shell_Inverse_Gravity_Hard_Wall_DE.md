## Laboratory strong force

The QCD potential between a static quark and antiquark is approximately Coulomb-like at short distance and linear at long distance:

\[
V(r)\approx -\frac{C\alpha_s}{r}+\sigma r.
\]

The linear term rises with \(r\). That rise is confining: it costs ever more energy to separate the charges, so free coloured states are absent from the spectrum. It is not a repulsive force that pushes the charges apart with increasing strength; the force is attractive in the sense that the system is bound, and the potential minimum is at finite \(r\).

At nuclear distances the residual colour force between colour-singlet nucleons is attractive (tens of MeV). At atomic and larger scales the electromagnetic force dominates the residual colour interaction; colour singlets are neutral under the long-range colour force.

**Key.** \(\sigma\): string tension \(\approx(440\,\mathrm{MeV})^2\). The linear rise is confinement, not an inverse-gravity repulsion.

**Plain language.** The strong force does not push quarks farther apart more strongly as they separate. It pulls them back harder the farther you try to pull them. Once three quarks form a colour-neutral proton, the leftover strong force between protons is weak and attractive at nuclear distances, and is completely overwhelmed by electromagnetism (and later by gravity) at atomic and larger scales.

## The proposed inverse-gravity behaviour

The query posits a strong force that becomes repulsive at the particle-shell level and grows more repulsive with increasing distance — the inverse of gravity — then is overcome by electromagnetism at atomic scales, and whose hierarchical globbing at the hard wall sources dark energy.

In laboratory QCD this sign and distance dependence are not observed. The confining potential rises, but the force does not reverse into a growing repulsion. Residual colour between singlets is attractive.

In the Superblock frame residual-\(L\) gravity is attractive compression toward the highest concentration of bound fields. A force that grows more repulsive with distance would be the opposite sign. Promoting the colour residual itself to that long-range repulsive role would invert the floor binding that supplies the mass of the valence lines.

**Plain language.** The picture of a strong force that pushes harder the farther you go is the opposite of what the strong force does in the laboratory and the opposite of the residual-\(L\) gravity already locked in the Superblock. Gravity in this framework pulls bound systems together; the proposed force would push them apart more strongly as they separate.

## Hierarchical globbing at the hard wall

Hierarchical globbing of residual-\(L\) is already present: each generation of coherent bound sets carries its own residual \(L\), and the effective \(G\) of a set belongs to the dominant glob’s center of gravity. That hierarchy produces laboratory-matching attraction, not repulsion.

Globbing a repulsive colour force out to the hard wall and identifying its collective potential with \(\rho_\Lambda\) would require:

1. A sign reversal of the colour residual at large hierarchical depth or at the wall.
2. A screening mechanism that suppresses the repulsion inside atomic, solar-system, and galactic scales so that the residual-\(L\) match to laboratory \(G\) is not spoiled.
3. A free efficiency or coupling that is fitted to the observed dark-energy density.

None of these is supplied by locked structure. The construction therefore adds free parameters and does not derive \(\rho_\Lambda\).

It also conflicts with the 2026-10-07 author cut if the colour (or Casimir/virtual) sector is promoted from a floor object to a dynamical source of \(\rho_\Lambda\). The existing non-locking candidate for large-scale repulsion is the entropic wall potential (gradient of the collective unbound-Mirron potential, attracted to lack of mass), whose strength is fitted, not derived from a repulsive strong force.

**Key.** Screening length and coupling efficiency remain free. Hard wall remains a causal edge, not a dynamical source under live locks. Residual-\(L\) laboratory match is preserved only if the new repulsion is screened or localized at the wall.

**Plain language.** Nesting bound systems already produces ordinary gravity in this framework. Turning the strong force into a long-range repulsion that gets stronger with distance and then “globbing” it at the edge of the universe to make dark energy would require new free knobs (how strongly it is screened, how efficiently it couples at the wall). Those knobs can be adjusted to match the observed dark energy, but they are not calculated from the existing locks. The framework already has a non-locking fitted annotation (the entropic wall potential) that does the same phenomenological job without inverting the strong force.

## Verdict

Laboratory QCD does not exhibit a strong force that becomes more repulsive with increasing distance; the potential rises because the force is confining. The Superblock residual-\(L\) account of gravity is attractive compression. A hierarchical repulsive colour force globbed at the hard wall can be written as a free phenomenological source for \(\rho_\Lambda\), but only after introducing screening and a free coupling, and only in tension with the floor-object cut. It does not derive the dark-energy density. The existing non-locking entropic wall potential already supplies a fitted large-scale repulsion attracted to lack of mass without inverting the strong force. The proposal remains non-locking speculation.
