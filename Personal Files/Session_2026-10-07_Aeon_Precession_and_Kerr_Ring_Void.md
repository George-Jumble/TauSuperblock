### 3.2 Center of the ring is not a disconnected void

Ring locus, curvature singularity:

\[
r=0,\qquad \theta=\frac{\pi}{2},\qquad \Sigma=0.
\]

**Key.** \(r,\theta\): Boyer–Lindquist radius and polar angle. \(\Sigma=r^2+a^2\cos^2\theta\). This set is a circle of radius \(a\). Dimension 1. Timelike in the vacuum extension.

Disk, not singular:

\[
r=0,\qquad \theta\neq\frac{\pi}{2}.
\]

**Key.** Interior of the ring in the equatorial embedding. Regular. The maximal extension sends this disk to the \(r<0\) asymptotic. Causal contact through the disk is the content of the extension. A causally disconnected void is the claim the chart contradicts.

Near the ring in \(r<0\) the vacuum solution has closed timelike curves. That is the opposite of a quiet, empty, disconnected chamber.

A cosmological void is an underdense region in the expanding 4-world, size tens of megaparsecs, future-isolated only inside its own \(r_{\rm eh}=c/H_\Lambda\). It is not the disk of a Planck–Kerr ring.

### 3.3 Vacuum-only white hole, mass drawn into the ring

Kerr with \(\Lambda=0\) is a vacuum solution: \(T_{\mu\nu}=0\) outside the ring. That does not mean a cosmological void has deleted the CMB, and it does not mean the white-hole block releases vacuum pressure. Vacuum pressure in the live frame is the chamber input \(\rho_{\rm vac}\), unnamed as a derived \(\Lambda_\Omega\) until a local \(\Omega\) is integrated.

Expansion toward infinity is \(r\to\infty\) in the exterior, or the scale factor of the ambient 4-world. Both sit at the opposite end of the diagram from the ring. The likeness in the charge swaps the throat for \(\mathscr{I}^+\).
