Yes, as an auxiliary incidence space. No, as a metric fifth dimension in which fields propagate, and no, as a source of \(\Lambda\), the mass floor, or the shadow ratio.

## 3. The construction that earns the name

### 3.1 Riemann sphere, stated once

The Riemann sphere is the one-point compactification of the complex line, with three identical models:

\[
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}\simeq\mathbb{CP}^1\simeq S^2.
\]

Stereographic projection from the north pole \(N=(0,0,1)\) sends

\[
\sigma:S^2\setminus\{N\}\to\mathbb{C},\qquad \sigma(x,y,z)=\frac{x+iy}{1-z}.
\]

Among spheres, only \(S^2\) carries an integrable complex structure compatible with this conformal picture. \(S^6\) admits an almost-complex structure from the octonions; integrability is not a lock and is not used here.

### 3.2 The five-dimensional analogue

Embed the unit sphere in \(\mathbb{C}^3\):

\[
S^5=\bigl\{(z_1,z_2,z_3)\in\mathbb{C}^3:\ |z_1|^2+|z_2|^2+|z_3|^2=1\bigr\}.
\]

The Hopf projection to the complex projective plane is

\[
\pi:S^5\to\mathbb{CP}^2,\qquad \pi(z)=\mathbb{C}\cdot z=[z_1:z_2:z_3].
\]

The fibre is a circle: if \(z\in S^5\) and \(\vartheta\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\), then \(e^{i\vartheta}z\) is the same point of \(\mathbb{CP}^2\). So

\[
S^1\longrightarrow S^5\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathbb{CP}^2.
\]

Dimension count: \(\dim_{\mathbb{R}}S^5=5\), \(\dim_{\mathbb{R}}S^1=1\), \(\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{CP}^2=4\).

This \(S^5\) is a CR manifold of hypersurface type (CR dimension 2, real codimension 1). That is the standard odd-dimensional analogue, in several complex variables, of the Riemann sphere as the boundary of the ball. The Riemann sphere itself is the CR 1-sphere \(S^3/\text{fibre}\), recovered below.

### 3.3 It contains the Riemann sphere; it does not replace it

A complex line in \(\mathbb{CP}^2\) is a copy of \(\mathbb{CP}^1\simeq S^2\). Its preimage under \(\pi\) is an \(S^3\). The ordinary Hopf fibration sits inside the five-dimensional one:

\[
S^1\longrightarrow S^3\longrightarrow S^2\simeq\mathbb{CP}^1\subset\mathbb{CP}^2.
\]

So “raised to five dimensions” is the step \(n=1\to n=2\) in the family

\[
S^1\longrightarrow S^{2n+1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^n,
\]

from total space \(S^3\) (base = Riemann sphere) to total space \(S^5\) (base = \(\mathbb{CP}^2\)).

### 3.4 What the Hopf circle is not

The fibre coordinate \(\vartheta\) is a dimensionless phase on the unit sphere in \(\mathbb{C}^3\). Set its radius to the embedding radius of that unit sphere. Do not assign it a length \(\tau\), a cosmological radius, or a Casimir energy. Identifying this fibre with the retired Tav circle is the move the query asked to disregard, and it remains retired.

## 4. The five already latent in the live frame

### 4.1 Null-ray space

An unparametrized null geodesic in Minkowski \(\mathbb{R}^{1,3}\) is fixed by a direction on the celestial sphere and a screen impact parameter, modulo sliding along the ray. The resulting space \(N(\mathbb{R}^{1,3})\) is five-real-dimensional. Equivalently, it is the projectivized cotangent bundle of the celestial Riemann sphere, with the null condition imposed.

This is the geometric content of the lock “unbound is the photon conformal sphere only.” The extra label is which ray, not a place the photon travels through. No field equation is written on \(N\) in this note.

### 4.2 Conformal group already in use

The live Mirron sits in

\[
\mathrm{Conf}(S^3)\simeq\mathrm{SO}(4,1).
\]

\(\mathrm{SO}(4,1)\) is the Lorentz group of an auxiliary five-dimensional Minkowski space \(\mathbb{R}^{1,4}\). That \(\mathbb{R}^{1,4}\) is the linear model of the group. It is not a Kaluza–Klein bulk, and particles are not given a fifth coordinate in it. The conformal compactification of ordinary Minkowski \(\mathbb{R}^{1,3}\) remains four-dimensional: \(S^1\times S^3/\mathbb{Z}_2\), with conformal group \(\mathrm{SO}(2,4)\). The \(S^1\) in that product is conformal time.

### 4.3 Why the octonionic ladder refuses a fifth slot

Projective lines over the division algebras:

\[
\mathbb{CP}^1\simeq S^2,\qquad \mathbb{HP}^1\simeq S^4,\qquad \mathbb{OP}^1\simeq S^8.

Real dimensions \(2,4,8\). Five is absent. The six domain residues and the phase-8 reset do not require a fifth metric axis to sit between \(S^4\) and \(S^8\). Forcing one would be a new postulate, not a lift of the Riemann sphere.
