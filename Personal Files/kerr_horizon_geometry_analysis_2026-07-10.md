## Standard Kerr Geometry Clarification (with Full Variable Key)

In Boyer-Lindquist coordinates (t, r, θ, φ), the Kerr metric for a rotating black hole of mass M and angular momentum parameter a = J/M (geometric units G = c = 1) is:

\[
ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{2Mr}{\Sigma}\right) dt^2 - \frac{4Mar \sin^2\theta}{\Sigma} dt d\phi + \frac{\Sigma}{\Delta} dr^2 + \Sigma d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta \left[ \frac{(r^2 + a^2)^2 - a^2 \Delta \sin^2\theta}{\Sigma} \right] d\phi^2
\]

**Complete Key to Variables and Functions**:
- \( ds^2 \): Line element (spacetime interval squared).
- \( t \): Time coordinate (asymptotic observer time).
- \( r \): Radial coordinate (Boyer-Lindquist).
- \( \theta \): Polar angle (0 at north pole, π at south pole).
- \( \phi \): Azimuthal angle.
- \( M \): Mass parameter of the black hole (geometric units).
- \( a \): Specific angular momentum (a = J/M, |a| ≤ M for black holes; a=0 recovers Schwarzschild).
- \( \Sigma(r, \theta) = r^2 + a^2 \cos^2\theta \): Auxiliary function (generalized "radius squared" including oblateness from spin).
- \( \Delta(r) = r^2 - 2Mr + a^2 \): Horizon function (vanishes at horizons).
- The cross term \( dt d\phi \) encodes frame-dragging (Lense-Thirring effect).

**Event Horizons** (where Δ = 0, null surfaces):
\[
r_\pm = M \pm \sqrt{M^2 - a^2}
\]
- \( r_+ \): Outer event horizon (relevant for "PK mirrors" in the paper; photon bounces referenced here).
- \( r_- \): Inner Cauchy horizon.
- **Shape**: Constant r = r₊ (independent of θ, φ). Topologically and geometrically a 2-sphere. The induced 2-metric on the horizon confirms spherical topology and (in standard treatments) spherical geometry for area and causal purposes. **Not elliptical**.

**Ergosphere / Static Limit** (where g_{tt} = 0, frame-dragging prevents static observers; CTCs possible inside):
\[
r_{\rm erg}(\theta) = M + \sqrt{M^2 - a^2 \cos^2\theta}
\]
- Depends on θ: smaller at poles (θ=0,π), larger at equator → **oblate spheroid** (flattened poles, more "elliptical" in cross-section through rotation axis).
- The paper states: "As it intersects the ergosphere radius of a microscale Planck-Kerr (PK) seed black hole (r₊ = M + √(M² - a²))" — note the formula given is actually r₊ (horizon), while term used is "ergosphere radius". This mixes the two surfaces slightly, common in heuristic descriptions but technically the ergosphere lies outside the horizon (r_erg ≥ r₊, equal at poles).

**Ring Singularity** ("Kerr ring"):
- Located at r = 0, θ = π/2 (equatorial plane), a ring of coordinate radius |a|.
- Curvature singularity (Kretschmann scalar diverges).
- Enclosed by both horizons; does **not** deform the outer event horizon into an ellipse. The horizon remains spherical; the ring is the "source" of rotation inside.

**Why the event horizon stays spherical**:
- The Killing vector field and stationarity/axisymmetry preserve the constant-r surface as null and spherical in the coordinate atlas.
- Rotation distorts the **ergosphere** and **ergoregion** (frame-dragging), and creates the ring singularity, but the event horizon location r₊ is θ-independent.
- For slow rotation (a << M), both surfaces nearly spherical. For near-extremal (a → M), ergosphere highly oblate, but horizon still spherical (r₊ → M).

**Embedding note**: When embedding the horizon 2-surface into 3D Euclidean space for visualization, Kerr horizons can appear slightly distorted in some embeddings due to the metric, but consensus in GR literature and textbooks (e.g., Chandrasekhar, Misner-Thorne-Wheeler) is that the event horizon is spherical while the ergosphere is oblate.
