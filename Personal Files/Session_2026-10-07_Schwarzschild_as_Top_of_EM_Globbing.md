## 4. Examination of the three clauses

### 4.1 “Top rung of EM globbing”

Accepted as a rename of \(\max\mathrm{TW}^{em}\), with the gravitic node left retired.

Refused as a generation. A glob is a coherent set with one \(\mathbf{R}^{(g)}\). \(r_s\) is a radius every glob already has, including the proton. If it were a generation, the hadronic glob would already sit on it.

The July fusion “Schwarzschild / rock-scale” is the part that does not survive the table above. A rock’s EM glob ends at its material surface. Its \(r_s\) is \(10^{-27}\,\mathrm{m}\) for a kilogram.

### 4.2 “Greatest glob of EM energy which can resist the flow of gravity”

This mixes three different saturations.

1. **Material EM vs self-gravity.** Gravity overcomes rigid-body strength at planetesimal scales (hundreds of kilometres), where \(R/r_s\) is still \(\sim 10^{8}\) or larger. That is where a body becomes round under its own weight. It is not \(r_s\).
2. **Degeneracy vs gravity.** Electron degeneracy fails near the Chandrasekhar mass \(\sim 1.4\,M_\odot\), at a white-dwarf radius still \(\sim 10^{3}\,r_s\). Neutron degeneracy plus residual strong force fails near a few solar masses, at \(R/r_s\sim\mathcal{O}(1)\), and only then does collapse drive \(R\) down to the horizon scale. The mass limit is not the radius \(r_s\).
3. **Charge vs gravity.** The actual GR bound on electromagnetic stress holding a mass up is the extremal Reissner–Nordström condition, horizon at \(r_s/2\), not at \(r_s\):

\[
r_{\pm}
=
\frac{Gm}{c^2}
\pm
\sqrt{
\left(\frac{Gm}{c^2}\right)^2
-
\frac{G Q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 c^4}
}.
\]

**Key.** \(r_{\pm}\): outer and inner horizons. First term: gravitational radius \(r_s/2\). Second term: charge contribution. Extremal when the radicand vanishes, \(r_+=r_-=Gm/c^2\). Above that charge, no horizon. This is “EM stress resisting gravity” in the classical charged solution. It is a bound on \(Q\) at fixed \(m\), not a greatest EM glob located at \(r_s\).

Unbound light is a different channel. At \(r_{\rm ph}\) a photon orbit is unstable. At \(r_s\) an outgoing null geodesic does not reach future null infinity. In live bounce language that is the failure of an unbound Past\(\leftrightarrow\)Future completion to leave the glob, not the greatest bound-EM object.

### 4.3 “Where gravity becomes the dominant force”

True only for the unbound channel of *that* glob, and only at saturation.

False as a universal onset:

- Inside a rock, EM binds the solid; gravity is the cross-cut \(\sigma^{(g)}\), and \(R\gg r_s\).
- Between planets, gravity dominates orbits while EM still binds each body.
- Dominance over light-like escape is the horizon condition, which ordinary EM globs never meet.

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
R^{(g)}\gg r_s(m^{(g)}) & \text{EM glob intact; }r_s\text{ is an interior mathematical radius.}\\
R^{(g)}\sim r_s(m^{(g)}) & \text{unbound completion cannot leave; apparent seam of this dominant glob.}
\end{array}
\]

**Key.** \(R^{(g)}\): material radius. \(r_s(m^{(g)})\): Schwarzschild radius of the same glob. First row: rock, planet, ordinary star. Second row: collapsed dominant glob. Gravity was already present on the first row as residual \(L\). The second row is not a new force.
