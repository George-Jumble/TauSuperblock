# Session 2026-10-18: Electromagnetic Manipulation of the Casimir Effect – Plain-Language Summary

**Date**: 2026-10-18  
**Query**: Recall the paper on electromagnetic manipulation of the Casimir effect and give a plain-language summary.

## Primary Paper: Magnetic-field tuning of the Casimir force

**Citation**: Yichi Zhang et al., *Nature Physics* 20, 1282–1287 (2024). DOI: 10.1038/s41567-024-02521-0. Published online 24 May 2024.  
**Lead groups**: University of Science and Technology of China (Zeng Changgan et al.).

### What the Casimir effect is (plain language)
Empty space is not truly empty. According to quantum mechanics, the electromagnetic field is constantly fluctuating even when no light is present. When two objects sit close together, they restrict which of those vacuum fluctuations can exist between them compared with the free space outside. The imbalance produces a tiny force—the Casimir force—that usually pulls the objects together.

The strength and even the direction (pull or push) of this force depend on how the materials respond to electric and magnetic fields, i.e., their dielectric permittivity and magnetic permeability.

### What the paper did
Most materials’ electric properties are hard to change with ordinary laboratory fields. Magnetic properties of ferrofluids (liquids containing tiny magnetic nanoparticles) can be changed easily with a modest external magnetic field. The researchers used this fact to control the Casimir force.

**Setup**:
- A gold-coated sphere and a silica (SiO₂) plate (or gold-coated plate) immersed in a water-based ferrofluid.
- Magnetic field applied to the ferrofluid, which changes its magnetic permeability.
- Special cantilever sensor designed to measure the force while everything is submerged in the liquid.

**Key result**:
By changing the magnetic-field strength, the distance between the sphere and plate, or the concentration of magnetic particles in the fluid, the force could be switched from attractive to repulsive (and back). Theoretical calculations using Lifshitz theory predicted the transitions; the cantilever measurements confirmed them. No significant hysteresis was observed, so the switching is reversible.

### Why it matters
This is one of the first clear experimental demonstrations that the Casimir force can be actively and reversibly controlled by an external magnetic field in a liquid environment. It opens a route toward switchable micro-electromechanical systems (MEMS) that use the quantum vacuum force itself as a controllable actuator or switch.

## Related theoretical work (Ma et al. 2026)

**Citation**: Long Ma et al., *Physical Review Materials* 10, 025002 (2026). arXiv:2601.00483.  
**Title**: Prediction of a measurable sign change in the Casimir force using a magnetic fluid.

This is a theoretical (Lifshitz-formalism) study predicting stable Casimir trapping / quantum levitation in a system of polystyrene facing a Teflon-coated metal, with the gap filled by a toluene–magnetite ferrofluid. Attraction–repulsion transitions and a stable equilibrium are predicted in the 10–200 nm range, tunable by metal type, coating thickness, nanoparticle concentration, and solvent. It is a calculation, not an experiment.

## Key variables appearing in the literature (for completeness)
- \(\varepsilon(i\xi)\): imaginary-frequency dielectric permittivity  
- \(\mu(H)\): magnetic permeability (field-dependent in ferrofluids)  
- Lifshitz pressure \(P\): force per unit area from vacuum fluctuations, obtained by summing reflection coefficients of TE/TM modes over Matsubara frequencies  
- \(\Phi\): volume fraction of magnetic nanoparticles  
- \(H\): applied magnetic field  
- \(\ell\): plate or sphere–plate separation
