## 0. Verdict

**The singularity is a circle. A circle is one-dimensional.**

Circumference and radius are properties of that circle in the equatorial plane. They do not make the locus a surface. The filled disk is two-dimensional. Its boundary is the ring. Its center is not the singularity.

---

## 1. Complete key

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| \(r,\theta,\phi\) | Boyer–Lindquist coordinates. |
| ring | Locus \(r=0\), \(\theta=\pi/2\), \(\phi\in[0,2\pi)\). Topology \(S^1\). |
| \(a\) | Kerr spin parameter. Embedding radius of the ring. Circumference \(2\pi a\). |
| disk | Equatorial set \(r=0\), \(\theta\) from \(0\) to \(\pi/2\). Two-dimensional. Bounded by the ring. |
| throat | Disk center \(r=0\), \(\theta=0\). Regular in the maximal extension. Not the singularity. |
| dimension | Manifold dimension: number of free coordinates on the set. |

---

## 2. The locus

\[
\text{ring}
=
\{\,r=0,\;\theta=\pi/2,\;\phi\in[0,2\pi)\,\}.
\]

**Key.** \(r\) and \(\theta\) are fixed. Only \(\phi\) runs. One free coordinate, so dimension \(1\). Embedding radius \(a\), circumference \(2\pi a\). A circle drawn in a plane still has dimension \(1\). The plane is \(2\). The circle is the boundary.

The disk the ring bounds is the set of points with \(r=0\) and \(\theta\) not fixed at \(\pi/2\). That set is two-dimensional. The singularity is not that disk. The center of the disk is the throat, which is regular. Calling the ring two-dimensional identifies the boundary with the surface it bounds.
