## 2. The simple split

We already factored

\[
T_{00}
=
U^4\,t_{00}.
\]

**Key:** \(U^4\) is “Planck stuff per cubic metre.” \(t_{00}\) is “what fraction of that stuff is actually the vacuum we live in.”

\(U\) is huge because it is built from \(\ell_{\mathrm{Pl}}\). So \(U^4\) is about \(10^{113}\,\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}\).

What we measure as dark energy is about \(10^{-10}\,\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}\).

The ratio is about \(10^{-123}\). That single tiny number *is* the cosmological-constant problem. In these variables it has a name: \(t_{00}\).

## 3. Why “regularized norm at \(\mathscr{I}\)” is the same problem

\(f_+\) is a formula on the celestial sphere. Taken literally it has a **pole**: it blows up in one sky direction. An infinite spike is not a density you can put in Einstein’s equation.

To get a finite number you must decide:

- which part of that spike counts as **completed light** (reached \(\mathscr{I}\), unbound),  
- which part is thrown away as **stopped / bound**,  
- how you average the rest over the sky and over the six domains.

That decision is a **regulator**. Different regulators give different finite \(t_{00}\). The observed \(\rho_\Lambda\) corresponds to one very small value, \(t_{00}\sim 10^{-123}\).

We do not yet have a rule, forced by \(W\) or by \(\Phi_7,\Phi_8\), that picks out that value and no other. Until we do, we have renamed the problem, not solved it.

In ordinary QFT the same gap is: zero-point energy of every mode \(\sim \hbar\omega/2\), cut off at the Planck scale, overshoots \(\rho_\Lambda\) by \(\sim 10^{120}\). Here the cutoff is \(U\), the modes are \(f_+\) plus the six domain copies, and the unknown is the measure on completed generators at \(\mathscr{I}\). Same mismatch, different nouns.

## 5. One-sentence version

We know the Planck-sized brick \(U^4\). We do not know how much of the photon’s conformal mode, after you throw away the bound part and tame the pole on the sky, is left as a uniform vacuum. That leftover fraction is \(t_{00}\). Getting it small and unique without putting \(\rho_\Lambda\) into the rule *is* the cosmological-constant problem.
