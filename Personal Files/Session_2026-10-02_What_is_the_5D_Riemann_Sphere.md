## 1. Short answer

The 5D Riemann sphere is the unit 5-sphere in complex 3-space,

\[
S^5=\bigl\{(z_1,z_2,z_3)\in\mathbb{C}^3:\ |z_1|^2+|z_2|^2+|z_3|^2=1\bigr\},
\]

read as the total space of the Hopf map

\[
S^1\longrightarrow S^5\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^2.
\]

It is the odd-dimensional, Cauchy–Riemann analogue of the ordinary Riemann sphere. It is not a complex manifold of dimension 5, and it is not a Kaluza–Klein circle.

In this project it is a thought experiment only. The live arena remains 4-dimensional.

## 2. Why it is called a Riemann sphere at all

The ordinary Riemann sphere is three names for one object:

\[
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}\simeq\mathbb{CP}^1\simeq S^2.
\]

Stereographic projection from the north pole \(N=(0,0,1)\) is

\[
\sigma:S^2\setminus\{N\}\to\mathbb{C},\qquad\sigma(x,y,z)=\frac{x+iy}{1-z}.
\]

It is a real 2-sphere with a complex structure. Among spheres, only \(S^2\) carries an integrable complex structure of that kind. So “Riemann sphere raised to five dimensions” cannot mean a complex projective space of real dimension 5: \(\mathbb{CP}^n\) has real dimension \(2n\), always even. \(\mathbb{CP}^2\) is four-real-dimensional.

The step that actually lands on five real dimensions is the Hopf lift, not the projective base.

Ordinary Hopf map, base = Riemann sphere:

\[
S^1\longrightarrow S^3\longrightarrow S^2\simeq\mathbb{CP}^1.
\]

Next Hopf map, base = complex projective plane:

\[
S^1\longrightarrow S^5\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^2,\qquad\pi(z)=\mathbb{C}\cdot z=[z_1:z_2:z_3].
\]

Real dimensions: \(5=1+4\). A complex line \(\mathbb{CP}^1\subset\mathbb{CP}^2\) pulls back to an \(S^3\), and the ordinary Riemann sphere sits inside as that coded sub-fibration. The 5-sphere contains the Riemann sphere. It does not replace it.

As a real hypersurface in \(\mathbb{C}^3\), \(S^5\) carries a CR structure of CR dimension 2 (a complex 2-plane in the tangent space, plus one real transverse direction). That is the standard odd-dimensional analogue, in several complex variables, of the Riemann sphere as the boundary of a ball.

## 3. What the fifth direction is

On the fibre, \(z\) and \(e^{i\vartheta}z\) are the same point of \(\mathbb{CP}^2\). The coordinate \(\vartheta\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\) is a phase redundancy. It is dimensionless on the unit sphere in \(\mathbb{C}^3\). It is not a length, not \(\tau\), and not \(7\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}\).

Quotienting by that phase leaves \(\mathbb{CP}^2\), real dimension 4. Keeping the phase leaves \(S^5\), real dimension 5. There is no fifth place for a particle to travel.

## 4. Cousin already in 4D physics, not an identification

The space \(N(\mathbb{R}^{1,3})\) of unparametrized null rays in Minkowski 4-space is five-real-dimensional: a direction on the celestial sphere, a screen impact parameter, modulo sliding along the ray. In twistor language the projective null locus

\[
\mathrm{PN}=\bigl\{Z\in\mathbb{CP}^3:\ Z\cdot\bar Z=0\bigr\}
\]

is a five-real-dimensional CR manifold. The CR 5-sphere is the flat model of that structure. This note does not set \(S^5=\mathrm{PN}\). \(\mathbb{CP}^3\) itself is real dimension 6. An event is still a \(\mathbb{CP}^1\) of twistors, an ordinary Riemann sphere.

The live Mirron group \(\mathrm{Conf}(S^3)\simeq\mathrm{SO}(4,1)\) is a different five: the linear model of the conformal group of the phase-8 three-sphere. That \(\mathbb{R}^{1,4}\) is not \(S^5\), and it is not a propagation space.

## 5. What it is not

- Not \(\mathbb{HP}^1\simeq S^4\) or \(\mathbb{OP}^1\simeq S^8\). The division-algebra projective ladder is \(2,4,8\) and skips 5.
- Not the retired Tav circle, and not an open fifth axis with \(\rho_\Lambda=\hbar c/\tau^4\).
- Not colour \(\mathrm{SU}(3)\), even though \(\mathrm{SU}(3)\) rotates \((z_1,z_2,z_3)\) and preserves \(S^5\).
