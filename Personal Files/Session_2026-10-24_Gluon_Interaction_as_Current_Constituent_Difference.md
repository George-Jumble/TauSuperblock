## Numerical size of the difference

For the up quark:

\[
m_u^{\rm const}-m_u^{\rm cur}
\approx
309.3\,\mathrm{MeV}-2.16\,\mathrm{MeV}
=
307.1\,\mathrm{MeV}
\]

(using the Superblock printed floor) or \(\approx 334\,\mathrm{MeV}\) (using a typical NRQM constituent value).

For a nucleon the total current-mass contribution of the three valence quarks is only a few MeV. Subtracting that from \(M_N\) and dividing by three recovers the dressing share. The difference is therefore \(\approx 99\%\) of the constituent mass.

**Key.** The arithmetic is bookkeeping. \(M_N\) and the current masses are experimental inputs. The division by three is the equal-share protocol, not a dynamical derivation.

**Plain language.** Almost the entire “constituent mass” of a light quark is the extra energy that appears once the strong force is turned on. The bare (current) mass is only a couple of MeV; everything else is the interaction.

## Is that difference the gluon interaction?

**Yes, phenomenologically.**

In QCD the nucleon mass is dominated by:

- the kinetic energy of the valence and sea quarks,
- the energy stored in the gluon field,
- the chiral condensate,
- the trace anomaly.

Current quark masses contribute only a few MeV. Lattice decompositions confirm that the gluon field and the quark kinetic energy account for the bulk of \(M_N\).

In the Superblock reading the same difference is identified with the residual \(L\) of the stopped colour rebound: the inertial energy \(E_g\) transmitted from the bound gluon field to the valence line. The Present at which the rebound stops is the local \(T_{\rm priv}\) of the containing glob (kinematic).

\[
E_g
\approx
m_q^{\rm const}-m_q^{\rm cur}
\approx
\text{residual }L\text{ of the colour line}.
\]

**Key.** This is an identification of scales, not a derivation. The digits remain the nucleon protocol insertion. TEP continues to supply only the inequality.

**Plain language.** Yes. The gap between the tiny current mass and the much larger constituent mass is precisely the energy of the gluon interactions (and the associated quark motion) that bind the nucleon. In the Superblock language that energy is the leftover angular momentum of the colour bounce that gets stopped at the local “now.”

---

## What it does not do

- It does not derive the Yang–Mills mass gap. The Clay problem is a pure-glue existence theorem; the valence dressing share is a different operator (lattice glueball masses sit near \(1.5\)–\(1.8\,\mathrm{GeV}\)).
- It does not turn the floor into a clock output. The digits are still inserted by the nucleon protocol.
- It does not restore the Tau package or convert the kinematic UPS into a dynamical source of residual \(L\).

Residual-\(L\) laboratory match to \(G\), floor-only DR, and Tau rejection remain intact.

---

## Verdict

Yes. The difference between the constituent mass and the current mass is, to an excellent approximation, the contribution of the gluon (and sea-quark) interactions. In the Superblock frame that difference is the residual \(L\) / \(E_g\) of the stopped colour rebound and is numerically identical to the printed floor insertion \(\approx 309.3\,\mathrm{MeV}\). The identification is phenomenological and consistent with live locks; it is not a derivation of the Yang–Mills mass gap.
