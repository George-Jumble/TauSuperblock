## 0. Verdict

**No, it need not be printed in MeV. No, pascals do not make the derivation easier.**

A pascal is a pressure. The floor has been an energy. They match only after a volume is named. The missing object is still an anchor that is not the floor. Changing the unit moves that gap from a mass to a pressure times a volume.

| Symbol | Meaning |

## 2. The conversion

\[
E=PV,
\qquad
[P]=\mathrm{Pa}=\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}},
\qquad
[\Delta]=\mathrm{MeV}.
\]

**Key.** \(E\): energy. \(P\): pressure. \(V\): volume. A pascal is already an energy per volume. Printing the floor in pascals requires a \(V\) that was not required in MeV. One independent quantity becomes two, unless the floor is redefined as a pressure and the energy claim is dropped.

The 26 July ratio \(P_{\rm EM}/|P_{\rm vac}|\approx 10^{44}\) was not a measured pressure. It set \(\rho_{\rm EM}\) equal to \((0.313\,\mathrm{GeV})^4\) and divided by a vacuum density. The numerator was the floor written as a density. A density built from \(\Delta\) cannot anchor \(\Delta\).

Laboratory Casimir pressure is in pascals. It is a plate effect at separation \(a\). It was refused as a source of the floor and of gravity. A measured scrape pressure that was not computed from \(\Delta\), together with a volume for one line, would be a new anchor. None is in the notes.
