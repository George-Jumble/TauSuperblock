## Analysis of the Proposal

The second law of thermodynamics (entropy of an isolated system does not decrease; free energy tends to minimize under constraints) is compatible with the overall Superblock dynamics, but the specific claim that the void wall acts as an energy sink drawing energy toward a lower state (analogous to the start of an aeon) conflicts with the residual-\(L\) arrow.

### Residual-\(L\) direction
The locked thesis is that bound universal fields tend toward the highest concentration of the bound fields. Residual angular momentum of stopped rebounds produces spatial compression that is stronger where the bound-field density is already higher. This is positive feedback toward clumping (overdense regions become more compressed; underdense regions experience relative opening). Voids are therefore not sinks that attract energy; residual-\(L\) acts to empty them further relative to the dense globs.

A void wall as a continuous energy sink that draws bound energy toward a lower state would reverse this arrow. No such reversal is supplied by the residual-\(L\) mechanism. The wall remains a causal edge of occupancy; residual-\(L\) coupling across it is co-located and does not constitute a crossing ray that transports energy into the wall as a sink.

### Relation to the second law
In self-gravitating systems the second law is subtle: gravitational clumping can increase total entropy (negative specific heat, virialization, radiation of binding energy). The residual-\(L\) tendency toward high concentration is consistent with this: bound systems evolve toward higher-entropy configurations via hierarchical globbing and radiation, while the local free energy associated with unbound modes is minimized by completed bounces. The void wall does not function as an external heat bath or energy sink that enforces a global draw to a lower state; the entropy increase is internal to the hierarchical residual-\(L\) dynamics.

### Aeon start
The original start of an aeon is the occupancy of a root womb (first six-phase seed) and the one-engine sign flip (white stroke) that converts residual-\(L\) compression into temporary repulsion. It is not a continuous energy sink located at a void wall. The white stroke is local and finite; it does not establish a persistent gradient that draws energy across the chamber edge for the duration of the aeon. Infinite Kerr ladders, CTC batteries, and universe-sized black-hole containers remain refused.

### Open reading (non-lock)
One may annotate the high-Weyl complement as a region of lower bound-field density in which residual-\(L\) compression is weaker, so that the chamber interior (low-Weyl, clocked side) appears, under coarse-graining, to lose energy relative to the wall. This is the flux-choking / Timescape-style contrast already admitted. It does not make the wall itself an energy sink, does not reverse residual-\(L\), and does not source \(\rho_\Lambda\) from a wall Casimir. The second law continues to be satisfied by the entropy production internal to residual-\(L\) clumping and radiation.
