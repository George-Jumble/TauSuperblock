## Part I. Verbose summary — what the construction actually says

### I.1 The problem in one sentence, then in ordinary language

**Technical sentence.** If a Mirron has a *fixed* proper radius \(r_{\mathrm{M}}\), Hubble expansion cannot stretch existing mirrors without drifting \(c\), \(Z_0\), and the Tav tick. The only TSB-consistent way to fill the new proper 3-volume is to *nucleate additional* \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{M}}\) 2-surfaces. The order parameter for that nucleation is a scalar already present in the corpus: the KK stiffness \(\varphi\), the conformal factor \(\Omega\), or their composite packing field \(\chi_{\mathrm{M}}\).

**Plain language.** Imagine the vacuum is not empty. It is tiled, at a sub-nuclear scale, with tiny rigid mirrors. Each mirror is a Planck–Kerr engine that bounces the Past node of the 8-phase clock into the Future node and back. Those bounces are what we measure as a finite speed of light and a finite vacuum impedance.

Now let space expand, the way raisins separate in rising dough. Two naïve pictures both break the theory:

1. *The mirrors stretch with the dough.* Then each clock gets larger. The tick lengthens, or \(c\) changes. Laboratory physics does not allow that at Hubble order.
2. *The mirrors stay the same size but the same number is spread over more volume.* Then the tiling thins out. The vacuum’s electrical “stiffness” (impedance \(Z_0\)) should drift as the density of mirrors falls. That drift is also not seen at Hubble order.

So the dough must grow *and* new rigid mirrors must appear in the new volume, at a rate that keeps the proper density roughly constant. That appearance is not “a particle popping out of the vacuum.” It is a geometric fold: the compact fifth circle of the Tav cylinder pinches, and a new reflecting 2-surface is written. A scalar field is the switch that says *when and where* that fold is allowed.
