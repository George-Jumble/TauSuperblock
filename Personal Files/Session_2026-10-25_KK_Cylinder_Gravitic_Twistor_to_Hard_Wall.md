## Analysis

### The Statement
Each local KK cylinder (microscopic, associated with one Mirron) carries its own gravitic twistor that encodes the residual-\(L\) gravitational field of that cylinder. This twistor extends outward through the interior of the local Tau cell and reaches the hard-wall, which is the ultimate glob of the \(T_{\rm priv}\) hierarchy.

### Compatibility and Advantages

1. **Twistor description of residual-\(L\)**  
   The Superblock framework already employs twistor-space conformal gravity. Assigning a gravitic twistor to each KK cylinder gives a natural spinor/twistor encoding of the residual angular momentum generated by bound processes at that Mirron. The twistor is local to the cylinder yet can be propagated (or defined as a congruence) out to larger scales.

2. **Extension to the hard-wall**  
   Because the hard-wall is the ultimate glob and the locus of completed unbound bounces, the extension of the gravitic twistor to the wall supplies a continuous geometric link between the microscopic residual-\(L\) source and the outermost boundary condition. At the wall the unbound sector has \(L_{\rm res}=0\); the arriving twistors from interior cylinders are consistent with that condition for the unbound components while the bound residual-\(L\) data remain available for the hierarchical \(T_{\rm priv}\) assignment.

3. **Hierarchical collection**  
   Intermediate globs collect and dominate the residual-\(L\) contributions (via their own \(T_{\rm priv}\)). The twistors extending from the KK cylinders provide the underlying null or conformal data that the dominant centers of gravity integrate. The hard-wall remains the outermost collector, setting the ultimate \(T_{\rm priv}\) for the entire local Tau cell. No conflict with the hierarchical rule arises.

4. **Scale separation preserved**  
   The KK cylinders themselves remain microscopic (\(\sim 0.63\,\mathrm{fm}\)). Their gravitic twistors extend through ordinary 4D space (or the conformal structure) without requiring the extra dimension itself to become large. Heavy KK modes stay decoupled; only the residual-\(L\) gravitational information reaches macroscopic distances. This matches the laboratory requirement that ordinary gravity look Newtonian.

5. **Consistency with monodromy and topology**  
   The compact circle of the KK cylinder supplies the monodromy that locks residual \(L\) for bound processes. The gravitic twistor carries that monodromy information outward. Upon reaching the hard-wall the unbound components unwind (\(L_{\rm res}=0\)), while the bound residual remains available for gravitational sourcing. The topology and the twistor description reinforce each other.

### What Works Cleanly
- Microscopic source (KK cylinder + Mirron) \(\to\) gravitic twistor \(\to\) extension across the cell \(\to\) hard-wall (ultimate glob).  
- Residual-\(L\) gravity continues to match laboratory \(G\).  
- Bound/unbound split is preserved.  
- Local Tau cells and absence of a global repeating circle remain intact.  
- Fits the existing twistor/conformal-gravity language of the Superblock framework.

### Points of Care
- An explicit twistor incidence relation or Penrose transform linking the residual-\(L\) data on the cylinder to the hard-wall congruence still needs to be written if quantitative mode or shear calculations are required.  
- Overlapping twistors from many cylinders inside one cell must be shown to combine consistently under the dominant-glob rule.  
- The independent floor-number discrepancy (309.3 vs 313.1 MeV) is unaffected.

### Verdict
**Yes, the statement is consistent and useful.**  

Every KK cylinder can carry a gravitic twistor that extends to the hard-wall. This supplies a twistor-space bridge between the microscopic residual-\(L\) source and the ultimate glob of the local Tau cell, preserving hierarchical dominance, laboratory gravity, and the bound/unbound split without reintroducing a global extra dimension.
