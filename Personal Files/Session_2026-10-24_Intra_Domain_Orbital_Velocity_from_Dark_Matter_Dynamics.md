## Plain-language summary

Yes. The orbital dynamics of dark matter (and the baryonic matter that traces it) inside galaxies and clusters are exactly the observed motions inside one domain’s local light-cone. Galaxy rotation curves (SPARC sample) and cluster velocity dispersions give characteristic speeds of order 200–1000 km/s. In the Superblock reading these are the residual-L compressions of hierarchical globs—the same mechanism that matches laboratory \(G\) on smaller scales.  

These numbers assess **intra-domain** orbital velocities (motions of substructure relative to the center of a galaxy or cluster). They do **not** measure a bulk orbital velocity of the entire domain light-cone relative to the other five co-located copies. That inter-domain term remains a free phenomenological parameter, as previously noted.  

Using the observed speeds supplies a concrete observational anchor for the characteristic \(\beta\) inside a domain, but it does not fix the larger-scale domain orbital or derive dark energy.

## Analytical discussion

**Key to symbols**  
- Intra-domain: motions of hierarchical globs (galaxies, clusters, subhalos) inside the local light-cone of one \(T_{\rm priv}\).  
- Inter-domain: relative orbital of the six co-located copies (the earlier \(E_{\rm orb}\) term).  
- \(V_{\rm obs}\): observed circular or dispersion velocity.  
- \(\beta = V_{\rm obs}/c\).  
- Residual-L: residual angular momentum of stopped rebounds; sources attractive spatial compression (gravity).  
- Dominant glob: the largest coherent bound set whose center of gravity sets the effective \(G\) of a region.  
- SPARC: Spitzer Photometry and Accurate Rotation Curves sample (175 disk galaxies).

### What the observations supply

- **Galaxy scales (SPARC)**: Extended H I / H\(\alpha\) rotation curves. Outer flat segments typically \(V_{\rm obs}\sim 100\)–\(250\) km s\(^{-1}\) for spiral galaxies; dwarfs lower, massive systems higher. Baryonic contribution is subdominant at large radii for most systems, so the residual velocity is attributed to the dark halo. Characteristic \(\beta\sim 3\)–\(8\times 10^{-4}\).  
- **Cluster scales**: Line-of-sight velocity dispersions \(\sigma_v\sim 800\)–\(1200\) km s\(^{-1}\) for rich clusters (\(M\sim 10^{14}\)–\(10^{15}\,M_\odot\)). Virial theorem converts these into total mass, of which \(\sim 85\) % is dark. Characteristic \(\beta\sim 3\times 10^{-3}\).  
- Halo circular-speed peaks \(V_{\rm max}\) and anisotropy profiles further quantify the orbital structure of the dark component.

These are direct measurements of orbital (or dispersion-supported) dynamics inside the domain.

### Relation to the domain orbital

The earlier phenomenological term  
\[
E_{\rm orb}=3M\langle\beta^2\rangle c^2
\]  
was written for the equal-mass CPT domain orbital (inter-domain). Intra-domain velocities measured by SPARC and cluster kinematics are a different operator: they are relative to the center of a sub-glob, not a bulk motion of the entire light-cone.  

They can still be used as an observational data point to illustrate a characteristic intra-domain \(\langle\beta^2\rangle_{\rm intra}\sim 10^{-6}\)–\(10^{-5}\). They do not determine the free inter-domain speed, the volume factor, or \(\rho_\Lambda\).
