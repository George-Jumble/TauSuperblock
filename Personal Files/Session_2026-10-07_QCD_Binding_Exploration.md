## 2. Laboratory binding

Colour is exactly conserved. Asymptotic freedom makes the coupling small at short distance and large at long distance. Isolated coloured states are not in the spectrum. What propagates to the detector is a singlet.

Two singlet constructions:

- Meson: quark and antiquark, \(\mathbf{3}\otimes\bar{\mathbf{3}}\) contains \(\mathbf{1}\).
- Baryon: three quarks, \(\mathbf{3}\otimes\mathbf{3}\otimes\mathbf{3}\) contains \(\mathbf{1}\). The nucleon is this singlet.

The mass of the nucleon is not the current masses. \(m_u^{\rm cur}+m_d^{\rm cur}\) is a few MeV. The rest is kinetic energy of quarks and gluons, the chiral condensate, and the trace anomaly. Lattice glueball masses sit near \(1.5\)–\(1.8\,\mathrm{GeV}\) for the lightest scalar, not near \(300\,\mathrm{MeV}\). Nuclear binding is residual colour between singlets, tens of MeV per nucleon, not the confinement scale.

The Clay problem asks for a quantum Yang–Mills theory on \(\mathbb{R}^4\) with a mass gap \(\Delta>0\). Existence and the gap are unproved. A Superblock inequality \(E_{\rm violation}\ge\Delta\) is not that theorem.
