# Session 2026-10-24: Is the 16-Phase a Math Trick to Keep Measurements Easy?

**Date**: 2026-10-24  
**Framework**: Tau-Superblock (TSB / Superblock) — live 4D frame. Locked clock is the 8-phase G2 clockwork. The 16-phase cover is a non-locking product of the eight slots with a \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) index (domain/antipodal partner or bound/unbound). The index labels existing distinctions and does not generate new mass or energy.  
**Answer**: Yes, in the limited sense of optional bookkeeping.  
**Status**: Clarification. Non-locking.

## Plain-language summary

Yes. The 16-phase construction is a mathematical convenience for keeping track of binary distinctions that are already present. By attaching a simple +1/−1 tag to each of the eight locked G2 slots, one can label domain versus antipodal partner, or bound versus unbound, without having to invent new dynamical content. The underlying measurements—mass floor, residual-L matching to laboratory \(G\), domain-cross energies—remain exactly the same. The extra index simply makes certain phase counts or sector bookkeeping more uniform and easier to handle in calculations. It is not required; the locked clock stays 8-phase.

## Analytical note

**Key to symbols**  
- 8-phase G2 slots: locked discrete phases.  
- \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) index \(\varepsilon=\pm 1\): binary label.  
- 16-phase cover: product \(\{\text{G2 slots}\}\times\mathbb{Z}_2\).  

The cover is obtained by the trivial product above. All physical quantities (printed mass floor, residual-L compression, antipodal \(E_\times=\pi\Delta_{\rm Tav}\)) are independent of the extra index. The index only refines the labeling so that phase advances or sector distinctions can be written with a single uniform step \(\pi/8\) if desired. No new source term appears.
